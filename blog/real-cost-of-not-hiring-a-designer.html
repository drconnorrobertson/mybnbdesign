The Real Cost of Not Hiring a Designer

By MyBnBDesign Team • October 02, 2026

The Hidden Price Tag of DIY Design

Most short-term rental owners skip professional design help because they see it as an unnecessary expense. The irony is that not hiring a designer is often the most expensive decision they make. The cost is not visible on any invoice. It shows up as lower booking rates, reduced nightly prices, mediocre reviews, and the slow bleed of revenue that happens when your property underperforms its potential.

A professional designer does not just make a space look pretty. They optimize every square foot for guest experience, photographic impact, operational efficiency, and revenue generation. The absence of that optimization has a quantifiable cost that most owners never calculate.

Calculating Your Lost Revenue

The average professionally designed short-term rental earns 20 to 40 percent more per year than a comparable self-designed property in the same market. On a property generating $40,000 annually, that gap represents $8,000 to $16,000 in lost revenue every year. A professional design package typically costs $5,000 to $15,000 as a one-time investment.

The payback period on professional design is typically three to nine months. After that, the revenue premium is pure profit for years. Compare that to most other property improvements where the payback timeline stretches across years or decades.

Mistakes That Cost More Than a Designer

DIY designers make predictable expensive mistakes. Buying furniture that does not fit the space and cannot be returned. Choosing paint colors that photograph poorly and require repainting. Selecting materials that do not withstand rental use and need replacement within a year. Overlooking lighting design that cripples listing photo quality.

Each of these mistakes costs money to correct. Added together, the cost of fixing DIY design errors often exceeds what professional design would have cost in the first place. A designer prevents these mistakes entirely while also optimizing elements that most owners do not even consider.

What Designers See That You Miss

Professional vacation rental designers understand guest psychology, photography optimization, durability requirements, cleaning efficiency, and market positioning in ways that even experienced hosts do not. They know which furniture survives heavy use, which colors photograph consistently across devices, how to create the visual focal points that drive listing clicks, and how to maximize perceived space in any room.

They also bring vendor relationships that save money on furniture, accessories, and materials. Trade discounts of 20 to 40 percent on furnishings can significantly offset the design fee while providing access to commercial-grade products not available to retail consumers.

Making the Decision

The question is not whether you can afford a designer. The question is how much revenue you are leaving on the table without one. Calculate your current annual revenue, estimate the 20 to 40 percent premium that professional design delivers, and compare that gap against the cost of a design consultation.

For most property owners, the math is overwhelmingly clear. Professional design is not an expense. It is the highest-return investment available for your rental property.

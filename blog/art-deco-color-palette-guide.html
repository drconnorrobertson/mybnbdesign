Art Deco Color Palette Guide

Published on October 02, 2026 by MyBnbDesign Team

Glamour Meets Geometry in Art Deco Design

Art Deco is the design language of glamour, luxury, and bold self-expression. Originating in the 1920s and 1930s, Art Deco combines geometric patterns, rich materials, and a jewel-toned color palette to create spaces that feel opulent and exciting. For short-term rentals, Art Deco design creates a memorable, photogenic experience that stands out dramatically from the neutral-palette competition. It appeals to guests seeking luxury, personality, and a sense of occasion.

Art Deco works particularly well in urban properties, boutique-style rentals, and any property that targets guests looking for something extraordinary. It is not for every market, but in the right context, an Art Deco palette can command premium nightly rates and generate the kind of listing photos that go viral.

The Core Art Deco Palette

Deep Emerald Green

Emerald green is the quintessential Art Deco color. Rich, deep, and undeniably luxurious, it creates a dramatic backdrop for gold accents and geometric patterns. Use it on accent walls, in velvet upholstery, or as a bold bathroom tile. Benjamin Moore Chrome Green 2036-10 or Sherwin-Williams Shamrock SW 6454 provide the depth needed for true Art Deco impact. Pair emerald with gold and cream for the most authentic Art Deco combination.

Sapphire Blue

Deep sapphire blue offers an alternative or complement to emerald green. This rich blue references the precious gemstones that inspired Art Deco designers. Use it in bedrooms for a dramatic, enveloping atmosphere or in a powder room for maximum impact. Sapphire blue velvet furniture is a showstopper that guests will photograph and share. Benjamin Moore Champion Cobalt 2061-20 captures this deep, jeweled blue.

Gold and Brass

Gold is not just an accent in Art Deco design. It is a core palette element. Use brass and gold-toned metals in light fixtures, hardware, mirror frames, and furniture legs. A gold sunburst mirror, brass bar cart, or gold-framed gallery wall immediately communicates Art Deco luxury. The warmth of gold prevents the dark jewel tones from feeling cold or heavy. Choose satin or brushed gold finishes for a modern interpretation.

Black and Cream

High contrast between black and cream creates the graphic quality that defines Art Deco. Black and cream checkerboard floors, black lacquer furniture against cream walls, and geometric patterns in these two tones create the bold visual rhythm of the era. Use cream rather than pure white for warmth, and use true black rather than charcoal for drama. This contrast is the backbone of the Art Deco aesthetic.

Art Deco Patterns and Details

Geometric patterns are essential to Art Deco design. Chevrons, sunbursts, fan shapes, and stepped geometric motifs appear in tile work, textiles, and decorative elements. Use patterned tile in bathrooms and entryways. Add geometric wallpaper to a powder room or accent wall. Include geometric throw pillows and art. These patterns provide the visual energy that makes Art Deco spaces feel dynamic and exciting.

Materials in Art Deco design should look and feel luxurious. Velvet upholstery, marble surfaces, lacquered wood, mirrored furniture, and crystal or glass accents all contribute to the glamorous atmosphere. A velvet sofa in emerald or sapphire is the cornerstone piece for an Art Deco living room. A marble-topped console or coffee table adds substance and shine. These materials catch and reflect light beautifully, making listing photos sparkle.

Mirrors are a powerful tool in Art Deco design. Oversized mirrors in geometric frames expand the space and reflect light. Mirrored furniture surfaces add glamour without color. A sunburst mirror above a fireplace or console is a classic Art Deco statement piece. Use mirrors strategically to amplify the richness of your jewel-toned palette and create the sense of abundance that Art Deco design promises.

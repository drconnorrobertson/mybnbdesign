Security Camera Placement Guide for Short-Term Rentals: Where, What, and Why

Best practices for exterior security camera placement at your STR property, including legal requirements and recommended models.

Exterior security cameras protect your vacation rental investment, deter theft and vandalism, and provide valuable evidence if incidents occur. Proper placement maximizes coverage while respecting guest privacy and complying with platform policies. Here is a strategic approach to camera placement at your short-term rental.

Where to Place Cameras

Focus on entry and exit points first. The front door should have dedicated coverage, ideally from a doorbell camera supplemented by a wider-angle camera mounted under the eaves. The back door or patio entrance is the second priority, as this is where most break-ins occur at residential properties. Cover the driveway or parking area to monitor arrivals and departures. If your property has a detached garage, shed, or storage area containing valuable equipment, point a camera at those structures as well.

Where NOT to Place Cameras

Never install cameras that can see into private guest areas. This means no cameras with views into bedrooms, bathrooms, or enclosed outdoor spaces like hot tub enclosures or fenced backyards used for sunbathing. Airbnb and VRBO prohibit interior cameras entirely, including in common areas like living rooms and kitchens. Even exterior cameras must be disclosed in your listing. If a camera can see into a window, reposition it so the view captures only the approach to the building, not the interior.

Camera Height and Angle

Mount cameras between 8 and 10 feet off the ground. This height is high enough to prevent tampering but low enough to capture useful facial details. Angle cameras slightly downward at about 15 to 30 degrees to capture faces rather than the tops of heads. For driveway cameras, position them to capture license plates of parked vehicles. Use the camera's field of view specifications to determine optimal placement. A 130-degree wide-angle lens covers a larger area than a 90-degree lens but may sacrifice detail at the edges of the frame.

Wired vs Wireless Cameras

Wired cameras like the Reolink RLC-810A are more reliable for permanent installations. They maintain consistent video quality, never run out of battery, and are not affected by WiFi congestion. The trade-off is installation complexity since you need to run cables. Wireless cameras like the Arlo Pro 5 and Ring Stick Up Cam offer flexible placement and easy installation but require battery changes every three to six months and depend on strong WiFi signal strength. For vacation rentals, a hybrid approach often works best: wired cameras at primary locations and wireless cameras at secondary spots where running cable is impractical.

Night Vision and Low-Light Performance

Most security incidents at vacation rentals happen after dark, making night vision quality critical. Look for cameras with infrared night vision that provides clear black-and-white footage up to at least 80 feet. Color night vision, available on premium models, uses ambient light to produce color footage at night, which makes identifying people and vehicles much easier. If your property lacks outdoor lighting near camera locations, add a motion-activated flood light to supplement the camera's built-in illumination.

Storage and Monitoring Options

Cloud storage through a subscription service is the most convenient option for STR hosts. Ring Protect, Arlo Secure, and Google Nest Aware all offer 30 to 60 days of cloud video storage with plans starting around $3 to $8 per month per camera. Local storage via an NVR (network video recorder) or SD card eliminates monthly fees but requires physical access to retrieve footage. For vacation rentals, cloud storage is strongly recommended because you can review footage and download clips from anywhere without visiting the property.

Disclosure and Compliance

Transparency about security cameras is both a legal requirement and a trust-building opportunity. List every exterior camera location in your Airbnb and VRBO listing descriptions. Mention cameras in your house rules document. Some hosts place small signs near camera locations stating that the area is under video surveillance. Check your local laws regarding surveillance disclosure, as requirements vary by state and municipality. Guests who feel surprised by undisclosed cameras will leave negative reviews and may report you to the platform, so full transparency is always the safest approach.

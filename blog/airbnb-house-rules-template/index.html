Airbnb House Rules Template: What to Include for Fewer Problems

Set clear expectations and protect your property with well-crafted house rules that guests actually read and follow.

By MyBnBDesign TeamOctober 3, 2026Guest Experience

Why House Rules Matter More Than Most Hosts Realize

Every short-term rental host has a story about the guest who threw an unauthorized party, smoked in the bedroom, or left the kitchen looking like a disaster zone. These situations are frustrating, expensive, and almost always preventable. The difference between hosts who deal with constant headaches and those who run smooth, profitable properties often comes down to one thing: clear, well-presented house rules.

House rules do more than protect your property. They set the tone for the entire guest experience. When guests understand exactly what is expected of them before they arrive, they feel more confident, more comfortable, and more likely to treat your space with respect. Rules eliminate ambiguity, and ambiguity is where most problems begin.

Strong house rules also protect you legally. If a guest causes damage or disturbs your neighbors, having documented rules gives you standing to file a claim through Airbnb's resolution center or pursue other remedies. Without published rules, you are left arguing about what was "implied" or "obvious," which rarely works in your favor.

If you are still in the early stages of acquiring your first short-term rental property, now is the perfect time to draft your house rules. Having them ready before your first guest arrives prevents the trial-and-error approach that costs many new hosts thousands of dollars in damage and lost reviews.

Essential House Rules Categories

Not every property needs the same rules, but certain categories apply to nearly every vacation rental. Below is a comprehensive breakdown of the rules you should consider for your listing.

Maximum Occupancy

State the maximum number of guests allowed clearly and firmly. This number should align with your listing capacity, your insurance policy, and local fire codes. Be specific about whether this includes children and infants. Many hosts write something like: "Maximum 6 guests including children. No additional visitors permitted without prior approval." This simple rule prevents unauthorized gatherings before they start.

Noise and Quiet Hours

Quiet hours are essential, especially in residential neighborhoods or multi-unit buildings. Most hosts set quiet hours between 10:00 PM and 8:00 AM, though you should adjust this based on your local noise ordinances and HOA requirements. Go beyond just stating the hours. Explain what quiet hours mean in practice: no loud music, no outdoor conversations at full volume, and no slamming doors. If your property is in a neighborhood with strict noise expectations, mention this early in your listing so party-minded guests book elsewhere.

Smoking Policy

Be direct about your smoking policy. If smoking is prohibited indoors, say so explicitly and mention that this includes vaping and cannabis products. If you allow smoking in a designated outdoor area, describe where that area is and provide an ashtray. Many hosts include a specific cleaning fee for smoking violations, typically between $250 and $500, which serves as both a deterrent and a practical recovery amount if you need to deep-clean soft furnishings. This approach pairs well with a thoughtfully designed welcome book that reinforces these guidelines upon arrival.

Pet Policy

Decide early whether you will accept pets. If you do, specify which types of animals are welcome, any size or breed restrictions, and whether there is an additional pet fee. Include rules about where pets are allowed (on furniture or not, in bedrooms or not) and require that pets are never left unattended in the property. If you do not allow pets, state this clearly and note that service animals are welcome in accordance with applicable laws.

Parking Instructions

Parking confusion leads to more guest complaints than most hosts expect. Specify how many vehicles are allowed, where they should park, and whether street parking is available as an alternative. If your driveway has specific requirements, such as not blocking a shared entrance, spell these out. For properties in urban areas, include information about nearby parking garages and typical costs. Clear parking rules also prevent awkward confrontations with neighbors.

Kitchen and Appliance Use

Your kitchen is one of the most damage-prone areas of any rental property. Include rules about cleaning up after cooking, not leaving the stove or oven unattended, and properly disposing of grease (never down the drain). If you have specialty appliances like an espresso machine or outdoor grill, provide brief usage instructions and care guidelines. Many hosts find that a simple rule like "please wash and put away all dishes before checkout" dramatically reduces turnover cleaning time.

Checkout Procedures

A clear checkout process makes life easier for everyone. Keep your checkout requirements reasonable so guests do not feel like they are doing your cleaning crew's job. Common checkout rules include: starting the dishwasher or washing dishes, taking out the trash, locking all doors and windows, turning off lights and the thermostat, and placing used towels in the bathtub. Provide a simple checklist in your property's welcome materials so guests can follow along without having to remember everything.

Additional Rules to Consider

  • No parties or events of any kind
  • No candles or open flames
  • Shoes off inside the property (especially for properties with light-colored carpets or hardwood floors)
  • Pool or hot tub safety rules, including no glass near the water
  • Trash and recycling sorting guidelines
  • Exterior security camera disclosure (required by Airbnb's policy)
  • No furniture rearrangement
  • Wi-Fi acceptable use policy

How to Present House Rules Through Design

Writing great house rules is only half the challenge. The other half is making sure guests actually read them. Presentation matters, and this is where professional STR interior design makes a real difference.

Framed Rules in Key Locations

A beautifully framed set of house rules in the entryway sets the tone the moment guests walk through the door. Keep the design clean and on-brand with your property's aesthetic. Use a legible font size, plenty of white space, and limit this framed version to your five or six most important rules. Think of it as the "highlights reel" of your full rules document. Additional framed reminders can be placed in specific areas: a "no smoking" sign near the patio door, pool rules near the pool gate, and a brief checkout checklist near the front entrance.

Digital Welcome Books

Digital guidebooks sent before arrival give guests a chance to review all your rules while they are still planning their trip. Platforms like Hostfully, Touch Stay, and Hospitable let you create branded digital guides that include house rules alongside local recommendations and property instructions. The advantage of digital guides is that you can update them instantly without visiting the property. Include your rules in a dedicated section near the top of the guide, not buried at the bottom where no one scrolls.

Printed Welcome Books

Even in 2026, a physical welcome book remains one of the most effective guest communication tools. Place it on the kitchen counter or coffee table where guests naturally congregate. Use tabs or a clear table of contents so guests can quickly find the house rules section. Pair the rules with helpful context. Instead of just writing "quiet hours 10 PM to 8 AM," add a line explaining that neighbors are close by and that this policy helps everyone enjoy their stay.

Tone and Language Tips

The tone of your house rules can make the difference between a guest who feels welcomed and a guest who feels policed. Here are some guidelines for getting the language right.

Be warm but clear. You can be friendly without being vague. "We kindly ask that you keep noise to a minimum after 10 PM to respect our wonderful neighbors" is better than "NO LOUD NOISE AFTER 10 PM!!!" Both communicate the same rule, but the first version makes guests feel like partners in maintaining a good experience rather than suspects being warned.

Explain the "why" behind important rules. When guests understand the reason for a rule, they are much more likely to follow it. "Please do not pour grease down the kitchen drain, as this can cause expensive plumbing blockages" is more effective than a bare prohibition. People cooperate more readily when they see the logic.

Use positive framing when possible. Instead of "Do not slam the doors," try "Please close doors gently, especially during quiet hours." Instead of "No shoes in the house," try "We provide slippers for your comfort; please leave shoes at the entryway." Positive framing reduces the feeling that your property is full of restrictions.

Avoid legal jargon. Your house rules should read like a note from a thoughtful host, not a lease agreement drafted by an attorney. Save the formal language for your rental agreement. House rules are a communication tool, and they work best when they sound human.

Legal Considerations for House Rules

While your house rules are primarily a communication tool, they do carry some legal weight that is worth understanding.

Local ordinance compliance. Your rules should align with local short-term rental regulations. Many municipalities have specific requirements around maximum occupancy, noise ordinances, parking, and trash collection. Check your local STR permit requirements and make sure your house rules reflect these obligations. Ignorance of local rules will not protect you if a neighbor files a complaint.

Fair housing laws. Your house rules cannot discriminate against protected classes. Rules about the number of occupants are generally acceptable when tied to safety or building codes, but rules that could be seen as targeting families with children, people with disabilities, or other protected groups can create legal exposure. When in doubt, consult with a local attorney who specializes in short-term rental law.

Platform policy alignment. Airbnb, VRBO, and other platforms have their own policies about what hosts can and cannot require. For example, Airbnb requires hosts to disclose exterior security cameras and prohibits cameras in private spaces. Make sure your house rules do not conflict with your platform's terms of service, as this could jeopardize your listing or your ability to win disputes.

Insurance requirements. Review your STR insurance policy to understand what rules your insurer expects you to enforce. Many policies have requirements around pool safety, fire safety, and guest screening that should be reflected in your house rules. If your insurance requires a specific maximum occupancy, your house rules should match.

Handling Rule Violations

Even the best house rules will occasionally be broken. How you handle violations matters just as much as the rules themselves.

For minor violations, a polite message through the Airbnb platform is usually sufficient. Reference the specific rule, keep the tone friendly, and give the guest a chance to correct the behavior. Most guests will apologize and comply immediately. Something like "Hi [Guest], just a friendly reminder that quiet hours begin at 10 PM. The neighbors have let us know about some noise. Would you mind keeping things down? Thank you so much!" resolves 90% of situations.

For serious violations, such as an unauthorized party, smoking indoors, or exceeding the maximum guest count significantly, act quickly. Document everything with photos, timestamps, and any communication from neighbors. Contact Airbnb support through the platform and request assistance. If the situation poses a safety risk, do not hesitate to involve local authorities. Having your rules clearly published in your listing, in your pre-arrival messaging, and inside the property gives you a strong foundation for any dispute resolution process.

Consider implementing a graduated response system. A first-time minor violation gets a friendly reminder. A repeated violation or a first-time serious violation triggers a formal warning through the platform. Continued violations may result in early checkout, forfeiture of the security deposit, or a review that accurately describes the guest's behavior. Documenting this system in advance helps you respond calmly and consistently rather than reacting emotionally in the moment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many house rules should an Airbnb listing have?

Most successful Airbnb listings include between 8 and 15 house rules. Focus on the rules that matter most for your specific property and neighborhood. Too few rules leave gaps that lead to misunderstandings, while too many rules overwhelm guests and reduce booking conversions. Prioritize safety rules, noise policies, and any property-specific requirements that protect your investment.

Should I include house rules in my Airbnb listing or only share them after booking?

Include your most important house rules in your Airbnb listing description so guests can review them before booking. This filters out guests who cannot comply and reduces disputes later. After booking, send a more detailed version through the Airbnb messaging system and reinforce key rules in your welcome book or printed materials at the property. Transparency before booking builds trust and attracts the right guests.

What should I do if a guest breaks my house rules?

Start by documenting the violation with photos, timestamps, or security camera footage from exterior areas. Send the guest a polite but firm message through Airbnb referencing the specific rule that was broken. For minor violations, a friendly reminder is usually enough. For serious violations like unauthorized parties or smoking indoors, contact Airbnb support immediately and request early checkout if needed. Having clear rules published in your listing strengthens your position in any dispute resolution.

EV Charger for Vacation Rentals: Attract More Guests and Charge Premium Rates

Why adding an EV charger to your short-term rental is a smart investment, which charger to choose, and how to offset the cost.

Electric vehicle ownership is growing rapidly, and EV drivers actively seek vacation rentals with charging capabilities. Adding an EV charger to your short-term rental property differentiates your listing, attracts a growing demographic of travelers, and justifies premium nightly rates. Here is everything you need to know about installing and managing an EV charger at your vacation rental.

The Business Case for EV Charging

EV drivers plan their trips around charging availability. Properties listed on platforms like PlugShare and ChargeHub attract travelers who filter specifically for rentals with EV charging. Hosts who add EV chargers report a 10 to 20 percent increase in bookings and the ability to charge $15 to $30 more per night. On Airbnb, you can add "EV charger" as an amenity filter, making your property visible to a growing segment of travelers who might otherwise skip your listing entirely. The investment pays for itself through increased occupancy and higher nightly rates.

Level 1 vs Level 2 Charging

Level 1 charging uses a standard 120-volt household outlet and adds about 3 to 5 miles of range per hour. This is too slow for most vacation rental guests who need a meaningful charge during their stay. Level 2 charging uses a 240-volt circuit and adds 25 to 40 miles of range per hour, providing a full charge overnight. For vacation rentals, Level 2 is the standard you should install. A guest arriving with a depleted battery can have a full charge by morning, which is exactly what EV drivers expect when they book a property with charging.

Best EV Chargers for Vacation Rentals

The ChargePoint Home Flex is the top choice for vacation rental installations. It supports 16 to 50 amps on a single unit, connects to WiFi for remote monitoring, and has a clean design suitable for exterior mounting. The Grizzl-E Duo offers dual charging connections from a single unit, which is valuable if your property has parking for multiple vehicles. For budget installations, the Lectron V-Box is a reliable Level 2 charger at a lower price point. All three options include the J1772 connector that works with every EV on the market except Tesla, and Tesla owners can use the adapter included with their vehicle.

Installation Requirements

A Level 2 EV charger requires a dedicated 240-volt circuit from your electrical panel. Most installations need a 40 to 60 amp circuit breaker and appropriate gauge wiring run from the panel to the charger location. Hire a licensed electrician for this work. Installation costs typically range from $500 to $1,500 depending on the distance from the panel to the charging location and whether your panel has available capacity. Some panels may need an upgrade to accommodate the additional load, which adds to the cost.

Location and Mounting

Mount the charger in a covered area near your designated parking spot. A garage is ideal, but a carport or exterior wall with an overhang works well. The charging cable should easily reach the charging port on vehicles parked in your designated spot. Most EV chargers come with 18 to 25 feet of cable, which is sufficient for most installations. Install a cable management hook to keep the cord off the ground when not in use. If the charger is outdoors, ensure it is rated for outdoor installation and protected from direct rain exposure.

Managing Electricity Costs

A full charge for a typical EV costs $8 to $15 in electricity. You can absorb this cost by building it into your nightly rate, or you can set a flat charging fee. Most vacation rental hosts simply increase their nightly rate by $10 to $15 when advertising EV charging, which more than covers the electricity cost while remaining attractive to EV-driving guests. If you want precise cost tracking, choose a smart charger that reports energy consumption per session, allowing you to monitor actual costs and adjust your pricing accordingly.

Marketing Your EV Amenity

List your EV charger prominently in your amenity list on Airbnb and VRBO. Include the charger type, charging speed, and connector information in your listing description. Add your property to PlugShare and ChargeHub so EV drivers can discover it when planning trips. Take a clear photo of the charger and parking area for your listing photos. Mention the EV charger in your property title or subtitle if character limits allow. This visibility drives bookings from a high-value guest demographic that tends to book longer stays and leave positive reviews.

The Return of Color in Vacation Rental Design

Published 2026-10-02

After years of all-white and greige domination, color is making a bold and welcome return to vacation rental design. The pendulum has swung decisively away from the neutral-everything approach, and STR hosts who embrace color strategically are seeing remarkable results in both booking rates and guest satisfaction. This does not mean painting every wall a different neon shade; the return of color is thoughtful, sophisticated, and grounded in an understanding of how color affects mood, perception, and the overall guest experience.

Why Color Is Coming Back

The neutral wave served its purpose, providing a safe, broadly appealing palette that helped professionalize the vacation rental industry. But safety became sameness, and guests scrolling through hundreds of white-walled listings began craving spaces with personality and warmth. The return of color responds to this fatigue by offering properties that feel alive, expressive, and memorable. Research in environmental psychology supports this shift, showing that thoughtfully applied color improves mood, reduces stress, and creates stronger positive memories. In practical terms, colored spaces are more likely to stop a scrolling guest, earn a save, and convert to a booking.

Strategic Color Placement

The key to successful color in STR design is strategic placement rather than blanket application. Not every wall needs to be colorful. Instead, choose moments where color will have the greatest impact. A rich terracotta accent wall behind the bed creates a warm focal point. A deep green bathroom feels spa-like and luxurious. A vibrant kitchen backsplash adds energy to the heart of the home. A boldly painted front door creates an instant first impression. By concentrating color in specific locations, you create visual variety and interest while maintaining the calm, cohesive flow that guests need in a space they are experiencing for the first time.

The New Color Palette

The colors dominating the STR design conversation are not the pastels or primaries of previous color trends. They are rich, complex tones drawn from nature and historical architecture. Warm terracottas and clay pinks reference Mediterranean earthenware. Deep forest greens and olive tones bring the outdoors inside. Warm navy and indigo add depth without darkness. Ochre and turmeric yellows provide sunny warmth. Dusty sage and eucalyptus create calming freshness. These colors share a common quality: they feel natural and organic rather than artificial, and they pair beautifully with natural materials like wood, stone, and linen.

Color and Mood in Different Rooms

Different rooms serve different purposes, and color choices should reflect those functions. Bedrooms benefit from calming colors that promote sleep: deep blues, soft greens, warm mauves, and rich earthy tones. Living areas can handle more energizing colors: warm terracotta, golden yellow, or vibrant green creates social energy. Bathrooms are ideal spaces for bold color experiments because their small size makes even dramatic colors feel intimate rather than overwhelming. Kitchens and dining areas respond well to warm, appetite-enhancing tones like terracotta, warm cream, and sage green. The key is matching the emotional quality of the color to the intended experience of the room.

Mixing Color with Neutrals

The most successful colored STR interiors maintain a strong neutral foundation and introduce color as a complement rather than a replacement. A warm neutral sofa grounds a room where colorful throw pillows, art, and rugs provide visual excitement. White kitchen cabinets pair beautifully with a colorful tile backsplash. Neutral bedding becomes more interesting against a colored headboard wall. This approach provides the flexibility to update colors as trends evolve without requiring major renovations. It also ensures that the space does not feel overwhelming to guests who may have different color preferences than the host.

Color Through Textiles and Accessories

For hosts who are not ready to commit to painted walls, color can be introduced through more easily changeable elements. Colorful throw pillows, vibrant rugs, bold artwork, colored glassware, and bright table linens all inject color into a neutral space without permanent commitment. These elements can be swapped seasonally, providing variety for repeat guests and allowing you to test color responses before committing to more permanent applications. A curated collection of colorful ceramics on open kitchen shelving, a bold striped outdoor rug on the patio, or a collection of colorful vintage books on a coffee table can transform the character of a room.

Making the Transition to Color

If your property currently follows the all-neutral playbook, the transition to color should be gradual and strategic. Start with one room, preferably the one with the best natural light and the most time spent by guests. Choose a single color family and introduce it through an accent wall, coordinating textiles, and one or two accessories. Photograph the result and compare it to your previous listing images. In most cases, the colored version will feel warmer, more inviting, and more distinctive. Once you are comfortable with color in one room, extend the palette into adjacent spaces, maintaining consistency through shared color tones while allowing each room its own character. The guests who book your colorful property will be doing so because they are drawn to that warmth and personality, and they will arrive predisposed to love the space that caught their eye online.

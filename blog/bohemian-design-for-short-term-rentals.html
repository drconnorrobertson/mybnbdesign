Bohemian Design for Short Term Rentals

By MyBnBDesign Team • October 02, 2026

Why Bohemian Design Creates Memorable STR Experiences

Bohemian design offers short term rental owners something that many other styles cannot: personality. In a market saturated with identical white-and-gray interiors, a well-executed bohemian space stands out immediately in search results and creates the kind of memorable experience that generates organic word-of-mouth marketing.

The bohemian aesthetic celebrates collected, layered, and globally inspired interiors. It appeals to a growing segment of travelers who value unique experiences over cookie-cutter accommodations. These guests are often willing to pay premium rates for a space that feels one-of-a-kind.

Creating a Cohesive Bohemian Palette

Bohemian design can feel chaotic if not approached with intention. Start with warm neutrals as your base: terra cotta, warm white, camel, and soft rust. Layer in deeper accent colors like mustard yellow, olive green, and burnt orange. The palette should feel warm and earthy rather than bright and jarring.

Pattern mixing is essential to bohemian design, but it requires a strategic approach. Choose a dominant pattern scale and let it lead. A large-scale kilim rug can anchor a room while smaller-scale patterns in pillows and throws provide supporting texture. Keep patterns within your established color family to maintain cohesion.

Furniture and Layout for Bohemian Rentals

Low-profile furniture creates the relaxed atmosphere that defines bohemian spaces. Floor cushions, low coffee tables, and daybeds encourage lounging and conversation. Mix vintage and new pieces to create a collected-over-time feeling. A mid-century credenza paired with a handwoven accent chair creates interesting visual tension.

Bedrooms should feel like a cocoon. Layer the bed with a textured coverlet, a woven blanket, and an assortment of pillows in complementary patterns. A canopy of sheer fabric draped above the bed adds drama and romance. Include bedside tables at different heights or styles for an intentionally mismatched look.

Create distinct zones within open floor plans using area rugs and furniture groupings. A reading nook with a hanging chair, a dining area with a low table and floor cushions, or a meditation corner with plants and candles all add functional interest that guests appreciate.

Textiles, Plants, and Accessories

Textiles are the backbone of bohemian design. Macrame wall hangings, woven tapestries, embroidered pillows, and fringed throws add layers of texture and visual warmth. Source textiles from global artisan markets or fair-trade suppliers to add authentic stories to your space.

Plants are non-negotiable in bohemian interiors. Trailing pothos in hanging planters, tall snake plants in woven baskets, and clusters of succulents on shelves bring life and freshness. If maintaining live plants is impractical, invest in premium artificial options and mix them with dried grasses and pampas for natural variety.

Lighting should be warm and atmospheric. String lights, Moroccan lanterns, salt lamps, and candles create the intimate glow that makes bohemian spaces magical in the evening. Include practical task lighting as well so guests can read comfortably.

Bohemian Design That Books

The key to profitable bohemian design is balancing personality with functionality. Every space must still be comfortable, clean, and intuitive for guests. Include plenty of storage so surfaces do not become cluttered. Provide clear workspace areas for digital nomads. Ensure the kitchen and bathroom are fully functional despite the decorative emphasis.

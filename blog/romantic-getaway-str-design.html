Romantic Getaway STR Design: Creating Unforgettable Escapes

By MyBnBDesign Team · October 2, 2026

The romantic getaway market is one of the most consistent and profitable niches in short-term rentals. Couples booking anniversary trips, honeymoons, proposals, and weekend escapes are willing to pay premium rates for a property that creates the right atmosphere. Unlike group properties that compete on capacity, romantic rentals compete on experience -- the feeling of intimacy, luxury, and escape that a couple experiences from the moment they arrive. Here is how to design an STR that couples never forget.

Privacy as the Foundation

Romance requires seclusion, and your property's sense of privacy starts before guests even walk through the door. The approach to the property should feel like an arrival at a private retreat -- a tree-lined driveway, a gated entry, or a winding path that separates the property from neighbors and road noise. Landscaping plays a critical role here -- mature hedges, privacy fencing softened with climbing vines, and strategic tree placement can create a sense of seclusion even in areas where properties are relatively close together.

Inside, the layout should feel cocooning rather than expansive. Romantic properties benefit from intimacy of scale -- a cozy one-bedroom cottage outperforms a large multi-bedroom house for this market. Every space should feel intentional and contained. Windows should frame views rather than expose the interior to neighboring properties. If your property has outdoor spaces like a patio, hot tub, or garden, screen them with lattice panels, bamboo fencing, or lush plantings so couples can enjoy the outdoors without feeling observed. A private outdoor shower surrounded by tropical plants or a garden wall creates a uniquely intimate amenity that couples find thrilling.

Dimmable Lighting and Candlelight

Lighting is the single most powerful tool for creating romantic atmosphere, and it is the element most rental properties get wrong. Every light fixture in a romantic getaway should be on a dimmer switch -- every single one. Overhead lighting should be supplemented or replaced by table lamps, wall sconces, and floor lamps that distribute warm, low light at eye level rather than harsh illumination from above. Choose bulbs in the 2200K to 2700K range for a warm amber glow that flatters skin tones and creates visual warmth.

Beyond electric lighting, invest in candlelight infrastructure. Place quality candle holders -- pillar candle stands, hurricane lanterns, and votive holders -- throughout the property in safe locations. Use LED flameless candles for fire safety in unattended areas, but provide real candles as well for guests who prefer the authentic flicker and subtle scent. A fireplace -- gas or electric -- provides both ambient light and the hypnotic movement of flame that draws couples close together. The bathroom should have dedicated candle placement around the tub, with a small tray or shelf designed to hold candles safely near the water.

The Luxury Bedroom

The bedroom is the centerpiece of any romantic property, and it should feel dramatically more luxurious than a typical rental bedroom. Start with the bed itself -- a king-size mattress of the highest quality you can afford, dressed in premium white hotel-weight sheets (at least 400 thread count sateen or percale), a plush down or down-alternative duvet, and an abundance of pillows in varying firmness. A tufted or upholstered headboard in velvet, linen, or leather adds visual luxury and provides a comfortable surface for sitting up in bed.

Layer the sensory experience. A cashmere or high-quality throw blanket at the foot of the bed invites touch. Blackout curtains in a rich fabric -- silk, velvet, or heavy linen -- allow couples to sleep in without the interruption of morning light. A bedside carafe of water with glasses eliminates the need to leave the room. A high-quality Bluetooth speaker on the nightstand lets couples set their own soundtrack. Place a silk or satin robe set in the closet -- his and hers or two matching sets -- as an amenity that guests will wear throughout their stay and associate forever with your property.

The Soaking Tub for Two

A soaking tub designed for two people is the signature amenity of a romantic getaway property. This is not a standard bathtub -- it is a freestanding statement piece positioned as the focal point of the bathroom or even the bedroom itself. A freestanding clawfoot tub, a modern oval soaking tub, or a Japanese-style deep soaking tub should be large enough for two adults to sit comfortably together. Position it near a window with a view if possible, or against a feature wall that creates a sense of drama.

The tub area needs supporting amenities. A bath tray that spans the width of the tub provides a surface for wine glasses, candles, and a book. Stock the bathroom with luxury bath products -- bath salts, bubble bath, essential oils, and body wash in full-size bottles rather than travel sizes. Heated towel racks ensure warm towels are always within reach. A rainfall showerhead in a separate glass-enclosed shower complements the tub for quick rinses. The bathroom flooring should be heated -- stepping out of a warm bath onto cold tile breaks the spell that the entire experience has been building.

Curated Experiences and Personal Touches

Romantic getaway guests want more than a place to sleep -- they want an experience that feels designed for their relationship. Create optional add-on packages that enhance the stay. A charcuterie and wine welcome package, a bouquet of fresh flowers, a private couples massage arranged through a local therapist, or a private dinner prepared by a local chef can all be offered at booking for an additional fee. These upgrades increase your revenue per booking and differentiate your property from competitors.

Design the property with experience moments built in. A record player with a curated collection of vinyl -- jazz, soul, bossa nova -- provides a romantic soundtrack that streaming cannot replicate. A library of poetry books and art collections on the coffee table offers quiet shared entertainment. A telescope on a patio positioned for stargazing, a hammock for two in a shaded garden corner, or a private fire pit with a stack of firewood and marshmallows creates moments that couples share together. Each of these touchpoints shows that you have thought about the experience of being in love in your space, not just the logistics of accommodation.

The Welcome Basket That Sets the Tone

First impressions define the entire stay, and a thoughtful welcome basket is your opening statement. For a romantic property, skip the generic snack basket and curate something that signals indulgence. A bottle of sparkling wine or champagne chilling in an ice bucket, a selection of artisan chocolates from a local chocolatier, fresh strawberries, and a handwritten welcome note set the tone from the first moment. Include a small printed guide to romantic activities in the area -- sunset viewpoints, couples' spa treatments, intimate restaurants that take reservations, and scenic walking trails.

The presentation matters as much as the contents. Use a beautiful tray, a woven basket, or a wooden crate that becomes part of the room's aesthetic rather than something to be unpacked and discarded. Tie a ribbon or add a small flower sprig as a finishing touch. This level of care communicates that your property was prepared specifically for this couple, not cleaned and restocked by a turnover crew. That feeling of personal attention -- even when it is systematized -- is what earns the five-star reviews, the social media posts, and the repeat bookings that build a successful romantic getaway brand.

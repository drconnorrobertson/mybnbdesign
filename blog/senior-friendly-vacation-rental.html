Senior-Friendly Vacation Rental Design Tips

By MyBnBDesign Team · September 23, 2026

The senior travel market is massive and growing. Adults over 65 represent one of the wealthiest and most active travel demographics in the country, and they are increasingly choosing vacation rentals over hotels. Many travel with extended family -- grandchildren, adult children, siblings -- and they want a property where everyone can gather comfortably under one roof. But senior travelers have specific needs that most rental properties overlook entirely. A few thoughtful design modifications can make your property welcoming to this audience while keeping it beautiful and appealing to all guests.

The best part is that senior-friendly design is not expensive or complicated. Most modifications improve the experience for every guest, not just older travelers. Here is how to design a vacation rental that senior guests love -- and that earns loyal, repeat bookings from multi-generational families.

Safety Lighting Throughout the Property

Falls are the leading cause of injury for adults over 65, and poor lighting is one of the primary contributing factors. Your property needs consistent, bright, non-glare lighting in every room, hallway, and outdoor pathway. Start by replacing any dim or flickering light fixtures with LED options that produce warm, even illumination. Avoid harsh overhead fluorescents -- they create glare that makes it harder for aging eyes to see, not easier.

Nightlights are essential in bathrooms, hallways, and bedrooms. Motion-activated LED nightlights that plug into outlets along the path from the bedroom to the bathroom prevent middle-of-the-night falls without requiring guests to find light switches in an unfamiliar space. Outdoor lighting is equally important -- illuminate the path from the parking area to the front door, and ensure porches, decks, and stairs are well-lit after dark. Solar pathway lights are inexpensive, require no wiring, and dramatically improve nighttime navigation.

Light switches should be easy to locate and operate. Rocker-style switches are easier to use than traditional toggle switches, especially for guests with arthritis or limited hand strength. Smart lighting that responds to voice commands eliminates the need to find switches entirely -- a simple "turn on the kitchen lights" solves the problem for everyone.

Slip Prevention on Every Surface

Slippery surfaces are a serious hazard for older guests. Start with the bathroom -- the highest-risk area. Apply non-slip strips or a non-slip mat inside the tub or shower. Choose a textured, matte-finish tile for the bathroom floor rather than polished porcelain or marble. Ensure bath mats have rubber backing that stays firmly in place when stepped on.

Throughout the property, secure all area rugs with non-slip pads or double-sided tape. Remove any rugs that bunch, curl at the edges, or slide on hard floors -- they are tripping hazards regardless of how beautiful they look. If the property has stairs, ensure every step has a non-slip surface and that handrails are installed on both sides. Outdoor surfaces -- decks, patios, walkways -- should be treated with non-slip coatings or made from textured materials that provide traction when wet.

Comfortable, Supportive Furniture

Furniture selection matters more for senior guests than most hosts realize. Low-slung sofas, beanbag chairs, and deep-cushioned seating that young guests find comfortable can be nearly impossible for older adults to get out of. Choose sofas and chairs with firm cushions, armrests, and a seat height of at least 18 inches. Armrests are particularly important -- they give guests something to push against when standing up.

The bed height should be between 20 and 23 inches from the floor to the top of the mattress. This is the optimal height for getting in and out of bed without straining. A mattress that is too high or too low creates a fall risk and makes the bed uncomfortable to use. Provide firm pillows alongside soft ones -- many older adults need more support for their neck and spine. A bedside table on each side of the bed, sturdy enough to lean on, provides a stable surface for glasses, medications, and a water glass.

Dining chairs should have backs and armrests. Bar stools are problematic for senior guests -- if your kitchen has a bar height counter, ensure there is also a standard-height dining option where everyone can eat comfortably. Avoid glass tables with sharp edges, and ensure all furniture is stable and heavy enough that it does not tip or slide when leaned on.

Lever Handles and Easy-to-Use Hardware

Round doorknobs require a tight grip and a twisting motion that can be painful or impossible for guests with arthritis. Replace all doorknobs with lever-style handles that can be operated with a light push. This is one of the simplest and most impactful modifications you can make -- it costs $15 to $30 per door and takes minutes to install. Apply the same principle to faucets. Lever or single-handle faucets in the kitchen and bathroom are dramatically easier to use than knobs or cross-handle designs.

Cabinet hardware should be pulls rather than knobs -- long, easy-to-grasp pulls that do not require pinching or twisting. Appliances with large, clearly labeled buttons are preferable to those with small touch screens or complicated digital interfaces. A simple drip coffee maker is more senior-friendly than an espresso machine with fifteen buttons. The TV remote should be a standard model with large buttons -- not a minimalist smart remote where every function requires navigating a menu.

Large Print Guides and Clear Communication

Create a house guide in large print -- at least 14-point font, with high contrast between text and background. Many hosts provide beautiful guidebooks with stylish typography that is virtually unreadable for guests with aging vision. Your guide should cover WiFi access, appliance instructions, emergency contacts, check-out procedures, and local recommendations. Use clear, simple language and include photos where possible.

Label light switches, thermostat controls, and appliances with simple, readable labels. A small laminated card next to the thermostat explaining how to adjust the temperature saves confusion and prevents guests from accidentally setting the heat to 90 degrees. In the kitchen, label any non-obvious appliances -- the garbage disposal switch, the instant hot water tap, the convection oven setting. These details reduce anxiety for guests who are not comfortable with unfamiliar technology.

The Multi-Generational Booking Advantage

Senior-friendly properties attract multi-generational families -- grandparents, parents, and children traveling together. These bookings are among the most valuable in the STR market. Multi-generational groups book large properties for extended stays, often during peak holiday periods. They are willing to pay premium rates for a property that comfortably accommodates everyone, from toddlers to grandparents. By making your property safe and welcoming for senior travelers, you are not narrowing your market -- you are opening the door to the largest, most lucrative family bookings available. The return on investment for senior-friendly design modifications is swift, substantial, and sustained over time.

Why Your Guest Reviews Mention Design Problems

By MyBnBDesign Team • October 02, 2026

Reading Between the Lines of Your Reviews

Guests rarely say 'the interior design was poor.' Instead, they write about symptoms. 'The bed was uncomfortable.' 'The bathroom felt outdated.' 'The place was smaller than expected.' 'It did not feel as clean as it looked in photos.' Each of these common review complaints has a design root cause that, once identified, can be fixed permanently.

Understanding how guests experience and describe design problems is the first step toward eliminating the review issues that keep your rating below 4.8 and your bookings below capacity.

Comfort Complaints Are Design Problems

When guests complain about comfort, they are telling you about design failures. An uncomfortable bed is a mattress and bedding purchasing decision. A bathroom that 'feels dated' is about fixtures, finishes, and accessories. A kitchen that 'is not well equipped' is about both tools and layout. A space that 'feels cramped' is about furniture scale, placement, and visual weight.

Track every review comment that touches on physical experience. Categorize them by room and by type: comfort, aesthetics, functionality, and cleanliness perception. Patterns will emerge quickly, and those patterns point directly to the design investments that will have the highest impact on your review scores.

Cleanliness Perception Is a Design Issue

Some of the most damaging review comments involve cleanliness, and many of them are actually design problems masquerading as cleaning issues. Dark grout shows every water spot. Certain countertop materials show fingerprints and smudges minutes after cleaning. Textured wall finishes trap dust visibly. Carpeted floors always look less clean than hard surfaces, even when freshly vacuumed.

Replacing materials that look dirty with materials that maintain a clean appearance between cleanings eliminates an entire category of negative reviews. Light-colored grout, matte-finish countertops, smooth wall surfaces, and hard flooring all reduce cleaning effort while improving perceived cleanliness.

The 'Smaller Than Expected' Problem

When guests say a space is smaller than expected, the issue is usually not the actual square footage but the way design choices make the space feel. Oversized furniture in small rooms, dark walls that visually shrink a space, heavy window treatments that block light, and cluttered surfaces all make rooms feel significantly smaller than their dimensions suggest.

The fix involves right-sizing furniture to room proportions, using light and reflective surfaces to create a sense of openness, choosing furniture with visible legs that allow sight lines to the floor, and maintaining clear pathways that make movement through the space feel effortless.

Turning Design Into Five-Star Reviews

The properties that consistently earn five-star reviews are not necessarily the most expensive or the most luxurious. They are the ones where every design decision supports guest comfort, convenience, and delight. Thoughtful touches like quality bedding, good lighting, intuitive layout, and cohesive aesthetics create the experience that guests describe as 'perfect' and 'exactly as pictured.'

Invest in solving the design problems your reviews identify, and watch your rating climb. Every tenth of a point above 4.8 translates directly to increased visibility, higher booking rates, and the ability to command premium nightly rates in your market.

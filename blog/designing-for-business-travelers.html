Designing Your STR for Business Travelers

By MyBnBDesign Team · September 17, 2026

Business travelers are the dream guest segment for short-term rental hosts. They book midweek when leisure travelers do not. They are tidy, low-maintenance, and often expense their stays -- meaning they are less price-sensitive than vacation guests. Best of all, a business traveler who has a great experience at your property will book it again and again, sometimes for weeks or months at a time.

But attracting this audience requires more than listing your property on Airbnb for Work and hoping for the best. Business travelers have specific needs, and properties that meet those needs will win their loyalty. Here is how to design a short-term rental that professional travelers choose over hotels every time.

The Dedicated Workspace

A desk in the corner of a bedroom is not a workspace -- it is an afterthought. Business travelers need a real, dedicated area where they can sit for eight or more hours and be productive. This means a full-size desk -- at least 48 inches wide -- with enough surface area for a laptop, notebook, and coffee. Position it near a window for natural light, but ensure there is no glare on the screen.

The chair matters enormously. A dining chair pulled up to a desk is a one-star review waiting to happen. Invest in a quality ergonomic office chair with adjustable height, lumbar support, and armrests. This single purchase can cost between $300 and $600, but it pays for itself in the first few bookings from business guests who specifically filter for workspace quality.

Provide an external monitor -- a 24 or 27 inch display with an HDMI cable and a USB-C adapter. This instantly elevates your property above 95 percent of competing listings. Add a power strip with USB-A and USB-C ports within arm's reach of the desk, and include an extra phone charger. If the room allows, position the desk away from the bed so the workspace feels separate from the sleeping area.

Internet That Actually Works

Fast, reliable internet is the single most important amenity for business travelers -- more important than the bed, the kitchen, or the view. You need at minimum 100 Mbps download speed, and ideally 200 Mbps or higher. But speed alone is not enough. The connection must be stable, with consistent performance throughout the day and evening.

Invest in a mesh WiFi system that eliminates dead zones throughout the property. Place a node in or near the workspace area to ensure the strongest possible signal where it matters most. Post the WiFi network name and password in a visible location near the desk -- do not make guests hunt for it. Include your speed test results in your listing description. Business travelers will screenshot their own speed tests, and a property that delivers on its internet promise earns trust and repeat bookings.

Consider providing an Ethernet cable and a small network switch at the desk for guests who need wired connections for video calls or large file transfers. This level of detail signals that you understand what business travelers actually need.

Quiet and Privacy

Business travelers often have early morning calls, late-night deadlines, and video conferences throughout the day. Noise is their enemy. If your property is in a busy area, invest in sound-dampening measures -- heavy curtains, weatherstripping on windows, and rugs on hard floors. A white noise machine on the nightstand helps guests sleep through urban sounds.

Privacy matters too. Ensure the workspace is not visible from windows or common areas if the property is shared. Blackout curtains in the bedroom allow guests to sleep on their own schedule, especially those traveling across time zones. If the property has multiple bedrooms, designate the quietest room as the primary suite and note it in your listing.

Self-Check-In and Seamless Arrival

Business travelers arrive at unpredictable hours -- late flights, early mornings, and everything in between. A smart lock with keyless entry is essential. No lockboxes with fumbling combinations in the dark. No coordinating key handoffs with a local manager. The guest should receive a code, walk in, and find everything they need without human interaction.

Create a simple, one-page digital guide that covers the essentials -- WiFi password, thermostat instructions, parking, and nearby restaurants for delivery. Business travelers do not want to read a 20-page house manual. They want to drop their bag, connect to WiFi, and get to work. Respect their time and they will respect your property.

Kitchen and Coffee Essentials

Business travelers eat differently than vacationers. They want a quick breakfast, coffee that rivals a cafe, and the ability to heat up takeout. A quality drip coffee maker or single-serve machine with premium pods is a must. Stock the kitchen with basics -- olive oil, salt, pepper, and a few pantry staples -- so guests can make simple meals without a grocery run.

A full kitchen is a selling point over hotels, but it does not need to be gourmet. Focus on the appliances that matter -- a reliable microwave, a toaster, and a dishwasher. Provide a set of to-go containers and a reusable water bottle as a thoughtful touch. Many business travelers will eat out for dinner but appreciate the ability to make breakfast and lunch at the property.

Building Repeat Booking Relationships

The real revenue from business travelers comes from repeat bookings. After a great first stay, send a brief thank-you message and offer a small discount for direct rebooking. Many business travelers visit the same city monthly or quarterly. A property that becomes their go-to is essentially a guaranteed income stream. Keep their preferences on file -- pillow firmness, coffee brand, check-in time -- and they will never look at another listing again. This attention to detail is what separates amateur hosting from professional hospitality.

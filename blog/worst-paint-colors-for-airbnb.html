The Worst Paint Colors for Airbnb

By MyBnBDesign Team • October 02, 2026

Color Choices That Cost You Money

Paint is the cheapest way to transform a space, but the wrong color choice can make an entire property feel uninviting, dated, or clinical. Certain colors that look acceptable in paint swatches create problems when applied to full rooms, especially under the mixed lighting conditions and varying screen calibrations that affect how your listing photos appear to potential guests.

The worst paint colors for short-term rentals share common problems: they photograph poorly, they clash with most furniture and decor, they create unwanted psychological effects, or they show dirt and wear faster than alternatives.

Colors That Photograph Terribly

Bright white is the most common mistake. While it seems safe and clean, pure white walls photograph as cold, sterile, and institutional. Cameras amplify the blue undertones in bright white paint, creating an unwelcoming hospital-like appearance. Instead, use warm whites with yellow or cream undertones that photograph as bright but inviting.

Builder beige, the default color in most newly constructed properties, photographs as dull and muddy. It lacks the warmth of true tan and the crispness of true white, landing in an unflattering middle ground that communicates 'default choice' rather than 'intentional design.' Certain grays shift dramatically under different light sources and on different screens. A gray that looks sophisticated on your phone might appear purple, blue, or green on a guest's monitor.

Colors That Create the Wrong Mood

Dark red and burgundy create an oppressive, heavy feeling in bedrooms where guests need to feel relaxed and restful. While these colors can work as accents, full walls in deep reds make spaces feel smaller and more intense than comfortable for sleeping. Bright yellow, despite its cheerful reputation, creates anxiety and visual fatigue in large doses. Yellow walls in bedrooms and living areas are consistently rated as less appealing in guest surveys.

Dark charcoal and black accent walls absorb light and make rooms feel smaller and moodier than they actually are. In listing photos, dark walls create exposure problems that make the rest of the room look washed out or the dark wall look like a black void. Bright, saturated accent colors like lime green, electric blue, or hot pink polarize guests and date quickly. What feels fresh and bold during installation looks trendy and tiresome within a season.

The Proven Palette for Bookings

Warm neutrals with subtle undertones are the proven winners for vacation rentals. Creamy whites, soft greiges (gray-beige blends), warm putty tones, and muted sage greens photograph consistently well across devices and create universally appealing spaces that let furnishings and art take center stage.

If you want color, apply it strategically on a single accent wall and keep remaining walls in a complementary neutral. Deep navy, forest green, and warm terracotta work as accent colors that photograph beautifully, create visual interest, and remain appealing across design trend cycles. Always test paint colors in the actual room at different times of day and photograph them with the same camera and lighting you use for listing photos before committing to a full room.

The Repainting ROI

Repainting a property in optimized colors typically costs between $2,000 and $5,000 depending on size. Properties that switch from poorly chosen colors to photographically proven palettes consistently report increased booking rates within the first month of updated listing photos. That makes paint one of the highest-ROI design investments available to rental property owners.

Choose colors that work for the camera, not just for the eye. Your listing photos are your primary sales tool, and every color in every room should support, not undermine, their selling power.

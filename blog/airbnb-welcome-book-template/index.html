Airbnb Welcome Book Template: Complete Guide to Guest Communication

Everything you need to create a welcome book that impresses guests and reduces questions from check-in to checkout.

By MyBnBDesign TeamOctober 3, 2026Guest Experience

What Is an Airbnb Welcome Book and Why Does It Matter?

A welcome book is a comprehensive guide that provides your guests with everything they need to know about your short-term rental property. Think of it as a combination of an instruction manual, a local travel guide, and a personal note from you as the host. It covers the practical details of your space, sets expectations through house rules, and helps guests feel at home from the moment they walk through the door.

The welcome book serves a dual purpose. For guests, it creates a smooth, stress-free experience by answering common questions before they even arise. For hosts, it dramatically reduces the number of late-night texts asking for the WiFi password or instructions on how to operate the coffee maker. A well-crafted welcome book can be the difference between a four-star review and a five-star review, because it shows guests that you have thought about every detail of their stay.

Properties with professional welcome books consistently earn higher ratings for communication and check-in experience. These are two of the most heavily weighted categories in Airbnb's review system. If you are acquiring a new short-term rental property, building a welcome book should be one of the first items on your launch checklist, right alongside creating your cleaning protocol and finalizing your house rules.

Digital vs. Physical Welcome Books: Which Format Works Best?

One of the first decisions you will face is whether to create a digital welcome book, a physical printed version, or both. Each format has distinct advantages, and the best hosts typically offer both options to cover every scenario.

Physical Welcome Books

A printed welcome book placed on the coffee table or kitchen counter gives guests an immediate, tangible resource. There is no app to download, no link to click, and no password to enter. Guests can flip through it the moment they arrive, even if their phone is dead or they are still waiting for WiFi to connect. Physical books also create a polished, professional impression that reinforces the quality of your property.

The downsides are straightforward. Printed books are harder to update, they can get damaged or stained over time, and reprinting costs add up if your information changes frequently. You also need to ensure your cleaning team replaces worn copies regularly.

Digital Welcome Books

Digital welcome books, delivered as a PDF, a dedicated app, or a hosted webpage, offer flexibility and convenience. You can send them to guests before arrival so they can plan ahead. Updates are instant and free. You can include clickable links to restaurant menus, attraction websites, and map directions that a printed book simply cannot provide.

However, digital books require guests to use their devices, which can feel impersonal. Some guests, particularly older travelers, may find them less accessible. Connectivity issues at rural or remote properties can also make digital-only books unreliable.

The Recommended Approach

Offer both. Send a digital version 24 to 48 hours before check-in so guests can review it at their leisure, and place a beautifully designed physical copy in the property. This way, every guest has access to your information regardless of their preferences or circumstances.

Essential Sections Every Welcome Book Needs

A complete welcome book covers seven core sections. Each one addresses a specific category of guest questions and contributes to an overall experience that feels thoughtful and well-organized.

1. Property Information and Welcome Message

Open with a warm, personal welcome message that thanks guests for choosing your property. Include the property address, your name or business name, and a brief description of the space. This section sets the tone for the entire stay and gives guests confidence that a real person is behind their booking.

Include a quick overview of the property layout, noting which rooms are included in their reservation and any shared spaces. If the property has unique features like a rooftop deck, a hot tub, or a game room, highlight them here so guests know what is available from the start.

2. WiFi and Technology

This is the single most referenced section of any welcome book. Place the WiFi network name and password prominently, using a large, clear font that is easy to read. Consider including a QR code that guests can scan to connect automatically.

Beyond WiFi, cover instructions for the smart TV (streaming services available, how to log in, how to switch inputs), any Bluetooth speakers, smart home devices, and entertainment systems. Keep instructions simple and include photos where possible.

3. Appliance and Equipment Instructions

Walk guests through every appliance they might use. This includes the coffee maker, dishwasher, washer and dryer, oven, microwave, thermostat, and any specialized equipment like a pizza oven or outdoor grill. Even common appliances can vary significantly between brands, so do not assume your guests will know how to operate yours.

For each appliance, provide step-by-step instructions with numbered lists. Note any quirks, such as a stove burner that takes a moment to ignite or a dryer that works best on a specific setting. These small details prevent frustration and protect your equipment from misuse.

4. House Rules

Your house rules section should be clear, direct, and organized. Cover noise policies, smoking restrictions, pet policies, parking instructions, maximum occupancy, and any areas that are off-limits. Frame rules positively when possible. Instead of writing "Do not slam the doors," try "Please close doors gently, as sound carries in the neighborhood."

Include your policies on parties, events, and additional guests. Address quiet hours with specific times, and mention any neighborhood-specific rules that guests should be aware of. Being upfront about expectations prevents misunderstandings and protects your property.

5. Local Recommendations

This is where your welcome book transforms from a manual into a genuine hospitality experience. Curate a list of your favorite local restaurants, coffee shops, grocery stores, attractions, and outdoor activities. Organize them by category and include approximate distances or drive times from the property.

Personal recommendations carry far more weight than generic lists. Add a sentence or two about why you love each spot. For example: "The breakfast burrito at Corner Cafe is the best in town. Get there before 9 AM on weekends to avoid the line." These personal touches make guests feel like they have a local friend guiding their trip.

Include a mix of options across price points and interests. Cover family-friendly activities, romantic dinner spots, outdoor adventures, rainy-day options, and quick grab-and-go meals. Seasonal recommendations are especially valuable, so update this section quarterly to keep it relevant.

6. Emergency Contacts and Safety Information

List essential emergency numbers, including 911, the local non-emergency police line, the nearest hospital or urgent care facility, poison control, and your own contact information or that of your property manager. Include the property address again in this section so guests can provide it quickly in an emergency.

Point out the locations of fire extinguishers, first aid kits, smoke detectors, and carbon monoxide detectors. If the property has a security system, explain how it works and what to do if the alarm triggers accidentally. For properties in areas prone to specific natural events like hurricanes, earthquakes, or severe winter storms, include relevant safety procedures.

7. Checkout Procedures

End your welcome book with a clear, simple checkout checklist. Keep it reasonable. Most guests are happy to follow three to five straightforward steps, such as starting the dishwasher, taking out the trash, stripping the beds, turning off lights and the thermostat, and locking the door. Specify your checkout time clearly and explain the key return process.

Avoid making checkout feel like a chore. Thank guests again for their stay, invite them to leave a review, and mention that you would love to host them again in the future. A warm closing leaves a lasting positive impression.

Design Tips for Branding Your Welcome Book

A welcome book is an extension of your property's brand, and its design should reflect the same level of care and attention you put into your interior design. Here are the key principles to follow.

Consistency with Your Property's Aesthetic

Your welcome book should feel like it belongs in your space. If your rental features a modern minimalist design, your welcome book should use clean lines, generous white space, and a simple color palette. If your property has a cozy cabin aesthetic, warmer tones, textured backgrounds, and rustic typography will feel more appropriate. The visual language of the book should mirror the visual language of the property.

Typography and Readability

Choose no more than two fonts: one for headings and one for body text. Both should be highly legible, especially for the WiFi password and emergency contact sections. Use a minimum font size of 12 points for printed books and ensure strong contrast between text and background colors. Guests will be reading your book in various lighting conditions, so clarity is essential.

Photography and Visual Elements

Include high-quality photos of your property throughout the book. Show the exterior so guests can identify the building on arrival, highlight key features like the pool or outdoor dining area, and use photos to supplement appliance instructions. Images break up walls of text and make the book feel more inviting and professional.

Layout and Organization

Use clear section dividers, a table of contents, and consistent formatting throughout. Each section should start on a new page in a printed book or under a distinct heading in a digital version. Number your pages and use tabs or color-coded sections to help guests find information quickly. The goal is to make any detail accessible within seconds.

Template Structure Breakdown

Here is a page-by-page template structure you can adapt for your own property:

  1. Cover Page: Property name, a beautiful hero photo, and your logo or brand mark.
  2. Welcome Message: Personal greeting, property overview, and your contact information.
  3. WiFi and Technology: Network name, password, QR code, smart TV instructions, and streaming details.
  4. Kitchen Guide: Coffee maker, dishwasher, oven, microwave, and any specialty appliances with step-by-step instructions.
  5. Laundry and Utilities: Washer and dryer instructions, thermostat settings, and water heater notes.
  6. House Rules: Noise, smoking, pets, parking, quiet hours, and guest policies.
  7. Local Dining: Breakfast, lunch, dinner, and coffee recommendations with addresses and personal notes.
  8. Activities and Attractions: Outdoor recreation, cultural sites, family activities, and seasonal events.
  9. Grocery and Essentials: Nearest grocery stores, pharmacies, gas stations, and convenience shops.
  10. Emergency Information: Emergency numbers, hospital location, safety equipment locations, and property address.
  11. Checkout Checklist: Step-by-step departure instructions and a closing thank you message.

This structure works for both physical and digital formats. For a digital version, you can expand each section with clickable links, embedded maps, and photo galleries that would be impractical in print.

How Interior Design Impacts the Welcome Book Experience

Your property's interior design and your welcome book are deeply connected. The design choices you make for your space directly influence what needs to go in your welcome book, and they shape how guests perceive the book itself.

A property with hotel-quality design finishes creates an expectation of professionalism that your welcome book must match. If guests walk into a beautifully staged living room with curated art and designer furniture, they will notice if the welcome book is a stack of photocopied pages in a plastic binder. The quality gap undermines the overall experience.

Conversely, a well-designed welcome book can elevate a property. When every touchpoint feels intentional, from the decor to the printed materials, guests perceive the entire experience as premium. This translates directly to higher nightly rates, better reviews, and more repeat bookings.

Design choices also affect the practical content of your book. Unique or high-end appliances require more detailed instructions. Smart home features need clear explanations. Custom furniture arrangements or convertible spaces benefit from photos showing their intended configuration. The more design-forward your property, the more your welcome book needs to guide guests through those choices so they can fully enjoy the space.

If you are investing in professional STR interior design, your designer should also help you plan the welcome book experience. The color palette, typography, photography style, and overall tone should all be coordinated so that every element of your rental feels cohesive and intentional.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should an Airbnb welcome book be?

A good welcome book typically runs 8 to 15 pages. It should be long enough to cover all essential information like WiFi, house rules, appliance instructions, local recommendations, and checkout procedures, but concise enough that guests will actually read it. Use clear headings and bullet points so guests can scan for the details they need without reading cover to cover.

Should I use a digital or printed welcome book for my vacation rental?

The best approach is to offer both. A printed welcome book on the coffee table gives guests immediate access without needing their phone, while a digital version sent before arrival lets guests prepare and serves as a backup they can reference anywhere. Digital books are easier to update, but physical copies feel more personal and are accessible even when guests have connectivity issues.

How often should I update my Airbnb welcome book?

Review your welcome book at least once per quarter. Update it immediately whenever you change WiFi passwords, swap appliances, adjust house rules, or discover new local restaurants worth recommending. Seasonal updates are also valuable for adding relevant activities, local events, and weather-specific tips that keep your recommendations feeling current and helpful.

Minimalist Design for Short Term Rentals

By MyBnBDesign Team • October 02, 2026

The Power of Less in Short Term Rental Design

Minimalist design is more than an aesthetic choice for short term rentals. It is a strategic approach that reduces maintenance costs, simplifies guest communication, and creates spaces that appeal to the widest possible audience. In an industry where operational efficiency directly impacts profitability, minimalism offers tangible financial advantages alongside visual appeal.

Guests increasingly seek calm, uncluttered spaces as a counterpoint to their busy daily lives. A minimalist rental provides a visual and mental reset that travelers find deeply appealing. This translates directly into positive reviews that mention words like peaceful, serene, and exactly what we needed.

Core Principles of Minimalist STR Design

Minimalism in a rental context means every item earns its place through either function or carefully considered beauty. Start by eliminating everything that serves no purpose. Remove decorative tchotchkes, excess throw pillows, and redundant kitchen gadgets. What remains should be high-quality and intentional.

The color palette should be restrained but not sterile. Warm whites, soft concrete grays, natural wood tones, and one or two accent colors create visual interest without clutter. Texture becomes your primary design tool. A linen sofa, a wool area rug, a stone countertop, and brushed metal hardware create richness through material variety rather than color or pattern.

Selecting Furniture with Purpose

Every piece of furniture in a minimalist rental should serve multiple purposes when possible. A bench at the foot of the bed provides seating, luggage storage, and a visual anchor. A dining table with clean lines serves as both a workspace and eating area. Built-in storage eliminates the need for additional storage furniture.

Invest in the highest quality you can afford for the pieces you keep. A beautiful solid wood dining table, a well-constructed sofa in a timeless silhouette, and a premium mattress justify their cost through longevity and guest satisfaction. Minimalism does not mean cheap. It means deliberate.

Beds should be simple but luxurious. A platform bed with an upholstered headboard, high-thread-count white sheets, and a single quality duvet create a hotel-level sleeping experience. Skip the decorative pillows and focus on comfort instead.

Storage and Organization

Behind-the-scenes organization is what makes minimalist spaces function for guests. Provide ample closed storage so personal items disappear. Include drawer organizers, labeled containers, and clear instructions about where things belong. When guests can keep the space tidy effortlessly, it stays looking great between cleanings.

Kitchen organization is particularly important. Store items logically and provide only what guests actually need. A well-curated set of cookware, dishes, and utensils serves better than overflowing drawers of mismatched tools. Consider open shelving for frequently used items and closed cabinets for everything else.

Why Minimalism Wins in the STR Market

Minimalist properties are easier to clean, faster to turn over, and less expensive to maintain. Fewer decorative items mean fewer things to break, replace, or clean. This operational efficiency compounds over time, improving your bottom line with every booking.

Photography is another advantage. Minimalist spaces produce clean, bright listing photos that perform exceptionally well in search results. The uncluttered compositions draw the eye to architectural features and spatial quality rather than distracting accessories.

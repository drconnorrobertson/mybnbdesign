Designing Your STR for Couples: Romance Meets Revenue

By MyBnBDesign Team · September 16, 2026

Couples are the backbone of the short-term rental market. Whether it is an anniversary weekend, a honeymoon, or simply a chance to escape the daily grind, couples are willing to spend more per night for a space that feels special. They are not looking for just a place to sleep -- they want an experience. And the hosts who understand this are commanding premium nightly rates while maintaining near-perfect occupancy.

Designing a couples-focused STR is about creating atmosphere, intimacy, and those small luxurious touches that make guests feel like they have stumbled into something truly elevated. Here is how to do it right.

The Bedroom as the Centerpiece

In a couples rental, the bedroom is not just where guests sleep -- it is the main event. Everything about this room should feel indulgent. Start with the bed itself. A king-size bed is mandatory, and the mattress should be hotel-quality or better. Invest in a premium mattress topper, high-thread-count sheets, and a duvet that feels like a cloud. Guests notice bedding immediately, and for couples, this is the single most reviewed element of the stay.

Frame the bed with a dramatic headboard -- upholstered in velvet, crafted from reclaimed wood, or designed as a statement piece. Flank it with matching nightstands, each with its own reading lamp on a dimmer switch. Ambient lighting is everything in a couples space. Install a dimmer on the overhead light, add a warm-toned LED strip behind the headboard, and place a candle or two on the dresser. Smart bulbs that guests can control from their phone add a modern, luxurious touch.

Consider the view from the bed. If the property has a scenic outlook -- mountains, water, a garden -- orient the bed to face it. Floor-to-ceiling curtains in a rich fabric frame the window and add softness to the room. Blackout capability is essential for sleeping in, but sheer layers allow natural light to filter through during the day.

The Spa-Inspired Bathroom

For couples, the bathroom is not a utilitarian space -- it is an extension of the bedroom experience. A soaking tub is the single highest-ROI amenity you can install in a couples-focused STR. Freestanding tubs photograph beautifully for listings, generate social media content from guests, and appear in reviews more than almost any other feature. If a full soaking tub is not feasible, a large walk-in rain shower with a bench and dual shower heads is an excellent alternative.

Stock the bathroom with premium toiletries -- not travel-size bottles from a warehouse store. Choose a cohesive brand with attractive packaging, and present them on a tray or shelf. Plush white towels, a heated towel rack if budget allows, and matching robes hanging on hooks complete the spa atmosphere. Add a Bluetooth speaker so guests can play music while they soak, and ensure the lighting includes a dimmable option.

Intimate Outdoor Spaces

Couples do not need a sprawling backyard -- they need a private, intimate outdoor space that feels like their own. A small patio with two comfortable chairs, a fire pit or chiminea, and string lights overhead creates an irresistible evening setting. If the property has a balcony, outfit it with a bistro table and chairs, potted plants, and a view that rewards sitting still.

Hot tubs are another high-impact amenity for couples. A two-person hot tub on a private deck, screened from neighbors, is a booking magnet. Surround it with landscaping for privacy, add exterior dimmers or solar pathway lights, and keep it impeccably clean between guests. The maintenance cost is real, but properties with private hot tubs consistently command 20 to 30 percent higher nightly rates.

Curated Amenities That Set the Mood

The details are what separate a good couples rental from an unforgettable one. A wine fridge stocked with a complimentary bottle of local wine signals thoughtfulness. A record player with a curated collection of vinyl adds analog warmth that a Bluetooth speaker cannot replicate. A coffee station with a quality espresso machine, local beans, and beautiful mugs makes mornings feel special.

Consider a welcome basket with local chocolates, artisan cheese, and a handwritten note. Provide a list of recommended restaurants with reservation links, scenic drives, and hidden local gems. Couples are often celebrating something -- an anniversary, an engagement, a birthday -- so offer optional add-ons like a charcuterie board, flowers, or champagne that they can pre-order before arrival.

Design Palette and Materials

The color palette for a couples rental should feel warm, rich, and sophisticated. Think deep greens, warm whites, soft terracotta, and muted gold accents. Avoid anything overly bright, juvenile, or clinical. Natural materials -- wood, stone, linen, leather -- create the organic warmth that makes a space feel inviting rather than staged.

Texture is your best friend here. Layer a chunky knit throw over a linen duvet. Place a sheepskin rug beside the bed. Use woven baskets for storage and ceramic vessels for flowers. Every surface should invite touch. Art should be original or high-quality prints -- not mass-produced decor from a big-box store. Choose pieces that evoke the local landscape or culture, giving couples a sense of place.

The Revenue Case for Romance

Couples-focused STRs enjoy several financial advantages. They experience less wear and tear than family or group properties. They attract guests who leave positive reviews about atmosphere and design -- exactly the kind of reviews that drive future bookings. They command premium pricing, especially around holidays like Valentine's Day, anniversaries, and long weekends. And they tend to attract repeat guests who return for the same experience year after year. Designing for couples is not just good hospitality -- it is smart business.

Scandinavian Design for Short Term Rentals

By MyBnBDesign Team • October 02, 2026

Scandinavian Design: The Art of Hygge in Your STR

Scandinavian design has become one of the most requested styles in the short term rental market, and for good reason. Its emphasis on light, warmth, and functionality creates spaces that feel immediately welcoming regardless of the season or location. The concept of hygge, the Danish word for cozy contentment, translates directly into the kind of guest experience that generates five-star reviews.

This design philosophy prioritizes human comfort and connection above all else. Every element serves a purpose, either functional or emotional, and nothing exists merely for decoration. For rental properties, this approach creates spaces that are beautiful, easy to maintain, and deeply satisfying for guests.

The Scandinavian Color Story

Light is the driving force behind Scandinavian color choices. White walls and ceilings maximize available natural light, which is particularly important during darker months. Warm white is preferable to cool or blue-toned whites, creating a cozy rather than clinical atmosphere.

Layer warm tones onto the white foundation. Pale blush, soft gray, muted sage, and dusty blue work as accent colors. Natural wood in light tones like birch, ash, and pale oak brings warmth and organic texture. Black accents used sparingly provide definition and visual anchoring.

Furniture and Spatial Design

Scandinavian furniture prioritizes form and function equally. Look for pieces with gently rounded edges, tapered legs, and visible wood grain. The furniture should feel lightweight and airy even when solidly constructed. Avoid anything heavy, ornate, or visually bulky.

Seating should be supremely comfortable. A generously proportioned sofa in light gray or cream fabric, paired with a sheepskin throw and knit cushions, creates the ultimate hygge seating area. Accent chairs in natural wood with cushioned seats add additional seating without visual weight.

Dining areas benefit from a round or oval table in light wood paired with mixed seating. Combine two or three matching chairs with a bench for a relaxed Scandinavian feel. Pendant lighting above the table creates an intimate dining atmosphere.

Textiles and the Art of Layering

Textiles transform a Scandinavian space from cool to cozy. Wool throws draped over sofas and chairs, sheepskin rugs beside the bed, linen curtains filtering light, and chunky knit cushions all contribute to the tactile richness that defines Scandinavian interiors.

Bedrooms should feel like a nest. Layer a white duvet with a wool blanket at the foot, add linen pillowcases, and include a sheepskin or faux fur throw. The bed should look inviting enough that guests want to photograph it for their social media.

Candles, Plants, and Natural Elements

Candles are central to Scandinavian design and create instant atmosphere. Place them on dining tables, bathroom counters, and living room surfaces. Use unscented or lightly scented options in simple holders. LED candles provide the same warm glow without fire risk in a rental setting.

Incorporate natural elements throughout the space. A branch in a ceramic vase, a bowl of smooth stones, potted herbs in the kitchen, and trailing plants on shelves bring the outdoors inside. These touches reinforce the connection to nature that defines Scandinavian living.

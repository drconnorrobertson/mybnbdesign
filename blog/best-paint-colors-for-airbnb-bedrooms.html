Best Paint Colors for Airbnb Bedrooms

Published on October 02, 2026 by MyBnbDesign Team

Why Bedroom Color Matters for Airbnb Success

The bedroom is the most important room in your Airbnb. Guests spend more time there than anywhere else, and it is the room they think about when leaving a review. The right paint color can transform a basic bedroom into a retreat that earns five-star ratings and repeat bookings. Choosing the wrong shade, however, can make even a well-furnished room feel cold, cramped, or dated.

Color affects mood, perceived room size, and photo quality. Listing photos are your first impression, and the bedroom is often the hero shot. A well-chosen paint color makes the room look larger, brighter, and more inviting in photographs. This translates directly to higher click-through rates and more bookings.

Top Paint Colors for Airbnb Bedrooms

Soft White (Benjamin Moore Simply White OC-117)

Soft white remains the gold standard for Airbnb bedrooms. It creates a blank canvas that works with any decor style, photographs beautifully in both natural and artificial light, and appeals to the widest range of guests. Unlike stark white, soft white has warm undertones that prevent the room from feeling clinical. Pair it with warm wood tones and textured linens for a hotel-quality look.

Warm Gray (Sherwin-Williams Agreeable Gray SW 7029)

Agreeable Gray is one of the most popular paint colors in the hospitality industry for good reason. This warm greige (gray-beige) works in virtually every lighting condition and complements both modern and traditional furnishings. It adds depth without making the room feel dark, and it hides scuffs and marks better than pure white, reducing your maintenance costs between turnovers.

Sage Green (Benjamin Moore Saybrook Sage HC-114)

Sage green brings nature indoors and creates a calming atmosphere that guests love. This muted green works particularly well in bedrooms because it promotes relaxation and sleep. It pairs beautifully with natural materials like linen, wood, and rattan. Sage green photographs well and adds a touch of personality without being polarizing.

Dusty Blue (Farrow and Ball Parma Gray No.27)

A soft dusty blue evokes calm and serenity, making it perfect for bedrooms in coastal or traditional properties. Blue has been shown to lower heart rate and reduce anxiety, which means guests sleep better and wake up happier. This translates to better reviews. Choose a muted, grayed-down blue rather than a bright or primary blue to keep the look sophisticated.

Warm Blush (Benjamin Moore Pale Oak OC-20)

For a bedroom that feels luxurious without being overly feminine, consider a warm blush or pale pink undertone. Pale Oak walks the line between pink and beige, creating a warm glow that flatters skin tones in photos (think selfies your guests will post). This subtle warmth makes the room feel expensive and curated.

Colors to Avoid in Airbnb Bedrooms

Stay away from bold reds, bright oranges, and neon tones in bedrooms. These colors stimulate energy rather than relaxation and can make the space feel smaller. Dark colors like charcoal or navy can work as accent walls but should not cover all four walls in a standard-sized bedroom. Also avoid trendy colors that will date quickly, as repainting between guests is costly and time-consuming.

Pro Tips for Painting Your Airbnb Bedroom

Always test paint colors in the actual room before committing. Paint a large swatch on two different walls and observe it in morning light, afternoon light, and lamplight. Colors look dramatically different depending on lighting and surrounding furnishings. Buy the best quality paint you can afford. Premium paints like Benjamin Moore Regal or Sherwin-Williams Emerald are more durable, easier to clean, and provide better coverage, saving you money on touch-ups and repaints over time.

Consider using eggshell or satin finish in bedrooms. These finishes are easier to clean than flat paint while still hiding wall imperfections. They also reflect light gently, making the room feel brighter in listing photos. If your ceilings are standard height, paint them the same color as the walls in a flat finish to create a seamless, enveloping effect that makes the room feel larger.

Airbnb Cleaning Checklist: Room by Room Guide for 5-Star Reviews

A comprehensive room-by-room cleaning checklist that keeps your vacation rental spotless and your reviews glowing.

By MyBnBDesign TeamOctober 3, 2026Operations

Why Cleaning Is the Foundation of 5-Star Reviews

Cleanliness is the single most mentioned factor in Airbnb reviews. Guests will forgive a slightly dated kitchen or a smaller-than-expected bedroom, but they will never forgive a dirty bathroom or stained sheets. According to Airbnb's own host data, properties that consistently score 5 stars on cleanliness earn up to 20% more in annual revenue than those that score 4 stars or below. That gap compounds over time through better search placement, more bookings, and the ability to charge higher nightly rates.

Whether you clean the property yourself or hire a professional turnover team, having a detailed, room-by-room checklist is essential. It removes guesswork, creates accountability, and ensures every guest walks into the same spotless experience. If you are still in the process of acquiring your first short-term rental property, building your cleaning systems now will save you from painful early reviews later.

The checklist below covers every room in a typical vacation rental. Print it out, share it with your cleaners, and customize it to match the unique features of your property. Pair it with a thoughtful welcome book and clear house rules, and you will have the operational foundation for a top-performing listing.

Kitchen Cleaning Checklist

The kitchen is where guests spend a surprising amount of time, and it is also where cleaning shortcuts become obvious the fastest. Sticky counters, a greasy stovetop, or a lingering smell in the fridge will tank your reviews. Work through these items in order during every turnover.

  • Empty and wipe down the refrigerator, including shelves, drawers, and door compartments. Discard all guest food.
  • Clean the inside and outside of the microwave. Remove the turntable and wash it separately.
  • Wipe the oven door, stovetop burners, and drip pans. Run a cleaning cycle if the oven shows heavy use.
  • Run the dishwasher on a rinse cycle even if it appears empty. Wipe down the door, handle, and edges.
  • Sanitize all countertops, the backsplash area, and the sink basin. Polish the faucet until it shines.
  • Check every cabinet and drawer for leftover food, crumbs, or misplaced items.
  • Inspect all dishes, glasses, and utensils. Replace anything chipped, stained, or worn.
  • Empty and reline all trash cans with fresh bags. Wipe down the exterior of each can.
  • Clean the coffee maker by running a water-only brew cycle. Restock coffee, filters, and tea bags.
  • Sweep and mop the floor, paying close attention to corners and the area under the stove and fridge.
  • Restock dish soap, sponges, paper towels, and any consumables listed in your amenities guide.

Bathroom Cleaning Checklist

The bathroom is the most scrutinized room in any vacation rental. A single hair on the shower wall or a ring in the toilet can erase every other positive impression a guest has formed. Take your time here and use hospital-grade disinfectant on all high-touch surfaces.

  • Scrub the toilet bowl with a toilet-specific cleaner. Wipe the seat, lid, base, and the floor around the base.
  • Clean the shower or bathtub walls, floor, door, and tracks. Remove any soap scum or mildew buildup.
  • Wipe down the vanity, sink basin, and faucet. Polish all chrome or brushed nickel fixtures.
  • Clean the mirror until it is streak-free. Check from multiple angles to catch missed spots.
  • Launder or replace the bath mat, hand towels, and all hanging towels with freshly washed sets.
  • Restock toilet paper (leave at least two full rolls), tissues, hand soap, shampoo, conditioner, and body wash.
  • Empty the trash can and replace the liner. Wipe down the exterior.
  • Check the exhaust fan for dust buildup and wipe the vent cover.
  • Inspect the grout for discoloration. Spot-treat with a grout cleaner as needed.
  • Mop the floor, including behind the toilet and along the baseboards.

Bedroom Cleaning Checklist

A clean, inviting bedroom sets the tone for a guest's entire stay. The bed itself is the centerpiece. Invest in quality linens and a professional-looking bed-making technique.   • Strip all bedding, including sheets, pillowcases, duvet covers, and mattress protectors. Replace with freshly laundered sets.
  • Inspect the mattress for stains. Spot-clean or replace the mattress protector if needed.
  • Make the bed with crisp, wrinkle-free linens. Add decorative pillows or a folded throw for a hotel-quality look.
  • Dust all surfaces: nightstands, dresser tops, headboard, lampshades, and window sills.
  • Wipe down light switches, door handles, and any remote controls with disinfectant.
  • Empty and wipe out dresser drawers and the closet. Check for items left behind by previous guests.
  • Vacuum the floor thoroughly, including under the bed and in closet corners. Mop hard floors.
  • Check that all lamps, outlets, and the alarm clock (if provided) are functioning.
  • Arrange hangers evenly in the closet. Provide at least 10 per guest.
  • Place a luggage rack or bench in a visible spot so guests do not place suitcases on the bed.

Living Room Cleaning Checklist

The living room is the communal space where guests relax, watch TV, and form their first impression upon walking through the door. Cleanliness here reinforces the feeling that the entire home has been cared for.

  • Vacuum all upholstered furniture, including under and between cushions. Check for crumbs, coins, and forgotten items.
  • Wipe down coffee tables, side tables, shelves, and entertainment centers.
  • Clean the TV screen with a microfiber cloth. Wipe all remotes with disinfectant and check that batteries work.
  • Dust ceiling fans, light fixtures, and any decorative shelving.
  • Fluff and arrange throw pillows and blankets. Launder throws between guests.
  • Vacuum or mop the entire floor, moving furniture slightly to reach hidden areas.
  • Wipe all light switches, door handles, and window locks.
  • Clean windows and sliding doors for streak-free glass. Check that blinds or curtains operate smoothly.
  • Reset the thermostat to a neutral, comfortable temperature (around 72 degrees Fahrenheit).
  • Stage the space with a few design-forward touches like a neatly stacked book arrangement or a small plant.

Outdoor and Entry Area Checklist

First impressions start at the front door. If a guest arrives to find cobwebs on the porch light and dead leaves on the welcome mat, every room they enter after that will be viewed with suspicion. Outdoor spaces also need attention, especially patios, decks, and pool areas.

  • Sweep the front porch, entryway, and any walkways leading to the door.
  • Shake out or replace the welcome mat. Wipe down the front door and door handle.
  • Clean the lockbox or smart lock keypad with disinfectant.
  • Remove cobwebs from porch lights, eaves, and door frames.
  • Wipe down patio furniture, including tables, chairs, and any outdoor cushions.
  • Sweep or blow off the deck or patio. Remove debris from any outdoor grill and empty the grease trap.
  • Check the hot tub water chemistry if applicable. Wipe down the cover and surrounding area.
  • Test outdoor lighting to confirm all bulbs are working.
  • Empty outdoor trash cans and recycling bins.
  • Ensure the garage or storage shed is tidy, with bikes, beach gear, or sports equipment organized neatly.

Laundry and Linen Management

Laundry is the hidden engine of every successful turnover operation. If your linen process is slow or unreliable, your entire cleaning schedule falls apart. Build a system that runs smoothly every single time.

  • Maintain at least three full sets of sheets and towels per bed and bathroom. This gives you a buffer for stains, damage, and laundry delays.
  • Wash all linens on a hot cycle with a quality detergent. Avoid heavy fragrances that may irritate guests with allergies.
  • Inspect every piece of linen for stains, tears, or fading before folding. Retire anything that looks worn.
  • Iron or steam sheets if wrinkles are visible after folding.
  • Store backup linen sets on-site in a locked closet so your cleaning team always has fresh supplies.
  • Track linen inventory monthly. Replace towels every 6 to 12 months and sheets annually, depending on your booking volume.

If you are managing multiple properties, consider partnering with a commercial laundry service. The per-piece cost is often lower than running residential machines, and the quality is more consistent.

Between-Guest Deep Clean

Every turnover includes the standard checklist above. But some tasks do not need to happen at every single turnover. Instead, schedule them on a rotating basis, such as every 4 to 6 turnovers or once a month, whichever comes first.

  • Pull out the stove and refrigerator to clean behind and underneath them.
  • Deep clean the oven interior with a degreasing spray or self-clean cycle.
  • Descale the coffee maker, kettle, and showerheads with white vinegar.
  • Wash all window screens and wipe interior window tracks.
  • Flip or rotate mattresses per the manufacturer's recommendation.
  • Steam clean upholstered furniture and area rugs.
  • Wipe baseboards, door frames, and crown molding throughout the property.
  • Clean inside the washer and dryer. Clear the dryer vent of lint buildup (this is also a fire safety issue).

Seasonal Deep Clean Items

Twice a year, schedule a comprehensive deep clean that goes beyond the monthly rotation. This is the time to address items that accumulate wear slowly and are easy to overlook until they become guest complaints.

  • Pressure wash the exterior, driveway, walkways, and deck or patio.
  • Clean gutters and downspouts.
  • Have the HVAC system serviced and replace air filters.
  • Inspect and clean the water heater.
  • Shampoo all carpets and professionally clean area rugs.
  • Touch up interior paint in high-traffic areas: hallways, door frames, and stairwells.
  • Reseal grout in bathrooms and the kitchen.
  • Service the fireplace or wood stove, including chimney inspection.
  • Inspect smoke detectors, carbon monoxide detectors, and fire extinguishers. Replace batteries.
  • Audit your entire property inventory: dishes, cookware, linens, towels, and decor. Replace anything that no longer meets your standards.

Creating SOPs for Your Cleaning Team

A checklist is only as effective as the system around it. If you hand a printed list to a new cleaner without context, training, or accountability, results will be inconsistent. Here is how to turn your checklist into a repeatable Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) that delivers consistent quality.

Step 1: Document Everything Visually

Walk through your property and take photos of every room in its "guest-ready" state. Capture the bed made, towels folded, kitchen counters clear, and bathroom amenities arranged. These photos become your cleaners' reference point for how each room should look when they leave.

Step 2: Build a Digital Checklist

Use a tool like TurnoverBnB, Breezeway, or even a shared Google Doc to create a digital version of the checklist. Digital checklists allow cleaners to check off items in real time, upload photos of completed rooms, and flag maintenance issues. This gives you visibility without requiring you to inspect in person after every turnover.

Step 3: Train in Person First

Walk through the first two or three turnovers alongside your cleaner. Show them exactly how you want the bed made, where supplies are stored, and what "clean" looks like in your property. This upfront investment saves dozens of correction conversations later.

Step 4: Set Quality Standards and Consequences

Be clear about expectations. If a guest complaint is traced to a missed cleaning item, discuss it with the cleaner directly and document the conversation. If patterns emerge, it may be time to find a new team member. Your reviews depend on it.

Step 5: Review and Update Quarterly

Guest expectations evolve. Review your checklist every quarter and incorporate feedback from guest reviews, cleaner suggestions, and any new amenities you have added to the property. If you recently redesigned a bathroom or added new furniture, update the SOP photos to reflect the changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a turnover clean take for a typical Airbnb?

A standard turnover clean for a two-bedroom vacation rental typically takes 1.5 to 2.5 hours for one cleaner. Larger properties or homes with extra amenities like hot tubs or pools may require 3 to 4 hours. The key is building a consistent checklist so your cleaning team can work efficiently without missing critical details.

How much should I pay my Airbnb cleaner per turnover?

Rates vary by market, but most Airbnb hosts pay between $75 and $200 per turnover for a standard two-bedroom property. Factors that influence pricing include property size, local labor costs, how many turnovers the cleaner handles for you per month, and whether laundry is included. Many hosts pass the cleaning fee to guests, so investing in quality cleaning pays for itself through better reviews.

What cleaning supplies should I keep stocked at my Airbnb?

Keep a locked closet or storage area stocked with all-purpose cleaner, glass cleaner, disinfectant spray, toilet bowl cleaner, stainless steel polish, microfiber cloths, scrub sponges, a vacuum with attachments, a mop and bucket, trash bags, rubber gloves, and a caddy to carry everything between rooms. Buying in bulk from a warehouse store keeps costs down and ensures your cleaners never run out mid-turnover.

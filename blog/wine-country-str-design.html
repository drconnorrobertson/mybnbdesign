Wine Country STR Design: Vineyard Views and Tasteful Interiors

By MyBnBDesign Team · October 1, 2026

Wine country destinations attract a sophisticated traveler who values culinary experiences, natural beauty, and refined living. These guests are not looking for a basic vacation rental -- they want an immersive experience that mirrors the elegance of the vineyards they are visiting. Designing a short-term rental for wine country requires a deep understanding of what draws people to these regions and how your property can extend that experience from the tasting room to the home. Here is how to create a wine country STR that feels like it belongs to the landscape.

The Wine Fridge and Tasting Setup

A dual-zone wine refrigerator is not optional in wine country -- it is expected. Guests will return from tasting rooms with bottles they have purchased throughout the day, and they need proper temperature-controlled storage for both reds and whites. A unit that holds at least 30 bottles gives groups enough space for a multi-day trip's worth of acquisitions. Position the wine fridge in the main entertaining area rather than hidden in the kitchen so it becomes part of the social experience.

Create a dedicated tasting area near the wine fridge with a counter or bar surface at standing height, proper wine glasses -- at least Burgundy and Bordeaux stems, plus universal tasting glasses -- a decanter, a quality corkscrew, and a pour spout collection. A slate or marble board for cheese and charcuterie pairings completes the setup. Many guests will host their own private tasting with bottles they have collected during the day, and your property should make that experience feel intentional rather than improvised. Include a wine journal or tasting notes cards where guests can record their favorites -- a small touch that enhances the experience and makes your property part of their wine country memories.

A Chef-Quality Kitchen

Wine country travelers are food enthusiasts, and many will plan at least one or two elaborate meals during their stay. Your kitchen needs to support serious cooking with a professional-grade range -- a 36-inch six-burner gas range is ideal -- a convection oven, and ample counter space for multi-course meal preparation. Quality cookware is essential -- a full set of stainless steel pots and pans, a Dutch oven, and specialty items like a pasta maker, mandoline, and microplane grater show that you understand how your guests cook.

Stock the kitchen with essentials that elevate cooking: high-quality olive oil, aged balsamic vinegar, flaky sea salt, whole peppercorns in a grinder, and a selection of dried herbs. A knife set that includes a proper chef's knife, bread knife, and paring knife -- sharp and well-maintained -- is critical. Dull knives in a rental kitchen are one of the most common complaints from food-savvy guests. Include a wood or end-grain cutting board large enough for real prep work, not the small decorative boards that dominate most rental kitchens. A stand mixer on the counter signals that this kitchen is equipped for baking as well as savory cooking.

Outdoor Dining and Entertaining

In wine country, the outdoor dining experience is paramount. A large table that seats the full guest count under a pergola, covered patio, or mature tree provides the setting for long, leisurely meals that define wine country travel. The table should be substantial -- a farm table in reclaimed wood or a stone-top table that can handle wine bottles, platters, and candles without feeling cluttered. Surround it with comfortable chairs that guests can linger in for hours because wine country dinners are not rushed affairs.

String lights overhead create warmth and ambiance as daylight fades. Candlestick holders and hurricane lanterns on the table provide intimate lighting at eye level. A fire pit or fireplace adjacent to the dining area extends the outdoor evening -- after the plates are cleared, the group moves to the fire with their last glasses of wine. If your property has any view at all -- hills, vineyards, open countryside, or a garden -- orient the outdoor dining area to maximize it. The combination of good food, good wine, and a beautiful setting under the stars is the experience guests came to wine country to find.

Vineyard Views and Window Strategy

If your property has vineyard views, every design decision should frame and celebrate them. Position the primary seating areas -- both living room and master bedroom -- to face the best vantage point. Use large windows or glass doors without heavy treatments that block the view. Sheer linen curtains that filter light while maintaining visibility work better than drapes that create a wall between the interior and the landscape.

Even without direct vineyard views, you can create a connection to wine country through sight lines to gardens, mature trees, or rolling hills. Landscape your property with Mediterranean-inspired plantings -- lavender, rosemary, olive trees, and grape vines on a trellis -- that echo the agricultural landscape of the region. A window seat or reading nook positioned at the best view point in the house creates a quiet retreat that guests will photograph and treasure. The interior should feel like an extension of the surrounding landscape, with natural materials and earth tones that blur the boundary between inside and outside.

Regional Design Language

The most compelling wine country properties speak the design language of their specific region. A Napa Valley rental should feel different from a Willamette Valley property, which should feel different from a Texas Hill Country estate. Study the architectural vernacular of your region -- the materials, colors, proportions, and details that define local building traditions -- and incorporate those elements authentically.

In Sonoma or Napa, think Mediterranean and California ranch influences -- terracotta tones, exposed timber, Spanish tile accents, and warm stucco textures. In Oregon wine country, lean toward Pacific Northwest modernism -- clean lines, natural wood, large windows, and restrained color palettes drawn from the gray-green landscape. In Virginia or the Finger Lakes, traditional farmhouse elements -- board-and-batten siding, wide plank floors, and muted Colonial colors -- connect to the local heritage. The goal is a property that could only exist in its specific location, not a generic wine-themed rental that could be dropped anywhere. Source art from local artists, pottery from regional makers, and textiles that reflect the local palette. These details create an experience that feels rooted in place, which is exactly what wine country travelers are seeking.

Curating the Complete Experience

Beyond the physical design, wine country guests expect a curated guide to the region. Compile a comprehensive guide to local wineries organized by style -- bold reds, crisp whites, sparkling, natural wines -- along with tasting room hours, reservation requirements, and your personal recommendations. Include restaurant suggestions categorized by price point and cuisine, farm stands and markets for sourcing local ingredients, and seasonal events like harvest festivals or barrel tastings. Partner with a local sommelier or wine educator who can offer private tastings at the property for an additional fee. This concierge layer transforms your STR from a beautiful place to stay into a complete wine country experience, and it is the detail that turns first-time guests into annual visitors.

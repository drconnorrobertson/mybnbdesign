Industrial Design for Short Term Rentals

By MyBnBDesign Team • October 02, 2026

Industrial Design: Raw Character for Urban Rentals

Industrial design transforms urban short term rentals into spaces with authentic character and visual impact. Born from the conversion of warehouses and factories into living spaces, this aesthetic celebrates exposed structural elements, raw materials, and the beauty of honest construction. For rental properties in urban markets, industrial design creates a sense of place that generic interiors cannot match.

Travelers booking stays in cities are often looking for an experience that feels distinctly urban and authentic. Industrial design delivers that experience by embracing the architectural bones of a building rather than concealing them. The result is a space that feels substantial, grounded, and cool without trying too hard.

Core Materials and Textures

Industrial design relies on a specific material vocabulary. Exposed brick, concrete floors, metal beams, and reclaimed wood form the structural palette. If your property lacks these raw elements, they can be introduced through strategic design choices. A concrete-effect wall treatment, metal shelving units, and reclaimed wood accent walls bring industrial character to any space.

The color palette stays grounded in neutrals: charcoal, slate gray, warm concrete tones, matte black, and oxidized metal finishes. Warm wood tones provide essential contrast and prevent the space from feeling cold or unwelcoming. Every industrial interior needs this balance between raw and warm elements.

Furniture Selection for Industrial Spaces

Furniture should combine metal frameworks with solid wood surfaces. A dining table with a steel base and thick wooden top is the quintessential industrial piece. Bar stools with metal frames, a leather sofa with visible stitching, and shelving units made from pipe fittings and wooden planks reinforce the vocabulary.

Seating should be comfortable despite the rugged aesthetic. A well-worn leather chesterfield, an oversized armchair in dark fabric, or a deep sectional sofa soften the harder elements of the space. Include plenty of cushions and throws in warm, textured fabrics to ensure guests feel comfortable during longer stays.

Bedroom furniture benefits from iron bed frames, wooden nightstands with metal hardware, and industrial-style clothing racks as open closets. The bed itself should be generously layered with soft bedding to create a contrast between the rugged room and the comfortable sleeping experience.

Lighting as a Design Feature

Industrial lighting is functional art. Edison bulb pendants, cage lights, articulating wall sconces, and oversized metal floor lamps serve as both illumination and decoration. Expose the wiring where possible for an authentic workshop aesthetic.

Track lighting and adjustable spotlights work well in industrial spaces, allowing guests to direct light where they need it. Include dimming capability so the space can shift from bright and functional during the day to warm and atmospheric in the evening.

Softening the Industrial Edge

The most successful industrial rentals balance raw materials with inviting comfort. Add area rugs in natural fibers to soften concrete or hardwood floors. Include indoor plants in galvanized planters or concrete pots. Hang large-scale art or photography that brings color and personality to the space.

Bathrooms in industrial rentals benefit from vessel sinks on wooden vanities, exposed copper plumbing, and concrete-look tiles. Include premium towels and bath products to ensure the spa experience matches the design ambition.

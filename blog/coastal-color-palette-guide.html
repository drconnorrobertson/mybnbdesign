Coastal Color Palette Guide

Published on October 02, 2026 by MyBnbDesign Team

Bringing the Coast Into Your Rental

A coastal color palette captures the essence of life by the water. It evokes sandy beaches, rolling waves, driftwood, and endless sky. For short-term rentals near the coast, this palette creates an immersive experience that connects guests to their surroundings. Even for inland properties, a coastal palette can create an escape that transports guests to the shore. The key is subtlety. The best coastal palettes draw from the natural colors of the coast rather than cartoonish nautical themes.

Forget about anchors, ship wheels, and bright red lifeguard motifs. Modern coastal design is sophisticated, relaxed, and rooted in nature. It uses a restrained palette of soft blues, warm whites, sandy neutrals, and weathered wood tones to create spaces that feel effortlessly elegant.

The Core Coastal Palette

Sandy White

The foundation of any coastal palette is a warm, sandy white. This is not a stark, cool white. It is a white with warm undertones that evokes sun-bleached wood and white sand. Benjamin Moore Simply White OC-117 or White Sand OC-10 are excellent choices. Use this color on most walls and ceilings to create a bright, airy base that reflects light and makes rooms feel larger.

Ocean Blue

Choose a soft, muted blue rather than a bright or primary blue. Think of the color of the ocean on a calm day, not a swimming pool. Benjamin Moore Quiet Moments 1563 or Sherwin-Williams Sleepy Blue SW 6225 capture this perfectly. Use blue sparingly as an accent wall, in textiles, or in tile work. Too much blue can feel cold, so balance it generously with warm tones.

Driftwood Gray

Weathered gray tones add depth and sophistication to a coastal palette. Think of the color of aged driftwood or rain-washed boardwalks. Use this tone in furniture, flooring, or accent pieces. Reclaimed wood, gray-washed oak, and distressed finishes all contribute to the authentic coastal aesthetic. This neutral bridges the gap between the warm whites and cool blues in the palette.

Sea Glass Green

A subtle sea glass green adds freshness to the coastal palette without competing with blue. Use it in bathroom tile, kitchen accessories, or glass vases. This color connects the palette to nature and provides visual variety. It works as a secondary accent alongside your primary blue tones.

Styling Your Coastal Space

Natural textures are essential to a successful coastal palette. Layer in jute rugs, linen curtains, woven baskets, and rattan furniture. Use natural fiber throw pillows and cotton bedding. Display collected shells, sea glass, or coral sparingly as decorative accents. The goal is to evoke the coast through materials and color, not through themed decor.

Keep the space uncluttered. Coastal design thrives on openness and light. Use sheer curtains or no curtains to maximize natural light. Choose furniture with clean lines and light profiles. Avoid heavy, dark pieces that weigh down the airy atmosphere. Every element should feel like something that could exist naturally by the shore.

For listing photos, shoot during golden hour when warm light pours through windows. This lighting brings out the warm undertones in your sandy whites and makes blue accents glow. Stage with fresh white flowers, a bowl of lemons, or a stack of sunhats to complete the coastal story.

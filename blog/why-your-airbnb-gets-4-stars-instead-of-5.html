Why Your Airbnb Gets 4 Stars Instead of 5

By MyBnBDesign Team • October 02, 2026

The Expensive Difference Between 4 and 5

A 4.7 rating and a 4.9 rating look similar on paper, but the revenue difference is dramatic. Higher-rated properties appear more prominently in search results, convert browsers at higher rates, and command premium nightly prices. The difference between four stars and five stars on a single stay might not seem significant, but over a year it represents thousands of dollars in booking volume and rate premium.

What separates four-star properties from five-star properties is almost never the big things. It is an accumulation of small design and experience details that together create the impression of effortless quality versus acceptable adequacy.

The Details That Guests Notice

Five-star guests notice the weight of your towels, the quality of your pillows, the softness of your sheets, and the brightness of your bathroom lighting. They notice whether the bedside lamp is easy to reach, whether the shower has adequate water pressure, whether the kitchen drawers slide smoothly, and whether the TV remote works without instructions.

Four-star properties get these details mostly right but miss enough of them that guests cannot give a perfect score. The guest had a good stay, but something was not quite right. The bed was comfortable but the pillows were flat. The bathroom was clean but the lighting was harsh. The kitchen was equipped but the knives were dull.

The Photography and Expectation Gap

Four-star reviews often come from properties where the reality does not quite match the listing photos. This does not mean the photos were dishonest. It means the photos showed the property at its best angle, in its best light, and the actual experience, while good, did not sustain that level of quality throughout the stay.

Five-star properties look as good in person as they do in photos because the design quality is consistent across every detail, not just the photogenic ones. The inside of closets, the underside of beds, the back of cabinets, and the guest bathroom all receive the same attention as the hero shots.

Elevating Every Touchpoint

Map every physical touchpoint in a guest stay, from arrival to departure. The front door, the entryway, the light switches, the thermostat, the kitchen drawers, the bathroom fixtures, the bed, the pillows, the towels, the checkout process. Each touchpoint is an opportunity to confirm quality or create disappointment.

Rate each touchpoint honestly on a scale from adequate to excellent. The touchpoints rated 'adequate' are where your four-star reviews are coming from. Upgrading those specific points to 'excellent' is the targeted design investment that lifts your rating.

The Five-Star Mindset

Five-star design is not about luxury or expense. It is about consistency and care. A modestly furnished property where every detail has been thoughtfully considered earns five stars more reliably than an expensively furnished property where quality varies by room.

Adopt the mindset that every element a guest sees, touches, or uses has been intentionally selected for their comfort and satisfaction. When that intention is visible throughout the property, guests feel it, and they express it in five-star reviews.

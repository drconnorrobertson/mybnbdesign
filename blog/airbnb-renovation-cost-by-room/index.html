Airbnb Renovation Cost by Room: Complete 2026 Breakdown

A detailed look at what every room renovation costs in a short-term rental, plus which upgrades deliver the strongest return on investment.

By MyBnBDesign TeamOctober 3, 2026Renovation & Budget

Why Renovation Costs Matter for STR Hosts

Renovating an Airbnb or vacation rental is one of the most effective ways to boost nightly rates, improve guest satisfaction, and stand out in a competitive market. But without a clear understanding of costs by room, it is easy to overspend in the wrong areas or underinvest where it counts most. Whether you are preparing a new property for its first listing or refreshing a rental that has been operating for several years, knowing exactly where your renovation dollars go is essential for making smart, profit-driven decisions.

In this complete 2026 breakdown, we walk through renovation budgets for every major room in a short-term rental property. We cover material and labor costs, highlight common overruns to watch for, compare DIY versus professional options, and reveal which room upgrades deliver the highest return on investment. If you are planning a full property design and renovation, this guide will help you prioritize your budget for maximum impact.

Kitchen Renovation Costs

The kitchen is one of the most photographed spaces in any Airbnb listing. Guests expect clean, functional kitchens with modern finishes, and listing photos featuring updated kitchens consistently drive higher click-through rates. In 2026, a full kitchen renovation for a vacation rental typically costs between $8,000 and $25,000, depending on scope and finishes.

Cabinets: $3,000 to $10,000

Replacing cabinets is often the largest single kitchen expense. Stock cabinets from home improvement stores run $3,000 to $5,000 for a standard STR kitchen. Semi-custom options range from $5,000 to $8,000, while fully custom cabinetry can exceed $10,000. For budget-conscious hosts, refacing existing cabinets with new doors and hardware costs $1,500 to $3,500 and can dramatically update the look without a full replacement.

Countertops: $1,500 to $5,000

Quartz countertops remain the top choice for vacation rentals in 2026 because of their durability, stain resistance, and low maintenance. Expect to pay $2,500 to $5,000 for quartz in a typical STR kitchen. Butcher block counters offer a warm, photogenic look for $1,500 to $2,500. Laminate is the most affordable option at $800 to $1,500 but may not hold up well under heavy guest use.

Appliances: $2,000 to $6,000

A complete appliance package including refrigerator, range, dishwasher, and microwave runs $2,000 to $4,000 for reliable mid-range brands. Stainless steel finishes remain the standard for listing photos. Upgrading to premium appliances can push costs to $6,000 or more, though the incremental guest satisfaction beyond mid-range options is often minimal. Focus your appliance budget on a quality dishwasher and a full-size refrigerator, as these are the two appliances guests use and comment on most frequently.

Kitchen Flooring: $1,000 to $3,000

Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) is the gold standard for STR kitchen floors. It is waterproof, scratch-resistant, and available in wood-look styles that photograph beautifully. LVP installation runs $1,000 to $2,000 for most vacation rental kitchens. Tile flooring costs $1,500 to $3,000 including labor but adds complexity and longer installation time. Bathroom Renovation Costs

Bathrooms have the single greatest influence on guest reviews. A dated bathroom with stained grout, a weak showerhead, or a chipped vanity will generate complaints regardless of how stunning the rest of the property looks. Bathroom renovations for STR properties typically range from $5,000 to $18,000 per bathroom.

Tile and Shower: $2,000 to $8,000

Retiling a shower and bathroom floor is often the most transformative upgrade you can make. Subway tile with contrasting grout remains a popular, timeless choice at $2,000 to $4,000 installed. Large-format porcelain tiles cost $3,000 to $6,000 but create a luxurious, spa-like appearance that justifies higher nightly rates. If the existing shower is in poor condition, a full shower replacement including pan, walls, fixtures, and glass door runs $5,000 to $8,000. A rainfall showerhead is a small addition at $100 to $300 that guests consistently mention in five-star reviews.

Vanity and Sink: $800 to $3,000

A floating vanity with a vessel or undermount sink creates a modern, high-end feel. Budget vanities start at $300 to $600, while mid-range options with soft-close drawers and stone tops run $800 to $1,800. Adding a framed mirror and updated lighting above the vanity can transform the space for an additional $200 to $500. Double vanities are worth the investment in primary bathrooms, as they allow couples to get ready simultaneously and are frequently called out in positive reviews.

Fixtures: $300 to $1,200

Replacing faucets, towel bars, toilet paper holders, and robe hooks with a matching set in brushed gold, matte black, or brushed nickel costs $300 to $800. Swapping out a builder-grade toilet for a comfort-height, elongated model adds $200 to $400 installed. These are small investments that collectively elevate the entire bathroom experience.

Bedroom Renovation Costs

Bedroom renovations for vacation rentals tend to be more cosmetic than structural, making them one of the most budget-friendly rooms to update. A full bedroom refresh typically costs $2,000 to $7,000 per room.

Flooring: $1,000 to $3,000

LVP or engineered hardwood are the best flooring choices for STR bedrooms. Both are durable, easy to clean between guest stays, and available in warm tones that create a welcoming atmosphere. LVP runs $1,000 to $1,800 per bedroom, while engineered hardwood costs $1,500 to $3,000. Carpet is generally not recommended for vacation rentals because of stain risk, allergen concerns, and higher cleaning costs between turnovers. For tips on creating a luxury feel on a budget, check out our detailed design guide.

Paint: $300 to $800

A fresh coat of paint is one of the highest-impact, lowest-cost upgrades available. Professional painting for a standard bedroom runs $300 to $600, while DIY paint and supplies cost $100 to $200. Stick with neutral, warm tones like soft white, light gray, or warm beige. These colors photograph well, appeal to the widest range of guests, and make rooms feel larger.

Closet and Storage: $200 to $1,500

Guests need a place to unpack and store their belongings. A basic closet organization system with shelving and a rod costs $200 to $500. For a more premium feel, a custom closet system runs $800 to $1,500. Adding luggage racks, extra hangers, and a small dresser ensures guests feel settled during their stay.

Lighting: $200 to $800

Replacing dated ceiling fixtures with modern pendant lights or flush mounts costs $100 to $400 per fixture installed. Adding bedside sconces or table lamps with USB outlets is a small touch that earns outsized positive feedback. Budget $200 to $800 per bedroom for a complete lighting upgrade including bedside, overhead, and accent options.

Living Room Renovation Costs

The living room serves as the social hub of any vacation rental. It is where guests gather, relax, and spend much of their time. Living room renovations for STR properties range from $3,000 to $10,000.

Flooring follows the same LVP or engineered hardwood recommendations as bedrooms, at similar price points. A fresh paint job runs $400 to $800 for a larger living space. The biggest variable is built-in features. A simple accent wall with shiplap or board-and-batten costs $500 to $1,500 and creates an eye-catching backdrop for listing photos. A mounted TV with concealed wiring runs $300 to $600 installed. Updated light fixtures, including a statement chandelier or modern ceiling fan, add $200 to $600.

Furnishing the living room is a separate cost from renovation, but it is worth noting that professional STR design services can help you select furniture and decor that maximizes both guest comfort and photographic appeal while staying within budget.

Outdoor Space Renovation Costs

Outdoor living areas are a major differentiator for vacation rentals, especially in warm-weather and resort markets. Even modest outdoor upgrades can significantly boost bookings and justify rate increases.

A basic patio refresh with power washing, new outdoor furniture, and string lights costs $1,000 to $3,000. Building or resurfacing a deck runs $3,000 to $10,000 depending on size and materials. Adding a fire pit costs $500 to $2,000, while a hot tub installation ranges from $4,000 to $10,000 including electrical work and a concrete pad. Landscaping improvements such as planting low-maintenance native plants, adding pathway lighting, and installing a privacy fence cost $1,500 to $5,000. Properties with well-designed outdoor spaces consistently command 15 to 30 percent higher nightly rates compared to similar listings without them.

Common Cost Overruns to Watch For

Renovation budgets have a tendency to grow, especially in older properties where hidden issues surface once demolition begins. The most common cost overruns in STR renovations include the following.

  • Hidden water damage: Discovering rot or mold behind walls or under flooring can add $1,000 to $5,000 in unexpected repairs.
  • Electrical upgrades: Older properties may need panel upgrades or rewiring to support modern appliances and HVAC systems, adding $1,500 to $4,000.
  • Plumbing surprises: Corroded pipes, improper venting, or outdated supply lines can surface during bathroom or kitchen work, costing $800 to $3,000 to resolve.
  • Permit and inspection fees: Depending on your municipality, permits for structural, electrical, or plumbing work can add $500 to $2,000 to the project total.
  • Scope creep: Upgrading finishes mid-project or adding rooms to the renovation plan is the most controllable overrun. Set your scope before work begins and stick to it.

A good rule of thumb is to add a 15 to 20 percent contingency buffer to your total renovation budget. This protects you from unexpected costs without requiring you to cut corners on planned improvements.

DIY vs. Professional Renovation

Many STR hosts wonder which tasks they can handle themselves and which require professional help. The answer depends on the complexity of the work and the potential consequences of mistakes.

Good candidates for DIY: painting, installing hardware and fixtures, assembling furniture, hanging shelves and art, replacing light switches and outlet covers, applying peel-and-stick backsplash, and basic landscaping. These tasks can save you 40 to 60 percent on labor costs and are forgiving of minor imperfections.

Always hire a professional for: plumbing modifications, electrical work beyond simple swaps, tile installation, countertop fabrication and installation, structural changes, HVAC work, and anything requiring permits. Poor workmanship on these systems can lead to code violations, insurance complications, guest safety issues, and repairs that cost far more than the original professional installation would have.

For hosts who want professional-quality results across the entire project, working with a dedicated STR design firm ensures every room is designed with guest experience and listing performance in mind.

ROI by Room Type

Not all renovation dollars are created equal. Understanding which rooms deliver the strongest return helps you prioritize when working with a limited budget.

  1. Bathroom (200 to 300% ROI): Bathroom upgrades consistently deliver the highest returns. A $5,000 bathroom renovation can increase nightly rates by $15 to $30 per night, paying for itself within 8 to 14 months of steady bookings.
  2. Kitchen (150 to 250% ROI): Kitchen updates drive better listing photos and higher booking conversion rates. Even a mid-range kitchen refresh of $8,000 to $12,000 typically pays for itself within 12 to 18 months.
  3. Outdoor spaces (150 to 200% ROI): Patios, decks, and hot tubs are powerful amenities that differentiate your listing and justify premium pricing, especially in markets where outdoor living is a key draw.
  4. Bedroom (100 to 150% ROI): Bedroom updates improve reviews and repeat bookings but have a more modest impact on nightly rates compared to kitchens and bathrooms.
  5. Living room (80 to 120% ROI): Living room renovations enhance the overall guest experience but are less likely to directly increase rates on their own. They work best as part of a whole-property renovation strategy.

Budgeting Tips for Your STR Renovation

Planning a renovation budget requires balancing ambition with financial discipline. Here are practical strategies for keeping your project on track.

  • Start with the highest-ROI rooms. If your budget is limited, renovate the bathroom first, then the kitchen. These two rooms have the greatest impact on guest satisfaction and nightly rates.
  • Get three quotes for every major trade. Pricing varies significantly between contractors, especially for tile, plumbing, and electrical work. Three competitive bids help you identify fair market rates.
  • Buy materials during sales cycles. Home improvement retailers run major sales around holiday weekends in May, July, September, and November. Timing your material purchases around these events can save 15 to 25 percent.
  • Consider phased renovations. You do not need to renovate every room at once. Tackle the highest-impact rooms first, start generating revenue, and reinvest profits into subsequent phases.
  • Account for downtime. Every day your property is under renovation is a day without booking revenue. Factor lost income into your total project cost when calculating ROI timelines.

When Does Renovation Pay for Itself?

The payback period for an STR renovation depends on your market, nightly rate increase, and occupancy rate. As a general framework, a $20,000 renovation that increases your nightly rate by $25 and maintains 70 percent occupancy generates approximately $6,400 in additional annual revenue. At that pace, the renovation pays for itself in roughly three years, with every year after that representing pure additional profit.

Properties in high-demand markets with strong seasonal occupancy often see faster payback periods of 12 to 24 months. The key is choosing renovations that directly improve listing performance, guest reviews, and booking conversion rates. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average total cost to renovate an Airbnb property in 2026?

The average total renovation cost for a short-term rental property in 2026 ranges from $15,000 to $75,000, depending on property size, scope of work, and local labor rates. A cosmetic refresh typically runs $15,000 to $30,000, while a full gut renovation of a 2-bedroom unit can reach $50,000 to $75,000 or more. Most hosts find the best value in targeted renovations that focus on kitchens, bathrooms, and outdoor spaces rather than whole-property gut jobs.

Which room renovation provides the highest ROI for Airbnb hosts?

Bathroom renovations consistently deliver the highest ROI for Airbnb hosts, often returning 200 to 300 percent of the investment through higher nightly rates and improved guest reviews. Kitchen updates rank second, especially when they include modern appliances and quality countertops that photograph well in listing images. Together, these two rooms have the greatest influence on booking conversion and guest satisfaction scores.

Should I hire a professional or DIY my Airbnb renovation?

For cosmetic updates like painting, hardware swaps, and light fixture changes, DIY can save 40 to 60 percent on labor costs. However, plumbing, electrical, tiling, and structural work should always be handled by licensed professionals. Poor DIY work on these systems can lead to code violations, insurance issues, and costly repairs down the road. Many hosts find the best approach is to DIY the simple tasks and hire professionals for skilled trades.

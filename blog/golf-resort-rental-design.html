Golf Resort Rental Design: Clubhouse Comfort at Home

By MyBnBDesign Team · September 30, 2026

Golf destination rentals attract a premium demographic -- guests who value quality, appreciate fine details, and expect an elevated experience from their accommodations. These travelers are comparing your property not just to other Airbnbs but to resort hotels and private club guest houses. To compete and win at this level, your design needs to deliver clubhouse-caliber comfort with the privacy and space advantages of a private home. Here is how to design a golf resort rental that keeps scorecards low and bookings high.

Club Storage Done Right

Golf clubs represent thousands of dollars in equipment, and serious golfers will not leave them in the trunk of a car or propped against a garage wall. A dedicated club storage area is essential -- think of it as the golf equivalent of a ski lodge mudroom. Install a purpose-built golf bag rack that holds at least four bags upright and secure, with individual compartments or straps that prevent bags from falling against each other. The storage area should be climate controlled or at least sheltered from temperature extremes, as heat and humidity can damage grips, shafts, and leather bags.

Adjacent to the bag storage, include a shoe cleaning station with a brush, damp towel, and a shoe tree or rack for drying golf shoes after a round. A small workbench area with a club-cleaning brush, groove sharpener, and towels lets guests maintain their equipment between rounds. Consider adding a secure lockable cabinet or closet for the storage area -- golf groups traveling with multiple sets of high-end clubs will appreciate the security, and it gives you an additional selling point in your listing.

The Bar and Beverage Area

The nineteenth hole is a sacred tradition in golf, and your property should deliver this experience without requiring a trip to the clubhouse. A dedicated bar area -- whether a built-in wet bar, a converted butler's pantry, or a standalone bar cart setup -- should be a focal point of the main living area. Stock it with quality glassware including rocks glasses, highball glasses, and wine glasses. A wine refrigerator, a small ice maker, and a cocktail tool set elevate the experience from casual to clubhouse quality.

The bar design should evoke the atmosphere of a private club -- dark wood, leather bar stools, brass accents, and warm lighting. A backlit shelf displaying a curated selection of local spirits or a framed guide to classic cocktails adds visual interest and conversation starters. For outdoor entertaining, extend the beverage experience to a patio bar or a drink station near the pool or fire pit. An outdoor mini fridge stocked with cold water and sports drinks provides a functional touch that golfers returning from a hot round will notice immediately.

Course Guides and Local Intelligence

Golf travelers are planners. They research courses, compare layouts, and strategize their rounds well before arriving. Support this by creating a comprehensive golf guide for your property. Frame aerial or illustrated course maps of the three to five closest courses and hang them in a hallway or game room. Include yardage books, scorecards from local courses, and a printed guide with contact numbers, green fee ranges, tee time booking links, and your personal course recommendations.

A coffee table book featuring the region's golf history or photography from iconic local courses serves as both decor and planning material. If your property is near a destination course -- a top-100 ranked layout or a course with historical significance -- lean into that connection. Display memorabilia, tournament programs, or photographs from notable events held at that course. These details signal to golfers that the property owner understands their world, and that recognition builds the kind of loyalty that generates repeat bookings.

Recovery and Relaxation Amenities

Golf is more physically demanding than many people realize, and guests who play thirty-six holes in a day arrive back at the property sore and tired. A high-quality massage chair positioned in a quiet corner of the living room or a dedicated relaxation nook provides immediate relief. Look for a full-body massage chair with heat therapy -- not a basic vibrating model, but a genuine massage mechanism that works on back, legs, and feet. This single piece of furniture will appear in reviews more often than you might expect.

A hot tub is an excellent complement for golf properties, providing muscle recovery in a social setting where the day's best shots and worst holes can be rehashed. If space allows, a steam shower in the primary bathroom or a compact sauna provides resort-level recovery amenities. Stock the bathrooms with Epsom salts, muscle recovery bath soaks, and quality body lotion -- small touches that acknowledge the physical reality of playing golf for four to five hours in the sun.

The Putting Green

A backyard putting green is the signature amenity that defines a golf property. Synthetic putting greens have advanced dramatically -- modern installations use multiple grass heights, realistic grain patterns, and professional-speed surfaces that closely mimic real greens. A well-designed putting green should include at least two hole cups, some subtle contouring for break practice, and a fringe area for chip shots. Size it at least 400 square feet to allow meaningful practice rather than a novelty.

Position the putting green visible from the main living area or patio so it becomes a natural extension of the social space. Add landscape lighting so it remains usable after sunset -- evening putting contests are a highlight of golf trips. Keep a set of quality putters and a bucket of practice balls available so guests do not have to unpack their own equipment for a casual putt. A chipping net in the adjacent yard adds another practice element without requiring a large footprint. These practice amenities keep guests engaged with golf even during downtime and make your property feel like a private golf retreat rather than just a place to sleep between rounds.

Resort-Quality Finishes Throughout

Golf travelers expect a level of finish that matches the clubhouses and resorts they frequent. This means solid surface countertops -- quartz or marble -- rather than laminate. Real hardwood or high-end luxury vinyl plank flooring rather than budget tile. Plumbing fixtures in brushed gold, matte black, or polished nickel from recognizable brands. Crown molding, wainscoting, or board-and-batten wall treatments that add architectural depth to rooms. These finish details communicate quality at a glance and justify the premium nightly rate that golf properties should command. Every touchpoint -- from the weight of a door handle to the quality of the bath towels -- should reinforce the message that this property was designed for guests who appreciate the finer things.

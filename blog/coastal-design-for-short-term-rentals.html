Coastal Design for Short Term Rentals

By MyBnBDesign Team • October 02, 2026

The Enduring Appeal of Coastal Design in Vacation Rentals

Coastal design remains one of the most sought-after aesthetics in the short term rental market. Whether your property sits on a beachfront or in a landlocked city, guests are drawn to the relaxed, airy atmosphere that coastal interiors create. This style evokes the feeling of a permanent vacation, which is exactly what travelers are paying for.

The beauty of coastal design lies in its ability to feel simultaneously curated and effortless. When executed well, guests walk into a space that makes them exhale. The palette, textures, and furnishings work together to lower stress and inspire relaxation from the moment they arrive.

Building Your Coastal Color Palette

Start with a base of crisp whites and sandy neutrals. Layer in soft blues ranging from seafoam to navy as accent colors. Incorporate warm natural tones through rattan, jute, and driftwood elements. The key is restraint. Avoid the temptation to turn every surface into a nautical theme park with anchors and seashells.

Sophisticated coastal design draws from the actual coastline rather than gift shop cliches. Think weathered wood tones, the blue-gray of morning fog over the ocean, and the warm ivory of sun-bleached linen. These are colors that occur naturally and feel timeless rather than trendy.

Essential Furniture and Textiles

Furniture in a coastal rental should feel approachable and comfortable. Slipcovered sofas in white or natural linen invite guests to sink in. Rattan accent chairs bring organic texture without heaviness. Wooden coffee tables with a weathered or whitewashed finish anchor the living space.

Bedrooms benefit from all-white bedding layered with a lightweight quilt in a subtle stripe or solid blue. Linen is the ideal fabric for coastal bedrooms because of its relaxed drape and natural texture. Include plenty of plush pillows and a woven throw at the foot of the bed.

Window treatments should maximize natural light. Sheer white curtains that billow gently are the classic choice. If privacy is needed, pair sheers with simple roller shades in a neutral tone. Heavy drapes have no place in coastal design.

Accessorizing Without Overdoing It

The line between tasteful coastal and tacky beach theme is thin but important. Choose accessories that reference the coast through material and form rather than literal imagery. A round mirror framed in natural rope, a ceramic vase in a sandy glaze, or a stack of coffee table books about surfing or marine photography all work beautifully.

Fresh greenery is essential. Tropical plants like fiddle leaf figs, bird of paradise, and monstera bring life and color to coastal interiors. If your property does not receive enough natural light for live plants, high-quality artificial options have become remarkably convincing.

Bathrooms are an opportunity to reinforce the coastal theme with natural stone tiles, wooden vanities, and spa-quality towels in white or soft blue. A rainfall showerhead and premium bath products complete the resort-like experience that earns glowing reviews.

Coastal Design as a Revenue Strategy

Properties with thoughtful coastal design consistently command premium rates, particularly in warm-weather markets. Even in northern cities, coastal interiors create an aspirational atmosphere that guests are willing to pay more for. The style photographs exceptionally well and creates scroll-stopping listing images.

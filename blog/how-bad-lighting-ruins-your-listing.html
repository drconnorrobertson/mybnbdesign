How Bad Lighting Ruins Your Listing

By MyBnBDesign Team • October 02, 2026

The Most Underestimated Design Element

Lighting is the single most impactful design element in any interior, yet it is consistently the most neglected in short-term rentals. Bad lighting affects everything: how your property photographs, how guests feel during their stay, how functional each room is, and how clean and maintained the space appears. No amount of beautiful furniture or thoughtful decor can overcome poor lighting.

The good news is that lighting improvements offer the highest return on investment of any design upgrade. A comprehensive lighting overhaul for a typical rental costs $500 to $2,000 and can transform both your listing photos and your guest experience overnight.

Why Overhead Lighting Fails

Most rental properties rely on a single overhead ceiling light per room. This creates flat, shadowless illumination from above that makes spaces look institutional and unflattering. Overhead-only lighting is the reason your rooms look fine in person but terrible in photos. The camera sees what your eyes compensate for: harsh downward shadows, uneven illumination, and cold, characterless light.

Overhead fixtures also create unflattering conditions for guests. Bright light from directly above casts shadows under eyes, emphasizes skin imperfections, and creates an atmosphere more appropriate for a conference room than a vacation retreat.

The Layered Lighting Formula

Professional designers use three layers of lighting in every room. Ambient lighting provides overall illumination and should be warm and diffused, never harsh. Table lamps, floor lamps, and wall sconces replace or supplement overhead fixtures. Task lighting illuminates specific work areas like kitchen counters, desks, and bathroom vanities where guests need focused, bright light for functional tasks.

Accent lighting highlights design features and creates visual interest through picture lights, LED strip lighting under cabinets, uplights behind furniture, and candles or flameless candles. The combination of these three layers creates the depth, warmth, and dimension that photographs beautifully and makes guests feel comfortable.

Every room in your rental should have at least three light sources. Bedrooms need bedside table lamps on both sides, overhead ambient lighting on a dimmer, and potentially a floor lamp or accent lighting. Living rooms need a minimum of four to five light sources to create adequate layering.

Lighting for Photos Versus Experience

Listing photos require bright, warm, even lighting that minimizes shadows and showcases the full room. Guest experience requires adjustable lighting that allows for bright morning illumination and soft evening ambiance. Installing dimmer switches on all ambient lighting lets you achieve both goals with the same fixtures.

Smart bulbs offer another layer of flexibility. Set them to bright and warm for photo sessions, then program evening scenes that create the cozy atmosphere guests love. The investment in smart lighting pays for itself through both better photos and better reviews.

Quick Wins for Better Lighting Today

Replace all cool-white bulbs with warm white bulbs in the 2700K to 3000K range. Add a table lamp to every surface that currently has none. Install dimmer switches on overhead lights. Place a floor lamp in dark corners. Add LED strip lighting under kitchen cabinets. These changes cost under $300 total and will visibly transform your listing photos and guest experience.

Light your property as if every room is being photographed at all times, because in the age of social media, it is.

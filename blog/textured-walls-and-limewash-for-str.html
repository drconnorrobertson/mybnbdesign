Textured Walls and Limewash for STR

Published 2026-10-02

Textured walls have become the signature design element of 2026 STR properties, with limewash paint leading the charge as the most sought-after wall treatment in the vacation rental industry. The flat, uniform painted wall that dominated rental interiors for decades is giving way to surfaces with depth, movement, and character. This shift reflects a broader desire for spaces that feel handcrafted and authentic rather than manufactured and identical. For hosts, textured walls offer a relatively affordable way to dramatically elevate the perceived quality of their property.

Understanding Limewash

Limewash is a paint technique with ancient origins that creates a soft, chalky, multidimensional finish on walls. Unlike conventional paint, which sits on the surface in a uniform layer, limewash partially absorbs into the wall material and dries with natural variation in tone and opacity. The result is a living, breathing surface that shifts subtly with changing light throughout the day. Morning sun reveals warm undertones, while evening light brings out cooler depths. This dynamic quality creates walls that feel organic and interesting, providing a beautiful backdrop for furniture and art without competing for attention.

Limewash Application for STR Properties

Applying limewash in an STR requires some planning but is well within reach for most hosts. Traditional lime-based limewash works best on porous surfaces like raw plaster, brick, or stone. For drywall, which is the most common wall surface in residential properties, mineral-based limewash products designed specifically for painted surfaces are available. These products mimic the appearance of traditional limewash while adhering properly to sealed surfaces. Application uses a large brush rather than a roller, and the technique involves working in random cross-hatch strokes to create the characteristic variation. The process is forgiving, and the natural imperfections are part of the charm.

Color Selection for Limewash Walls

The limewash color palette works best in soft, muted tones that allow the natural variation of the finish to shine. Warm whites, soft pinks, gentle taupes, muted sage greens, and pale terracotta are all excellent choices for STR properties. Because limewash creates its own visual complexity through tonal variation, even a single color creates a richly textured surface. Avoid bright or saturated colors for full-room limewash applications, as the variation in these intense tones can feel overwhelming. Instead, use bolder colors for accent walls where the textural effect adds drama to a single focal surface.

Venetian Plaster and Textured Plaster Finishes

Beyond limewash, other textured plaster techniques offer different aesthetic options for STR walls. Venetian plaster creates a smooth, polished surface with subtle depth and a slight sheen that feels luxurious and old-world. Roman clay produces a softer, more matte texture that resembles suede. Tadelakt, a traditional Moroccan plaster, creates a waterproof, stone-like finish perfect for bathrooms. Each technique requires different application skills and produces distinctive results. For STR properties, these finishes work best in high-impact areas like behind the headboard, on a living room accent wall, or in the bathroom where their texture can be appreciated up close.

Board and Batten, Wainscoting, and Paneling

Dimensional wall treatments using wood or MDF paneling add texture through physical depth rather than surface treatment. Board and batten creates clean vertical lines that add height and architectural interest. Wainscoting protects the lower portion of walls from wear while adding traditional character. Shiplap creates a casual, coastal character. Fluted or reeded paneling adds contemporary sophistication. These treatments are particularly practical for STR properties because they protect walls from scuffs and damage while simultaneously upgrading the visual quality of the space. The lower portion of walls, which takes the most abuse in high-turnover properties, becomes both protected and beautiful.

Wallpaper as Texture

Modern wallpaper offers textural effects that cannot be achieved with paint or plaster. Grasscloth brings natural woven texture and warmth to walls. Textured vinyl provides dimensional patterns that cast subtle shadows. Flocked wallpaper creates velvet-like surfaces that beg to be touched. For STR properties, these textured wallpapers work best on accent walls where their visual and tactile qualities can be fully appreciated. The bedroom wall behind the bed is the most popular location, creating a dramatic backdrop that photographs beautifully and adds a sensory dimension that guests notice when they lean back against the pillows.

Getting Started with Textured Walls

Begin with a single accent wall in your highest-traffic room. Limewash is the most accessible starting point for most hosts, requiring only a brush, the product, and willingness to embrace imperfection. A limewashed accent wall behind the bed or in the main living area can be completed in one day and will immediately transform the character of the space. Photograph the wall in different lighting conditions for your listing, as the way textured surfaces interact with light is one of their most compelling qualities. Once you see the guest response to your first textured wall, you will likely want to extend the treatment throughout the property. Each textured surface adds another layer of quality and character that distinguishes your listing from the competition.

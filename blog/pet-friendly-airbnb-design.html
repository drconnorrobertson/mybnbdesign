Pet-Friendly Airbnb Design: Welcoming Guests and Their Pets

By MyBnBDesign Team · September 18, 2026

Pet ownership has surged in recent years, and travelers increasingly refuse to leave their furry family members behind. According to industry data, over 60 percent of pet owners say they would only book accommodations that welcome their animals. That is a massive audience -- and most Airbnb hosts are turning them away. The hosts who embrace pet-friendly design are tapping into a guest pool with less competition and strong willingness to pay premium rates and pet fees.

But welcoming pets does not mean accepting damage. Smart design choices protect your property while creating an experience that pet owners rave about. Here is how to build a pet-friendly rental that is both durable and beautiful.

Flooring That Survives Claws and Accidents

Flooring is the most critical design decision in a pet-friendly rental. Carpet is out of the question -- it traps hair, absorbs odors, and stains permanently. Hardwood looks beautiful but scratches easily under dog nails and warps when liquids sit too long. The best options for pet-friendly properties are luxury vinyl plank (LVP) and porcelain tile.

LVP has become the gold standard for pet-friendly STRs. It is scratch-resistant, waterproof, easy to clean, and available in designs that convincingly mimic hardwood. Choose a textured finish in a medium tone -- too light shows every hair, too dark shows every scratch. Look for products with a wear layer of at least 20 mil for commercial-grade durability. Porcelain tile works beautifully in entryways, kitchens, and bathrooms where moisture is most likely.

Add washable area rugs in living spaces and bedrooms for comfort and style. Choose rugs with a flat weave or low pile that can be thrown in a washing machine or taken outside and hosed down. Indoor-outdoor rugs in natural fiber patterns look great and handle pet traffic without complaint.

Performance Fabrics on Every Surface

The days of choosing between pet-friendly and stylish are over. Performance fabrics like Crypton, Sunbrella, and Revolution have transformed the game. These materials resist stains, repel liquids, and clean up with a damp cloth -- all while looking and feeling like high-end upholstery. Use performance fabric on sofas, accent chairs, dining chairs, and any surface a pet might touch.

Choose sofa cushions with removable, washable covers in case of accidents. Dark or patterned fabrics hide pet hair better than solid light colors, but a good lint roller and regular vacuuming between guests keep any fabric looking fresh. Leather and faux leather are also excellent choices -- they do not trap hair, they wipe clean easily, and they develop a patina that only improves with age.

For bedding, provide a dedicated pet blanket that guests can place on the bed or couch. This gives pet owners permission to let their animals on the furniture while protecting your linens. Stock an extra set of towels specifically for drying wet dogs after walks or baths.

Creating Pet Zones

Designating specific areas for pets helps manage the experience for both the animals and the property. Near the entry, create a pet station with hooks for leashes, a shelf for treats, and a mat for muddy paws. Provide stainless steel food and water bowls -- ceramic bowls chip and plastic bowls slide. A waterproof mat under the bowls protects the floor.

In the living area, place a dog bed in a comfortable corner. Choose a bed with a washable cover and enough padding that dogs actually want to use it. Some guests bring their own beds, but having one available shows thoughtfulness and gives pets a designated spot. If you have the space, a crate or kennel stored in a closet can be offered to guests who need it for crate-trained dogs.

The Fenced Yard Advantage

A fenced yard is the single most searched-for amenity among pet-owning travelers. It is the difference between a guest choosing your property or scrolling past it. If your property has a yard, fencing it is one of the highest-ROI investments you can make. Choose a fence that is at least four feet high -- six feet for properties that welcome large dogs -- with no gaps at the bottom where small dogs could squeeze through.

Add a dog door or pet-accessible entrance to the yard if the layout allows it. This lets dogs go in and out freely without guests needing to monitor the door constantly. Include a waste station with bags and a covered trash can in the yard -- guests appreciate not having to carry bags back inside. A simple outdoor water bowl and a shaded area complete the setup.

Pet Fees as a Revenue Stream

Pet fees are not just a way to cover potential damage -- they are a legitimate revenue stream. Most pet-friendly Airbnbs charge between $25 and $75 per pet per night, or a flat fee of $50 to $150 per stay. Because pet-friendly inventory is limited, pet owners are accustomed to paying these fees and rarely push back. A $50 pet fee on a five-night stay adds $250 to your revenue with minimal additional cost.

Pair the pet fee with a pet damage deposit or pet damage protection through your booking platform. This gives you a safety net without discouraging bookings. In your listing, be specific about what pets are welcome -- size limits, breed restrictions if any, and maximum number of pets. Clear policies reduce misunderstandings and attract responsible pet owners who appreciate the transparency.

Marketing Your Pet-Friendly Property

Once your property is designed for pets, make sure your listing shouts it from the rooftop. Include photos of the fenced yard, the pet station, the dog bed, and the welcome amenities. Mention pet-friendly features in your title and description -- terms like "fenced yard" and "dog-friendly" are among the most-searched filters on every platform. List nearby dog parks, pet-friendly restaurants, hiking trails, and veterinary clinics in your guidebook. Pet owners plan their entire trip around their animal's needs, and a property that anticipates those needs earns loyalty that no hotel can match.

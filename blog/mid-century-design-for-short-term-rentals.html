Mid-Century Design for Short Term Rentals

By MyBnBDesign Team • October 02, 2026

Mid-Century Modern: A Timeless Choice for STR Properties

Mid-century modern design has proven to be far more than a passing trend. Its enduring popularity makes it an excellent choice for short term rental properties seeking a distinctive aesthetic that appeals across demographics. The style's emphasis on form, function, and connection to nature aligns perfectly with what today's travelers seek in vacation accommodations.

Properties styled in mid-century modern consistently rank among the most-saved and most-booked on major platforms. The style is inherently photogenic, with its iconic furniture silhouettes and warm wood tones creating listing images that stop scrollers in their tracks.

Defining the Mid-Century Look

Mid-century modern design originated in the post-war era and is characterized by organic curves, clean lines, and a seamless integration of indoor and outdoor living. The palette leans warm with teak and walnut wood tones, olive and avocado greens, mustard yellows, and burnt oranges against clean white or warm neutral backgrounds.

Architecture plays a significant role, but you do not need a vintage home to achieve the look. Focus on the furnishings and accessories. Hairpin legs, tapered furniture legs, geometric patterns, and atomic-inspired shapes all reference the era without requiring structural changes to your property.

Furniture That Defines the Space

Iconic mid-century furniture pieces serve as the foundation of this style. While original vintage pieces can be expensive, high-quality reproductions are widely available. A molded shell chair, a low-profile teak credenza, a tulip dining table, and a leather lounge chair with ottoman immediately establish the mid-century vocabulary.

Sofas should be low-slung with tapered wooden legs and clean cushions. Choose upholstery in a warm neutral or a period-appropriate accent color. The sofa is the visual anchor of the living room and should feel inviting without being overstuffed.

Bedroom furniture should include a platform bed or headboard in warm wood, matching nightstands with simple drawer pulls, and a low dresser that can double as a media console. Keep the bed frame visible and the overall profile low to the ground.

Lighting and Accessories

Lighting is a signature element of mid-century design. Sputnik chandeliers, arc floor lamps, globe pendants, and cone-shaped table lamps all reference the era beautifully. Choose fixtures in brass, brushed gold, or matte black for authentic appeal.

Wall art should lean toward abstract expressionism, geometric prints, or nature photography. Large-scale pieces in simple frames create the most impact. A starburst mirror or clock adds a playful mid-century accent to any wall.

Accessories should be minimal but purposeful. A collection of ceramic vessels in earth tones, a potted rubber plant or snake plant, and a stack of vintage-inspired books create vignettes that photograph well and feel authentic to the period.

Mid-Century Design ROI

The investment in mid-century modern design pays dividends through higher nightly rates and stronger occupancy. The style attracts design-conscious travelers who are willing to pay more for a distinctive experience. These guests also tend to treat properties with greater care, reducing wear and damage costs.

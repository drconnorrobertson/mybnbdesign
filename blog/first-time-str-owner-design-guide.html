First-Time STR Owner Design Guide

By MyBnBDesign Team • October 02, 2026

Your Complete Design Guide for Your First Short Term Rental

Launching your first short term rental is exciting, but the design decisions you make in the beginning will determine your property's earning potential for years to come. First-time owners often make costly mistakes by either overspending on items that do not impact revenue or underspending on the elements guests care about most.

This guide walks you through the essential design decisions in priority order, helping you allocate your budget where it matters most and avoid the traps that derail new rental owners. Follow this roadmap and you will launch with a property that competes effectively from day one.

Start with the Guest Experience, Not the Aesthetic

Before selecting a single piece of furniture, define your target guest and their expectations. A property targeting business travelers needs reliable workspace and fast wifi. A family-friendly rental needs durable materials and safety considerations. A romantic getaway needs atmosphere and luxury touches. Your design should serve your specific guest profile.

Walk through your property and identify every moment of guest interaction. The arrival, the first view of the living space, sitting on the sofa, climbing into bed, using the kitchen, and showering in the bathroom. Each of these moments is a design opportunity to exceed expectations or disappoint. Prioritize your budget toward the moments guests care about most.

Budget Allocation for Maximum Impact

Allocate your design budget roughly as follows for maximum return. Spend 30 percent on the bedroom, including the mattress, bedding, and bedroom furniture. Spend 25 percent on the living room, including the sofa and main seating. Spend 20 percent on the kitchen and dining area. Spend 15 percent on the bathroom. Reserve 10 percent for decor, accessories, and styling.

Within each room, spend the most on items guests physically touch. The mattress matters more than the bed frame. The sofa comfort matters more than the coffee table. The towel quality matters more than the bathroom art. Guests judge your property by tactile experience more than visual impressions.

Essential Purchases for Every New STR

Regardless of your style direction, certain items are non-negotiable for a competitive listing. A quality mattress with a mattress protector, hotel-weight white sheets in at least 300-thread count, four pillows per bed in two firmness levels, a quality duvet, and backup bedding sets for quick turnovers are your foundation.

The bathroom needs plush white towels in at least two full sets per guest, a bath mat, quality soap dispensers, a hair dryer, and a well-lit mirror. The kitchen needs a complete cookware set, sharp knives, essential utensils, matching dishware for your maximum occupancy plus two extra settings, and basic pantry staples like oil, salt, pepper, and coffee.

For the living room, invest in a comfortable sofa that seats your maximum occupancy, adequate lighting from multiple sources, and a smart TV with streaming capabilities. These three elements are the minimum viable living room for a competitive listing.

Common First-Timer Mistakes to Avoid

Do not use your own old furniture. Hand-me-downs communicate budget accommodation even if the individual pieces are decent. Start fresh with a cohesive plan. Do not skip professional photos. The best-designed property in the world will underperform with phone photos. Do not over-personalize. Your property should feel like a boutique hotel, not your living room. Family photos, political messaging, and highly specific decor alienate potential guests.

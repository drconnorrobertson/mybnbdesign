Wellness Retreat Design: Creating a Healing STR Experience

By MyBnBDesign Team · September 21, 2026

Wellness travel is no longer a luxury reserved for boutique resorts and destination spas. Travelers are actively seeking short-term rentals that support their physical and mental wellbeing -- properties where they can decompress, reconnect with nature, and return home feeling genuinely restored. The wellness tourism market is projected to reach over $1 trillion globally, and savvy STR hosts are capturing a meaningful share of that spend by designing properties with healing in mind.

Creating a wellness-oriented rental does not require a complete renovation or a mountainside location. It requires intentional design choices that engage the senses, reduce stress, and create an environment where guests naturally slow down. Here is how to transform your short-term rental into a wellness retreat that commands premium nightly rates.

Biophilic Design: Bringing Nature Inside

Biophilic design -- the practice of incorporating natural elements into built environments -- is the foundation of any wellness-focused space. Research consistently shows that exposure to nature reduces cortisol levels, lowers blood pressure, and improves mood. You do not need a forest view to achieve this effect. Indoor plants are the most accessible starting point. Place a large fiddle-leaf fig or monstera in the living room, pothos in the bathroom, and succulents on nightstands. Choose hardy, low-maintenance varieties that can survive between guest stays with minimal care.

Natural materials amplify the biophilic effect. Use wood surfaces -- reclaimed wood shelving, a live-edge dining table, or wooden ceiling beams -- to add warmth and organic texture. Stone accents in the bathroom or kitchen connect guests to the earth. Natural fiber textiles -- linen curtains, jute rugs, cotton throws -- complete the sensory palette. Avoid anything synthetic, plasticky, or overly processed in the design. Every material should feel like it came from nature, not a factory.

Maximize natural light throughout the property. Sheer curtains that filter sunlight without blocking it, skylights where possible, and light-colored walls that reflect daylight all contribute to a space that feels alive and connected to the outdoors. If the property has views of greenery, water, or sky, orient furniture to face those views.

Meditation and Yoga Spaces

A dedicated meditation or yoga space elevates your property from a nice rental to a true wellness destination. This does not need to be an entire room -- a corner of a bedroom, a section of a covered porch, or a flat area in the garden will do. The key is intentionality. Lay down a quality yoga mat, add a meditation cushion, and place a small table with a candle, a plant, and perhaps a singing bowl or a set of meditation cards.

If the property has outdoor space, create a meditation garden with a simple bench, gravel or stepping stones, and screening plants for privacy. A small water feature -- a tabletop fountain or a wall-mounted water wall -- adds a soothing audio element that masks ambient noise and invites stillness. Include a Bluetooth speaker nearby with a curated playlist of ambient sounds or guided meditations loaded on a tablet.

Provide a small bookshelf or basket with wellness-oriented reading -- books on mindfulness, yoga, nature, and personal growth. A journal and pen on the nightstand invites guests to reflect. These details cost very little but create an experience that guests photograph, share on social media, and mention in reviews.

The Spa-Inspired Bathroom

In a wellness rental, the bathroom is a sanctuary. Start with a soaking tub -- freestanding if possible -- and stock it with bath salts, essential oils, and a tray for holding a book or a glass of wine. A rain shower with eucalyptus bundles tied to the showerhead creates a spa-like aromatherapy experience that costs almost nothing to maintain.

Choose premium, natural toiletries -- products with clean ingredients, botanical scents, and attractive packaging. Present them on a wooden tray or stone shelf. Provide plush white towels, a terry cloth robe for each guest, and slippers. A heated towel rack adds a touch of luxury that guests remember. Soft, warm lighting on a dimmer switch -- no harsh overhead fluorescents -- completes the spa atmosphere.

Sleep Optimization

Quality sleep is the ultimate wellness amenity. Start with the mattress -- invest in the best you can afford. A medium-firm mattress with a cooling gel topper suits most sleepers. Provide a selection of pillows -- firm, medium, and soft -- so guests can choose what works for them. Use organic cotton or bamboo sheets in a neutral color, and layer the bed with a lightweight duvet and an extra blanket at the foot.

Blackout curtains are essential for sleep quality. Add a white noise machine or a smart speaker with a sleep sounds skill. Remove all blue-light-emitting devices from the bedroom, or at least provide blue-light-blocking glasses on the nightstand. A diffuser with lavender essential oil, set on a timer, creates a calming bedtime ritual without requiring guest effort. Keep the bedroom temperature between 65 and 68 degrees Fahrenheit -- the optimal range for deep sleep -- and provide a fan for guests who prefer air circulation.

The Organic Kitchen and Nourishment

Wellness travelers care deeply about what they eat. Stock the kitchen with the tools they need to prepare healthy meals -- a high-speed blender for smoothies, a juicer if budget allows, quality knives, and a well-seasoned cast-iron skillet. Provide a curated selection of organic teas, local honey, and a few pantry staples like olive oil, sea salt, and spices.

Include a guidebook section on local farmers markets, organic grocery stores, farm-to-table restaurants, and health food cafes. If the property has outdoor space, a small herb garden with basil, rosemary, mint, and thyme gives guests fresh ingredients and a connection to the land. A water filtration pitcher or under-sink filter ensures guests have access to clean, fresh water without plastic bottles. These kitchen touches reinforce the wellness narrative and show guests that every detail of their stay has been considered with their health in mind.

The Premium Pricing Advantage

Wellness-focused properties consistently achieve 25 to 40 percent higher nightly rates than comparable standard listings. Guests who prioritize wellness are typically affluent, health-conscious, and willing to pay for an experience that aligns with their values. They leave detailed, positive reviews. They share your property on wellness-focused social media accounts. And they return -- often booking annual retreats at the same property. Designing for wellness is not a niche play -- it is a premium positioning strategy that elevates your brand and your bottom line.

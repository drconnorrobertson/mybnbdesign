The Short-Term Rental Design Glossary

122 plain-English definitions covering interior design, guest experience, and rental performance, written for Airbnb and vacation rental owners by MyBnBDesign.

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ

Whether you are furnishing your first Airbnb or refreshing a portfolio of vacation rentals, these terms will help you speak the language of design and hospitality. Each entry explains how the concept applies to short-term rentals and links to the FAQ page where you can learn more.

A

Accent Wall

A single wall finished in a bolder color, pattern, or texture to draw the eye. In an Airbnb it creates a memorable backdrop behind the bed or sofa that photographs well and gives the listing a signature look.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

ADR (Average Daily Rate)

The average revenue earned per booked night over a given period. STR owners use ADR to measure how well design upgrades and pricing strategy are working, since a well-designed property can command a higher rate.

Read more: Revenue FAQ

Ambient Lighting

The general, overall illumination that fills a room and sets its base brightness. In a short-term rental it should feel warm and welcoming from the first moment a guest walks in.

Read more: Lighting FAQ

Amenity

Any feature or item provided for guest comfort or convenience, from Wi-Fi and coffee makers to hot tubs. Amenities directly influence search filters, reviews, and the nightly rate you can charge.

Read more: Amenities FAQ

Art Curation

The deliberate selection and placement of artwork to support a property's theme and story. For STRs, curated art adds personality and perceived value without adding clutter or risk of theft.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

B

Backsplash

The protective wall surface behind a sink or stove, usually tile or another wipeable material. In rentals, a durable, easy-to-clean backsplash handles heavy guest cooking while adding a design highlight to the kitchen.

Read more: Materials FAQ

Bed Configuration

The number, size, and arrangement of beds in a property. Smart configurations such as queens, bunks, or sleeper sofas raise sleeping capacity, which expands your guest count and your revenue potential.

Read more: Space Planning FAQ

Biophilic Design

A design approach that connects interiors to nature through plants, natural light, and organic materials. Guests often describe biophilic Airbnbs as calming, which tends to show up in higher cleanliness and comfort ratings.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Boutique Style

A design look that feels curated, characterful, and distinct from a standard hotel room. It lets an STR stand out in crowded markets by offering a one-of-a-kind stay that chain hotels cannot match.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Brand Identity

The visual and emotional personality of your rental, including its name, palette, tone, and style. A consistent brand identity helps repeat guests recognize your property and supports direct booking efforts.

Read more: Design Process FAQ

Breakage Fee

A charge or reserve built into the budget to cover damaged or lost items. Planning for breakage in a vacation rental helps owners keep inventory replacement predictable rather than a surprise expense.

Read more: Pricing FAQ

C

Check-in Experience

Everything a guest encounters from arrival through their first ten minutes in the property. A smooth check-in, clear signage, and a welcoming first impression set the tone for the entire review.

Read more: Guest Experience FAQ

Cleaning Protocol

A documented, step-by-step system for cleaning the property between stays. Consistent protocols keep standards high, protect your cleanliness score, and make design choices easier to maintain.

Read more: Cleaning and Maintenance FAQ

Color Palette

The specific set of colors chosen for a space, including walls, textiles, and accents. A tight palette makes an STR feel intentional and photographs cohesively across every room.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Color Story

The narrative thread created by repeating a few related colors throughout a property. It guides the eye from room to room so the home feels unified rather than assembled from random pieces.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Complementary Colors

Colors that sit opposite each other on the color wheel, such as blue and orange. Used sparingly, they add energy and contrast to a rental's decor and make listing photos pop.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Cost Per Guest

The furnishing and design spend divided by the number of guests the property can sleep. It helps owners compare design options and decide where extra budget will pay back through higher capacity or rates.

Read more: Pricing FAQ

Curb Appeal

How attractive a property looks from the street or approach. In STRs, curb appeal drives click-through on the cover photo and shapes guest expectations before they step inside.

Read more: Photography FAQ

Custom Fabrication

Furniture or fixtures built to a specific size, material, or design rather than bought off the shelf. Custom pieces such as built-in bunks or banquettes let owners fit more guests into awkward spaces.

Read more: Furniture FAQ

D

Decor Package

A bundled set of decorative items such as art, mirrors, lamps, and accessories delivered together. For Airbnb owners it speeds up setup and keeps the look consistent across every room.

Read more: Pricing FAQ

Deep Cleaning

A thorough, periodic clean that goes beyond routine turnover tasks, covering items like upholstery, grout, and vents. Scheduled deep cleans extend the life of furnishings and keep review scores strong.

Read more: Cleaning and Maintenance FAQ

Design Brief

A written summary of the project's goals, budget, audience, style, and constraints. It aligns the owner and designer from day one and keeps an STR project on target and on budget.

Read more: Design Process FAQ

Design Concept

The central idea and visual direction that guides all design decisions for a property. A strong concept gives an Airbnb a clear story that shows up in the listing title, photos, and guest reviews.

Read more: Design Process FAQ

Dimmable Lighting

Fixtures and bulbs that allow brightness to be adjusted. Dimmers let guests shift a rental from bright morning mode to relaxed evening mood, which is a small upgrade guests often mention.

Read more: Lighting FAQ

Direct Booking

A reservation made through the owner's own website rather than a marketplace like Airbnb. Strong branding and design help convert direct bookings and reduce platform commission fees.

Read more: Revenue FAQ

Durable Fabrics

Textiles built to resist wear, stains, and fading, such as solution-dyed acrylics or performance weaves. They are essential in STRs, where furniture sees constant use by many different guests.

Read more: Materials FAQ

E

Eco-Friendly Design

Design choices that reduce environmental impact, such as recycled materials, LED lighting, and low-VOC paints. Many modern travelers actively seek out sustainable stays, so eco choices can support bookings.

Read more: Materials FAQ

Entryway Experience

The look, feel, and function of the first space a guest enters. A well-planned entry with hooks, a bench, and a welcome touch makes guests feel at home immediately.

Read more: Guest Experience FAQ

Essential Amenities

The basic items every guest expects, such as towels, toilet paper, soap, and bedding. Airbnb requires these for listing quality, and missing them is a common cause of negative reviews.

Read more: Amenities FAQ

F

FF&E (Furniture Fixtures and Equipment)

The movable items in a property that are not part of the building structure, including furniture, lamps, and appliances. FF&E is usually the largest line item in an STR furnishing budget and may carry tax benefits, so owners should confirm details with a tax advisor.

Read more: Furniture FAQ

Focal Point

The element in a room that naturally draws the eye first, such as a fireplace, view, or bold headboard. Designing around a focal point gives each space purpose and creates standout listing photos.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Full-Service Design

A design engagement that covers concept, sourcing, ordering, delivery, and installation. It suits STR owners who want a finished, guest-ready property without managing vendors themselves.

Read more: Design Process FAQ

Functional Layout

A floor plan arranged so people can move, sleep, cook, and relax comfortably. In rentals, functional layouts reduce guest complaints and make it easier to fit more beds without crowding.

Read more: Space Planning FAQ

Furnishing Package

A pre-selected group of furniture and accessories delivered as a complete set for a property. It simplifies budgeting and speeds up launch for new Airbnb and vacation rental owners.

Read more: Pricing FAQ

G

Gallery Wall

A grouped arrangement of framed art, photos, or objects on one wall. It adds personality and local flavor to an STR while hiding minor wall imperfections.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Guest Experience

The total impression a guest has from booking through checkout. Design is a major driver of the experience, shaping comfort, convenience, and the memories that turn into five-star reviews.

Read more: Guest Experience FAQ

Guest-Proof

Designed to withstand heavy use, spills, and occasional misuse from many different guests. Guest-proof choices, such as wipeable finishes and secured decor, lower repair costs over time.

Read more: Materials FAQ

Guidebook

A digital or printed resource with local recommendations, property tips, and instructions. A good guidebook reduces guest questions and reinforces your brand as a thoughtful host.

Read more: Guest Experience FAQ

H

Headboard

The upright panel at the head of a bed that anchors the sleeping area. In STRs it acts as a visual focal point and a protective surface for the wall behind it.

Read more: Furniture FAQ

High-Touch Amenity

A surface or item guests handle frequently, such as door handles, remotes, and light switches. These areas need extra attention in cleaning protocols and benefit from durable, easy-to-sanitize materials.

Read more: Amenities FAQ

Home Staging

The process of arranging furniture and decor to present a property at its best. In the STR world, staging is used for photos and for creating an inviting arrival experience.

Read more: Photography FAQ

Hospitality Design

An approach to interiors borrowed from hotels, focused on comfort, durability, and guest delight. Applying hospitality principles to Airbnbs helps them deliver a boutique stay at a fraction of hotel cost.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

House Manual

A clear guide explaining how to use the home, including Wi-Fi, appliances, trash day, and house rules. A thorough house manual cuts down on guest messages and prevents avoidable mistakes.

Read more: Guest Experience FAQ

I

Indoor-Outdoor Living

A design approach that blends interior spaces with patios, decks, or yards through open doors and matching materials. It increases perceived square footage, which is a big draw for vacation rental guests.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Inventory List

A complete record of every item in the property, including quantity and condition. Owners and cleaners use it to track losses, plan replacements, and keep the setup consistent.

Read more: Design Process FAQ

Investment Property

Real estate purchased to generate income rather than serve as a primary residence. Design decisions for an investment property should be driven by guest appeal and return, not personal taste.

Read more: Revenue FAQ

J

Japanese Minimalism

A calm, pared-back style emphasizing natural materials, low furniture, and uncluttered space. It works well in compact STRs, creating a serene feel that is also easy to clean and reset.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

K

Key Exchange

The method by which guests receive access to the property, such as a lockbox, smart lock, or in-person handoff. A seamless key exchange removes arrival stress and supports self check-in.

Read more: Guest Experience FAQ

Kitchen Essentials

The core cookware, dishes, utensils, and small appliances a guest needs to prepare meals. A well-stocked kitchen is one of the most mentioned features in positive STR reviews.

Read more: Amenities FAQ

Kitchenette

A compact kitchen area with limited appliances, typically a small fridge, microwave, and sink. It adds value to studios and guest suites by letting guests prepare simple meals and stay longer.

Read more: Space Planning FAQ

L

Layering

The practice of combining textures, lighting types, and textiles to add depth to a space. Layered rooms feel cozy and finished, which comes across clearly in listing photos.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Lead Time

The time between placing an order and receiving the product. Planning around furniture lead times is critical for hitting an STR launch date and avoiding lost booking revenue.

Read more: Timeline FAQ

Lighting Plan

A layout showing the type and placement of ambient, task, and accent lighting in each room. A good plan improves usability, mood, and photography across the whole property.

Read more: Lighting FAQ

Linens

Bed sheets, towels, and other washable textiles used in a rental. Quality, matching linens, with extra sets for turnover, make the property feel clean and hotel-like.

Read more: Amenities FAQ

Listing Optimization

Improving a listing's title, photos, description, and amenities to rank higher and convert more views into bookings. Design upgrades feed directly into optimization by giving you better photos and stronger selling points.

Read more: Revenue FAQ

Luxury Amenity

A premium feature such as a hot tub, sauna, or cinema room that elevates a stay. Luxury amenities can justify a significantly higher nightly rate when matched with strong design.

Read more: Amenities FAQ

M

Material Selection

The process of choosing finishes, fabrics, and surfaces for a project. In STRs, selection balances beauty with durability, cleanability, and cost.

Read more: Materials FAQ

Maximalist Design

A bold style that embraces layered patterns, saturated color, and abundant decor. When done with intention, it gives an Airbnb a distinctive, memorable character, though it requires more upkeep.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Mid-Century Modern

A style from the mid-1900s featuring clean lines, tapered legs, and warm wood tones. Its popularity with travelers makes it a reliable choice for design-forward vacation rentals.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Minimalist Design

A style that favors simplicity, limited decor, and clear surfaces. Minimalist STRs are easy to clean and reset between guests, which keeps turnover efficient.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Mood Board

A collage of colors, textures, furniture, and inspiration images that captures a design direction. Mood boards help owners approve a concept before any money is spent on products.

Read more: Design Process FAQ

Multifunctional Furniture

Pieces that serve more than one purpose, such as sleeper sofas, storage ottomans, or fold-down desks. They help small STRs sleep more guests and stay uncluttered.

Read more: Furniture FAQ

N

Nautical Theme

A coastal-inspired look using navy, white, rope, and sea-related accents. It suits lake and beach rentals when applied with restraint to avoid feeling like a theme park.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Negative Space

The empty area around and between objects in a room. Leaving enough negative space prevents a rental from feeling cramped and improves how rooms appear in photos.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Neutral Palette

A color scheme built on whites, beiges, grays, and soft earth tones. Neutral palettes appeal to the widest range of guests and make it easy to refresh accents over time.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Nightly Rate

The price charged per night for a stay. Design quality, amenities, and photography all contribute to how high a nightly rate the market will support.

Read more: Revenue FAQ

O

Occupancy Rate

The percentage of available nights that are actually booked. Strong design, great photos, and a polished guest experience all help raise occupancy.

Read more: Revenue FAQ

Open Floor Plan

A layout where the kitchen, dining, and living areas flow together without walls. It makes spaces feel larger and more social, which suits group stays and family vacations.

Read more: Space Planning FAQ

Outdoor Living Space

A patio, deck, balcony, or yard styled for relaxing and entertaining. Well-designed outdoor spaces are a top booking driver and can extend the usable square footage of a rental.

Read more: Space Planning FAQ

P

Passive Income

Earnings that require limited day-to-day effort once systems are in place. STR income is rarely fully passive, but good design and clear systems reduce the time owners spend managing the property.

Read more: Revenue FAQ

Patio Design

The planning of outdoor seating, dining, lighting, and decor on a patio. Weather-resistant furniture and string or path lighting make a patio inviting and photogenic.

Read more: Space Planning FAQ

Performance Fabrics

Textiles engineered to repel stains, moisture, and wear. They keep sofas and chairs looking new through heavy guest use, reducing replacement costs.

Read more: Materials FAQ

Photoshoot Styling

Preparing a space with extra decor, linens, and props specifically for professional photos. Styling for the shoot ensures the listing images show the property at its very best.

Read more: Photography FAQ

Procurement

The sourcing, purchasing, and delivery coordination of all furnishings for a project. Good procurement keeps an STR build on budget and on schedule by managing vendors and lead times.

Read more: Design Process FAQ

Professional Photography

High-quality, technically skilled photos of a property taken with proper equipment and lighting. Listings with professional photos typically earn more clicks and bookings than those with phone snapshots.

Read more: Photography FAQ

Property Management

The day-to-day operation of a rental, including guest communication, cleaning, and maintenance. Design choices that are durable and simple to maintain make management easier and cheaper.

Read more: Property Management FAQ

Property Staging

Furnishing and decorating a property to showcase its potential to buyers or guests. For STRs, staging gives a new listing a launch-ready look quickly.

Read more: Photography FAQ

Q

Quality Assurance

The final inspection process that confirms a property is clean, stocked, and exactly as designed. QA checklists protect your reviews by catching small issues before a guest does.

Read more: Cleaning and Maintenance FAQ

Queen Bed Standard

The common practice of using queen-size beds as the default sleeping option in bedrooms. Queens balance comfort and guest appeal, and they fit most bedroom footprints without crowding.

Read more: Furniture FAQ

R

Refresh Cycle

A planned schedule for updating decor, linens, and worn items. A regular refresh cycle keeps an STR looking current and protects its competitive position over time.

Read more: Timeline FAQ

Remote Design

Design services delivered online through virtual consultations, digital plans, and shipped furnishings. Remote design lets investors furnish properties in any market without traveling.

Read more: Remote Design FAQ

Renovation

Structural or cosmetic updates to improve a property, such as new floors, kitchens, or baths. For STRs, renovation dollars should focus on changes that raise guest appeal and nightly rate.

Read more: Timeline FAQ

Revenue Per Available Night (RevPAR)

A metric calculated by multiplying occupancy rate by average daily rate. It gives a single picture of how well a rental performs, making it useful for comparing before and after a design update.

Read more: Revenue FAQ

ROI

Return on investment, the gain from a project compared to what it cost. In STR design, ROI is measured by how much additional income a furnishing or upgrade generates relative to its price.

Read more: Revenue FAQ

Room Layout

The arrangement of furniture and features within a single room. Smart room layouts maximize sleeping space and keep clear paths for guests to move comfortably.

Read more: Space Planning FAQ

Rustic Design

A style built on natural wood, stone, and handcrafted elements. It is a staple of cabin and mountain rentals, where guests want a cozy, getaway feel.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

S

Scale Drawing

A drawing in which dimensions are proportionally reduced to represent a real space. Designers use scale drawings to confirm furniture fits before ordering for your rental.

Read more: Space Planning FAQ

Schematic Design

The early phase of design where layouts and concepts are sketched out for approval. It lets STR owners review direction and budget before detailed selections begin.

Read more: Design Process FAQ

Seasonal Decor

Decorative items that change with the time of year or holidays. Light seasonal touches keep repeat guests engaged and help listing photos feel timely.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Short-Term Rental (STR)

A property rented for brief stays, typically under 30 days, through platforms like Airbnb and Vrbo. STRs rely on design, amenities, and guest experience to compete for bookings.

Read more: Revenue FAQ

Smart Home

A home equipped with connected devices such as smart locks, thermostats, and noise monitors. Smart technology streamlines self check-in and helps hosts manage the property remotely.

Read more: Amenities FAQ

Soft Furnishings

Textile-based items like curtains, pillows, rugs, and bedding. They add warmth, color, and comfort, and are the easiest items to swap during a refresh.

Read more: Furniture FAQ

Space Planning

The process of organizing a floor plan to make the best use of each area. For STRs, thoughtful space planning can add beds, improve flow, and raise the number of guests a property can host.

Read more: Space Planning FAQ

Staging

Arranging furniture and accessories to present a space attractively. In short-term rentals, staging is both a photo tool and an ongoing reset standard after each guest.

Read more: Photography FAQ

Statement Piece

A bold, eye-catching item such as a sculptural chandelier or oversized art. A well-chosen statement piece gives an Airbnb a signature moment that guests photograph and share.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Style Guide

A reference document defining a property's colors, materials, and decor rules. It helps owners and housekeepers keep the look consistent when replacing or restocking items.

Read more: Design Process FAQ

Superhost

An Airbnb status awarded to hosts who meet high standards for ratings, response rate, and reliability. Great design and guest experience support the strong reviews needed to earn and keep it.

Read more: Guest Experience FAQ

T

Task Lighting

Focused illumination for specific activities such as reading, cooking, or applying makeup. Adding task lighting at bedsides, desks, and kitchens makes a rental more functional for guests.

Read more: Lighting FAQ

Texturing

The use of varied surfaces like wood, linen, wool, and metal to add visual interest. Mixed textures make rooms feel richer and more inviting without adding color.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Theme Design

A design built around a central idea, such as a location, era, or activity. A restrained theme gives an STR a clear identity that helps it stand out in search results.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Throw Pillows

Decorative cushions placed on sofas, beds, and chairs. They are an affordable way to add color, comfort, and seasonal updates to a rental.

Read more: Furniture FAQ

Traffic Flow

The way people move through a space from entry to each room. Clear traffic flow prevents bottlenecks and makes a rental feel spacious even when it is full of guests.

Read more: Space Planning FAQ

Transitional Style

A blend of traditional and contemporary elements that feels timeless and balanced. It appeals to a broad range of guests, making it a safe and elegant choice for many STRs.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Turnover

The process of cleaning and resetting a property between guests. Efficient turnover depends on design that is easy to clean and clearly organized.

Read more: Cleaning and Maintenance FAQ

Turnover Kit

A prepared set of supplies and linens used by cleaners for each changeover. Having kits ready speeds up turnover and ensures every stay starts with the same standard.

Read more: Cleaning and Maintenance FAQ

U

Upholstery

The fabric, padding, and springs that cover sofas and chairs. Choosing durable, cleanable upholstery is one of the most important material decisions in a rental.

Read more: Furniture FAQ

Urban Chic

A sleek, city-inspired style mixing industrial details, modern lines, and refined accents. It fits downtown condos and lofts where guests expect a stylish, contemporary stay.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Utility Area

A functional space for laundry, storage, and supplies. A tidy utility area gives cleaners quick access to what they need and keeps clutter out of guest spaces.

Read more: Space Planning FAQ

V

Vacation Rental

A furnished property rented to travelers for short stays instead of long-term leases. Success in this category depends on creating an experience that feels better than a hotel.

Read more: Revenue FAQ

Value Engineering

Adjusting selections to lower cost without sacrificing quality or look. It helps STR owners stretch a furnishing budget and still deliver a polished result.

Read more: Pricing FAQ

Vignette

A small, styled grouping of objects, such as a tray with books and a plant. Vignettes add personality to shelves and tables and give photographers appealing detail shots.

Read more: Design Styles FAQ

Virtual Design

Design work done through digital tools like renderings, 3D models, and online mood boards. It lets owners visualize an Airbnb before buying a single item.

Read more: Remote Design FAQ

Visual Merchandising

The art of arranging items to attract attention and guide the eye. Applied to STRs, it ensures each room is styled to look inviting in photos and in person.

Read more: Photography FAQ

W

Wall Treatment

Any finish applied to walls, including paint, wallpaper, paneling, or plaster. Scrubbable and durable wall treatments are ideal for rentals that see constant use.

Read more: Materials FAQ

Wayfinding

Signs, labels, and cues that help guests find their way around a property. Simple wayfinding, like labeled switches or parking signs, reduces confusion and guest messages.

Read more: Guest Experience FAQ

Welcome Kit

A small collection of items, such as snacks, local goods, or a handwritten note, left for arriving guests. It creates a memorable first impression and often earns glowing review mentions.

Window Treatment

Curtains, blinds, or shades used to control light and privacy. Blackout options are especially valued by STR guests for sleeping well in unfamiliar places.

Work-from-Home Setup

A dedicated workspace with a desk, comfortable chair, and reliable internet. Remote workers increasingly filter for this amenity, making it a strong lever for longer stays.

X

Xenial

An adjective meaning hospitable, especially toward guests and strangers. It captures the core principle of STR design, which is creating a space that makes every visitor feel welcome.

Read more: Guest Experience FAQ

Y

Yield Management

The practice of adjusting prices based on demand, season, and events to maximize revenue. Strong design raises the ceiling on what guests will pay when demand is high.

Read more: Revenue FAQ

Z

Zoning

In interior design, dividing a room into distinct areas for sleeping, working, or lounging. Zoning helps open-plan and studio STRs feel organized and functional for multiple guests.

Local laws that control whether and how a property can be used as a short-term rental. Owners should check zoning rules before investing in design, since they can limit guest counts and permitted use.

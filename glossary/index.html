Short-Term Rental Design Glossary

121 terms every Airbnb and VRBO host should know, from ADR to Zoning.

By MyBnBDesign Team
ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ

A

Accent Lighting
Directional light used to highlight art, architecture, or decor. It adds depth and warmth in listing photos and evening stays.
Read more on the blog →
Accent Wall
A single wall finished in a contrasting color, texture, or material to create a focal point. In rentals it is a low-cost way to add personality and a photogenic backdrop.
Read more on the blog →
Accessibility Design
Design choices that make a property usable for guests with mobility, vision, or hearing needs, such as step-free entries and wider doorways. It can broaden your guest pool and improve inclusivity.
Read more on the blog →
ADR (Average Daily Rate)
The average revenue earned per booked night, calculated by dividing total rental revenue by nights sold. Design upgrades that improve perceived value are a common way to lift ADR.
Read more on the blog →
A-Frame
A cabin style with steeply angled roof planes that form a triangle, often with a large glass gable. It is popular with nature-focused guests and photographs exceptionally well.
Read more on the blog →
Amenity
Any feature a guest can use that adds value to their stay, such as a hot tub, game room, or coffee bar. Amenities are filterable on booking platforms and often justify higher rates.
Read more on the blog →
Appliance Package
The set of kitchen and laundry appliances specified for a property. Choosing reliable, easy-to-clean models reduces maintenance calls and guest complaints.
Read more on the blog →
Art Deco
A style marked by geometric patterns, bold symmetry, and luxe materials like brass and velvet. In STR design it suits glamorous city apartments and boutique stays.
Read more on the blog →
Artwork Sourcing
The process of finding and selecting art that fits a property's style and budget. Local and original pieces help a listing feel distinctive.
Read more on the blog →

B

Backsplash
The protective surface behind a sink or cooktop, often tile. A durable, easy-to-clean backsplash keeps kitchens looking fresh between guests.
Read more on the blog →
Bedding Layering
Building a bed with multiple textiles such as a duvet, coverlet, and accent pillows. Layering signals hotel-level comfort in photos and in person.
Read more on the blog →
Before-and-After Photography
Paired images showing a property pre- and post-design. They are useful for marketing, owner reporting, and demonstrating the impact of a redesign.
Read more on the blog →
Biophilic Design
An approach that brings natural materials, plants, and light into interiors. Guests tend to find these spaces calming, which supports positive reviews.
Read more on the blog →
Bohemian Style
A relaxed, layered aesthetic with global textiles, plants, and warm earthy tones. It works well in beach, desert, and creative-community markets.
Read more on the blog →
Booking Window
The amount of time between when a guest books and when they arrive. Strong design and photos can extend the booking window by driving earlier reservations.
Read more on the blog →
Bunk Room
A dedicated sleeping room with built-in or stacked beds, designed for kids or groups. It raises capacity and appeals to families and large parties.
Read more on the blog →

C

Capacity
The maximum number of guests a property can host comfortably and legally. Smart sleeping layouts increase capacity without crowding.
Read more on the blog →
Capsule Collection
A small, cohesive set of furniture and decor repeated across rooms or units. It simplifies sourcing and creates a consistent brand look.
Read more on the blog →
Channel Manager
Software that syncs calendars, rates, and listings across multiple booking platforms. It prevents double bookings and saves hosts time.
Read more on the blog →
Click-Through Rate (CTR)
The percentage of people who click on your listing after seeing it in search. Hero photos and titles are the biggest levers on CTR.
Read more on the blog →
Coastal Style
A light, breezy aesthetic using whites, blues, and natural textures such as rattan and linen. It is the go-to look for beach houses and lakeside homes.
Read more on the blog →
Color Psychology
The study of how colors influence mood and behavior. Hosts use it to make bedrooms restful and social spaces energetic.
Read more on the blog →
Commercial-Grade Furniture
Furniture built for heavy, repeated use, such as in hotels. It costs more up front but lasts longer in a high-turnover rental.
Read more on the blog →
Competitive Set
The group of nearby listings that compete with yours for the same guests. Reviewing your comp set reveals design and amenity gaps.
Read more on the blog →
Container Home
A dwelling built from repurposed shipping containers. It offers a distinctive industrial look that attracts adventurous guests.
Read more on the blog →
Conversion Rate
The share of listing views that turn into bookings. Photos, pricing, reviews, and design all influence it.
Read more on the blog →
Cost Per Night
The all-in expense to operate a booked night, including cleaning, supplies, and wear. Durable design lowers this over time.
Read more on the blog →
Curb Appeal
How attractive a property looks from the street. A strong first impression sets guest expectations before they walk in.
Read more on the blog →

D

Design Brief
A short document capturing goals, guest profile, style direction, and budget. It guides every decision during a project.
Read more on the blog →
Design ROI
The return generated by a design investment, measured in higher revenue, occupancy, or review scores relative to cost. Tracking it helps owners prioritize spending.
Read more on the blog →
Dynamic Pricing
Adjusting nightly rates based on demand, seasonality, and events. Strong visuals support higher price points during peak periods.
Read more on the blog →

E

Eclectic Style
A curated mix of eras, textures, and colors held together by a common thread. Done well, it feels collected and memorable.
Read more on the blog →
Egress Window
A window large enough to serve as an emergency exit from a sleeping room. It is often required for legal bedrooms.
Read more on the blog →
Entryway Design
The planning of a home's arrival zone, including storage, seating, and lighting. It shapes first impressions and keeps clutter under control.
Read more on the blog →

F

Farmhouse Style
A warm look built on natural wood, neutral palettes, and practical details. Modern versions avoid cliches and lean into clean lines.
Read more on the blog →
FF&E
Furniture, fixtures, and equipment, the movable assets used to furnish a property. Hosts track FF&E for budgeting and tax planning.
Read more on the blog →
Fire Pit Area
An outdoor seating zone built around a fire feature. It is a high-appeal amenity that encourages longer stays and strong reviews.
Read more on the blog →
Floor Plan
A scaled drawing of a property's layout. Including one in your listing helps guests understand sleeping arrangements.
Read more on the blog →
Focal Point
The element that draws the eye first in a room, such as a view, fireplace, or large artwork. Strong focal points improve both photos and guest impressions.
Read more on the blog →
Furniture Budget Tier
A pricing level used to plan purchases, such as $5K, $25K, or $100K. Each tier trades off quality, quantity, and customization.
Read more on the blog →

G

Game Room
A space with a pool table, arcade, or board games for entertainment. It is a signature amenity for group-oriented rentals.
Read more on the blog →
Golden Hour
The hour after sunrise or before sunset when light is soft and warm. Exterior photos often look best then.
Read more on the blog →
Gross Booking Value
The total amount a guest pays for a stay before fees and expenses. It is a key metric for tracking revenue performance.
Read more on the blog →
Guest Persona
A profile of your ideal guest, including travel party, preferences, and spending. Design decisions align to persona to boost reviews.
Read more on the blog →
Guidebook
A digital or printed home manual with local tips and house rules. A polished guidebook reinforces the quality of the stay.
Read more on the blog →

H

Hero Shot
The lead photo that appears first in search results and on the listing. It should show the property's strongest feature.
Read more on the blog →
High-Traffic Fabric
Upholstery rated for frequent use and cleaning, often performance weave or treated materials. It resists stains and wear in rentals.
Read more on the blog →
Home Theater
A dedicated media room with large screen, seating, and sound. It adds entertainment value for families and groups.
Read more on the blog →
Hospitality-Grade
Products built or specified to hotel standards. They handle repeated laundering and use better than residential versions.
Read more on the blog →
Hot Tub
A heated outdoor soaking tub and a top-requested amenity. Placement, privacy, and styling affect its appeal.
Read more on the blog →

I

Industrial Style
A look featuring exposed brick, metal, and raw wood. It suits lofts, warehouses, and urban rentals.
Read more on the blog →
Instagrammable Moment
A visually striking spot designed to be photographed and shared. It generates free marketing through guest posts.
Read more on the blog →

J

Japandi
A hybrid of Japanese minimalism and Scandinavian warmth. It emphasizes calm, natural materials, and restrained palettes.
Read more on the blog →

K

Key Lockbox
A secure box that holds property keys for self check-in. It is a common feature in remote-managed rentals.
Read more on the blog →
Kitchen Triangle
The spatial relationship between sink, stove, and refrigerator. An efficient layout makes cooking easy for guests.
Read more on the blog →

L

Layering
Combining textures, lighting, and accessories to create depth. It makes spaces feel warm and finished rather than staged.
Read more on the blog →
Lead Time
The time between ordering a product and receiving it. Planning for lead times keeps launch dates on track.
Read more on the blog →
Lifestyle Photography
Images showing people or props in use, such as a coffee set on a table. It helps guests imagine their stay.
Read more on the blog →
Listing Description
The written portion of your platform listing that explains the property and stay. It should match photos and highlight design features.
Read more on the blog →
Listing Title
The headline guests see in search. Concise, specific titles that name standout features improve click-through.
Read more on the blog →
Living Design
A design approach that makes a home feel lived-in and personal while remaining durable for guests. It contrasts with sterile staging.
Read more on the blog →
Luxury Rental
A high-end STR with premium finishes, services, and amenities. It commands top rates and demands exceptional detail.
Read more on the blog →

M

Material Palette
The curated set of woods, metals, fabrics, and finishes used throughout a home. A tight palette creates a cohesive look.
Read more on the blog →
Mediterranean Style
A look inspired by southern European homes, with terracotta, arches, and warm tones. It fits coastal and vineyard properties.
Read more on the blog →
Mid-Century Modern
A design style from the mid-1900s with clean lines, organic shapes, and warm woods. It remains popular in desert and urban markets.
Read more on the blog →
Minimalist Style
A pared-back approach that favors simplicity and open space. It photographs cleanly but must remain warm to feel inviting.
Read more on the blog →
Mood Board
A collage of colors, textures, and images that communicates a design direction. It aligns owner and designer before purchases.
Read more on the blog →
Mudroom
A transitional space for coats, boots, and gear. It is essential in ski, lake, and mountain properties.
Read more on the blog →

N

Natural Light
Daylight entering a space through windows and doors. It is the foundation of strong listing photography.
Read more on the blog →
Neutral Palette
A color scheme of whites, creams, grays, and tans. It provides a flexible backdrop that appeals to broad audiences.
Read more on the blog →

O

Occupancy Rate
The percentage of available nights that are booked. Design and photos help drive occupancy in slower seasons.
Read more on the blog →
Occupancy Tax
A lodging tax collected on short-term stays. Hosts need to understand local rules when pricing.
Read more on the blog →
Outdoor Living
Designed exterior spaces for dining, lounging, and recreation. Guests increasingly book properties for outdoor amenities.
Read more on the blog →
Overhead Lighting
Ceiling fixtures that provide general illumination. Pairing them with lamps creates layered, flattering light.
Read more on the blog →

P

Performance Fabric
A textile engineered to resist stains, fading, and abrasion. It is a staple of rental upholstery.
Read more on the blog →
Photo Staging
Arranging furniture and accessories specifically for a photo shoot. It highlights the best features and removes distractions.
Read more on the blog →
Pool Area
The decked space surrounding a swimming pool, including seating and shade. A well-styled pool area is a major booking driver.
Read more on the blog →
Primary Suite
The main bedroom, often with an en suite bathroom and sitting area. It anchors the guest experience in larger homes.
Read more on the blog →
Property Management Software
A tool that handles bookings, messaging, and operations. It works alongside design to deliver a smooth guest experience.
Read more on the blog →
Pull-Out Sofa
A sofa with a hidden bed that adds sleeping capacity. Quality mattresses are key to avoiding bad reviews.
Read more on the blog →

Q

Quiet Hours
Rules limiting noise during certain times. Design choices like rugs and soft furnishings help reduce sound.
Read more on the blog →

R

Ranch Property
A rural property with open land, often with rustic design. It appeals to guests seeking space and quiet.
Read more on the blog →
Refresh
A partial update of a property that replaces key items without a full redesign. It keeps the listing current at modest cost.
Read more on the blog →
Repeat Guest
A guest who books the same property again. Memorable design and comfort build loyalty and lower marketing costs.
Read more on the blog →
Review Score
The numerical rating guests leave after a stay. Cleanliness, accuracy, and comfort scores are heavily influenced by design.
Read more on the blog →
RevPAR
Revenue per available rental night, combining rate and occupancy. It measures overall revenue efficiency.
Read more on the blog →
Rug Sizing
Choosing the right rug dimensions for a room's furniture layout. Proper scale grounds a space and elevates the look.
Read more on the blog →

S

Scandinavian Style
A bright, functional aesthetic with light woods, white walls, and cozy textiles. It suits compact and cold-climate rentals.
Read more on the blog →
Seasonal Styling
Swapping soft goods and decor to match the time of year. It keeps photos and the guest experience fresh.
Read more on the blog →
Self Check-In
A process allowing guests to enter without meeting the host. Clear signage and an organized entry improve the experience.
Read more on the blog →
Shoppable List
A product-by-product list with links, quantities, and prices. It makes furnishing projects clear and trackable.
Read more on the blog →
Short-Term Rental (STR)
A property rented for stays typically under 30 days. STRs require durable, guest-oriented design.
Read more on the blog →
Sleeping Arrangement
The configuration of beds across a property. Clear arrangements set guest expectations and reduce confusion.
Read more on the blog →
Smartphone Photography
Capturing listing photos using a phone camera. With good light and composition, it can produce solid results on a budget.
Read more on the blog →
Soft Goods
Textiles such as bedding, pillows, curtains, and throws. They are the fastest and cheapest way to change a room's feel.
Read more on the blog →
Staging
Preparing a space visually for photos or showings. It focuses on composition and removing clutter.
Read more on the blog →
Superhost
An Airbnb status awarded to hosts who meet high standards for ratings and responsiveness. Design supports it through consistent guest satisfaction.
Read more on the blog →

T

Task Lighting
Focused light for activities such as reading or cooking. It improves function and guest comfort.
Read more on the blog →
Texture Layering
Mixing materials like wool, linen, wood, and metal. It adds richness and avoids a flat look.
Read more on the blog →
Tiny Home
A compact dwelling often under 400 square feet. Smart storage and multipurpose furniture are essential.
Read more on the blog →
Transitional Style
A blend of traditional and contemporary elements with neutral tones. It appeals to a wide range of guests.
Read more on the blog →
Treehouse Rental
An elevated cabin built among trees, popular as a unique stay. Design focuses on views, compact comfort, and safety.
Read more on the blog →
Turnover
The cleaning and resetting of a property between guests. Easy-care design shortens turnover time.
Read more on the blog →

U

Unique Stay
A non-traditional property such as a yurt, dome, or treehouse. Distinctive design is central to its appeal.
Read more on the blog →
Upholstery
The fabric or leather covering on seating. Choosing durable, cleanable upholstery cuts replacement costs.

V

Vacancy
Nights when a property is empty and unbooked. Design and pricing strategies aim to reduce it.

Value Engineering
Adjusting product choices to hit a budget without hurting the guest experience. It prioritizes spending on high-impact items.

Vertical Styling
Using height through tall plants, art, and shelving to add interest. It draws the eye upward and makes rooms feel larger.

Video Tour
A walk-through video of a property used in listings and social media. It builds trust and reduces booking uncertainty.

Vignette
A small styled grouping of objects, like a tray and candle on a console. Vignettes add polish and make photos feel intentional.

Virtual Staging

VRBO

W

Showplace review: services and owner checklist

Use this Showplace review to assess the service scope and the evidence you should request for your own rental. MyBnBDesign publishes this page and may compete for the same work. It is an editorial service review, not an independent customer review, a star rating or a report of a completed purchase.

What the published information establishes

Showplace presents rental design and furnishing, digital room visualization and property-planning services.

Source checked October 13, 2026: Showplace official service information. Provider descriptions establish advertised scope, not independently verified outcomes. Current availability and the signed proposal control your project.

The decision to resolve first

Turn the visualization into a measurable installation plan. Request a dimensioned layout, item specification list, delivery responsibility and the specific on-site setup option for the address.

Download this page’s decision worksheet (CSV). Record the actual proposal and evidence; the example below is hypothetical.

Worked owner scenario

An owner likes a rendering of an eight-person dining room. Test the actual table size, pulled-out chair space and route to the patio before approving purchases; the attractive view alone cannot establish circulation. This is an illustrative planning situation, not a case study or a claim about this provider’s execution.

Build an apples-to-apples proposal

Start with the same measured floor plan, photographs, retained-item inventory and target guest brief for every candidate. List required rooms and include the outdoor areas only if they are part of this assignment. State the desired finished condition: a plan for your team to execute, assembled furniture, or a fully stocked and checked rental.

Request separate lines for design, furnishings, sales tax, freight, receiving, storage, assembly, mounting, trades, household essentials and final styling. An omitted line is an unanswered question, not a zero-cost service. Compare totals only after assigning excluded work to someone and including their estimate.

Ask how the proposal handles unavailable products and damaged deliveries. Identify who approves substitutions, who pays for changes and which dates depend on owner decisions or site readiness. Keep the approved product schedule and plan version with the contract so the team can distinguish a necessary change from accidental drift.

Check the room before accepting the project

Walk through the actual guest routine after installation. Arrive with luggage, find the lighting controls, open storage, sit at the dining table and use the workspace. Compare the result with the approved room plan and record unresolved issues with photographs. Use the manufacturer’s instructions and appropriately qualified installers for installation requirements.

Then run a turnover rehearsal with the cleaning team. Check whether furniture can be cleaned around, whether supplies have a clear location and whether decorative items return to an unambiguous position. Make the final punch list specific: item, room, problem, responsible person and agreed completion date. A photo-ready room still needs these operating checks.

How to evaluate reviews and performance claims

Read customer feedback for the actual service tier and project type you are considering. Note whether the customer used virtual planning, furnishing installation or renovation support. A review of a different assignment may explain communication style but cannot establish that your required services are included.

For a revenue claim, request the comparison period, property changes, operating assumptions and what is actually measured. Design, pricing, management, season and availability can change together. A portfolio photograph or a provider’s advertised result is useful context, but it does not predict your property’s future performance.

Keep furnishing and improvement records usable

Keep itemized invoices, room locations, installation descriptions, payment records and completion dates together. Separate retained furnishings from new purchases and keep change orders with the original scope. This makes the design handoff easier to reconcile with the property’s financial records.

For a separate discussion of the owner’s tax planning, see AE Tax Advisors. For property-component records and study preparation, see Stratum’s STR cost segregation resources.

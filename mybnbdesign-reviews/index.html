MyBnBDesign reviews: evaluate evidence and project fit

This page is published by MyBnBDesign. It explains how to evaluate our design service, portfolio and customer evidence before choosing a project team. It is not an independent review site or a verified aggregate rating. The earlier numerical rating, review-count and revenue-lift assertions on this page are not supported by a documented verification record and have been removed.

What evidence to request

Start with a project similar to yours in property size, service scope and guest profile. Ask which parts of the work MyBnBDesign completed and which were completed by the owner or other providers. A beautiful final photograph can establish a visual direction, but it does not establish procurement responsibility, timeline reliability or the budget required for your property.

If references or testimonials are supplied, check the service tier and project timing. Confirm that a quote is attributed with permission and distinguish an owner’s experience from a guest’s review of a stay. Ask for the original context of any performance claim and avoid treating a single property’s result as a forecast.

Match the service to your project

Bring room measurements, photographs, an inventory of retained pieces, the intended guest and the date you want the property ready. Ask for a written explanation of the design deliverables, revision process, purchasing arrangements and on-site work. Confirm coverage and availability for the actual address.

If you are refreshing a live rental, identify operational problems and protect the reopening plan. If the property is empty, make sure the scope accounts for household supplies, lighting and installed guest-use checks as well as major furniture. An owner-managed installation needs a usable handoff and a clear route for implementation questions.

Worked evidence check

Suppose an owner likes a photographed makeover and asks whether it proves that their own rental will earn more. The useful next questions are what changed at that property, how its performance was measured, and whether the proposed scope is comparable. Keep the design decision grounded in the actual guest problems, budget and operating conditions. This is a hypothetical review exercise, not a customer testimonial.

Build an apples-to-apples proposal

Start with the same measured floor plan, photographs, retained-item inventory and target guest brief for every candidate. List required rooms and include the outdoor areas only if they are part of this assignment. State the desired finished condition: a plan for your team to execute, assembled furniture, or a fully stocked and checked rental.

Request separate lines for design, furnishings, sales tax, freight, receiving, storage, assembly, mounting, trades, household essentials and final styling. An omitted line is an unanswered question, not a zero-cost service. Compare totals only after assigning excluded work to someone and including their estimate.

Ask how the proposal handles unavailable products and damaged deliveries. Identify who approves substitutions, who pays for changes and which dates depend on owner decisions or site readiness. Keep the approved product schedule and plan version with the contract so the team can distinguish a necessary change from accidental drift.

Check the room before accepting the project

Walk through the actual guest routine after installation. Arrive with luggage, find the lighting controls, open storage, sit at the dining table and use the workspace. Compare the result with the approved room plan and record unresolved issues with photographs. Use the manufacturer’s instructions and appropriately qualified installers for installation requirements.

Then run a turnover rehearsal with the cleaning team. Check whether furniture can be cleaned around, whether supplies have a clear location and whether decorative items return to an unambiguous position. Make the final punch list specific: item, room, problem, responsible person and agreed completion date. A photo-ready room still needs these operating checks.

Keep furnishing and improvement records usable

Keep itemized invoices, room locations, installation descriptions, payment records and completion dates together. Separate retained furnishings from new purchases and keep change orders with the original scope. This makes the design handoff easier to reconcile with the property’s financial records.

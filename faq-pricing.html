Comparing STR design quotes and fees FAQ

Use these answers to prepare a clear rental-design decision. The scope is comparing str design quotes and fees faq, with practical evidence to gather from your actual property and proposal. Prices, timelines and tax treatment are not universal across projects.

How can two design fees be compared?

Give both candidates the same property information and required deliverables. Compare what the fee includes: design work, revisions, purchasing support, site visits, installation coordination and closeout. A lower fee may cover fewer tasks. Assign excluded work to someone and include its cost before comparing complete project totals.

Which line items should be separate?

Request visible amounts or allowances for design, goods, tax, freight, receiving, storage, assembly, mounting, trades, household essentials and final styling as applicable. An omitted line remains an unanswered question. Keep estimates, allowances and fixed commitments distinguishable so an uncertain amount does not become an assumed cap.

How should purchasing arrangements be described?

Ask who places orders, whose account is used, who pays vendors and who handles returns or damaged deliveries. Clarify any procurement fee or markup in the actual agreement. Keep product selections and approval records with the proposal so the owner can reconcile the goods received with the intended inventory.

What should a change-order price show?

Describe the original scope, requested change, affected products or work, additional cost or credit, and effect on timing. Identify who must approve it before commitment. A conversation about a possible change should not be confused with authorization to buy or an accepted revised delivery date.

Can a headline furnishing budget be used as a quote?

Only if the provider confirms that it covers your actual property and scope in writing. Published examples may involve different rooms, retained items, labor or delivery arrangements. Request the complete inventory and exclusions rather than extrapolating from an entry package or a selected project photograph.

How do I document the final total?

Reconcile approved design and goods invoices, delivery and installation work, changes, credits and unresolved claims. Keep the final inventory and completion records with that reconciliation. Professional financial review can then use an evidence-based record of the actual project instead of a single headline budget.

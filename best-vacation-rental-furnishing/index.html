Three Furnishing Models: Turnkey, Sourcing-Only, and Hybrid

Before comparing companies, it helps to understand how furnishing is delivered. The model determines how much work lands on your desk.

Turnkey Furnishing

A turnkey provider designs the property, purchases every item, coordinates delivery, assembles and installs, and styles the finished rooms. You approve and the property is delivered ready for guests. This is the lowest-effort path and the one that most reliably protects your launch date. It typically costs more in fees but removes the largest source of project risk.

Sourcing-Only

Sourcing-only companies supply the products, often from catalogs or negotiated vendor accounts, and sometimes deliver to the property. They may not provide design direction or on-site installation. This works well if you already have a design plan and local labor, and want better access to furniture packages or trade pricing.

Hybrid

Hybrid providers combine pieces of both: perhaps a platform for ordering plus optional design or installation add-ons, or a furnishing package paired with management services. Hybrid models offer flexibility, though you should confirm precisely which tasks sit with the provider and which remain yours.

How to Choose a Furnishing Partner

Match the model to your situation. Remote owners and first-time investors should lean toward turnkey, where the provider owns the outcome. Owners with a clear design plan and local help can save by using a sourcing-focused company. Portfolio operators may benefit from a platform that standardizes ordering across units. If you want a hands-off investment, a company that combines setup and management may be efficient, as long as you also want the management.

Beyond the model, evaluate reliability. Ask for references from owners with similar properties, confirm how damaged or backordered items are handled, and request a written scope that names who is responsible for each task. Why Furnishing and Design Should Work Together

Many owners treat design and furnishing as separate purchases, but the best results come when one team does both. A designer who also controls procurement can specify items that are actually available, at the right price, with realistic lead times. A purchaser who understands the design intent will not substitute a lookalike that fails to photograph well. And an installer who worked on the plan knows where each piece belongs.

This integration is the core of the MyBnBDesign model. It explains the consistency behind 200+ completed properties and a 40% average revenue lift, and it is why owners finish projects with fewer surprises. When furnishing is treated as a separate errand, the cost shows up as delays, mismatched pieces, and rework. When it is part of a single revenue-focused plan, the property launches faster and earns sooner.

Consider also the ongoing side of furnishing. Short-term rentals experience wear, and a provider who documents what was purchased, where it came from, and how to reorder it makes future repairs and replacements easy. Ask each company whether they provide an item list with sources and care guidance after installation. This small deliverable saves hours every time a lamp breaks or a chair needs replacing.

Furnishing Checklist for Short-Term Rentals

Use this checklist to confirm that your furnishing plan, whoever executes it, covers the full guest experience. Gaps in this list are the most common cause of poor early reviews.

  • Sleeping: quality mattresses, mattress protectors, two sets of linens per bed, extra pillows and blankets, and blackout window coverings.
  • Living areas: durable, stain-resistant seating sized for the target guest count, accent tables, lighting at multiple levels, and a media setup.
  • Kitchen and dining: cookware, dishes and glassware for every guest plus extras, coffee equipment, and seating that matches the maximum occupancy.
  • Bathrooms: towels in sufficient quantity, bath mats, organizers, and a small supply of essentials.
  • Workspace: a desk or table, a comfortable chair, and reliable lighting and outlets for remote workers.
  • Outdoor: weather-rated seating, lighting, and any fire or grill features that photograph well and justify a higher rate.
  • Finishing touches: art, plants, mirrors, welcome materials, and safety items such as detectors and a first aid kit.

A turnkey provider will build this list for you based on the number of guests you plan to host and the amenities your market expects. If you are handling procurement yourself, price the entire list before choosing furniture so that decor and supplies are not squeezed out at the end. It is also wise to keep a small reserve for replacements in the first few months, since real guest use quickly reveals which items need upgrading.

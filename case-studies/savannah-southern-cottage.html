Savannah Cottage: From Zero to Superhost in 90 Days

By MyBnBDesign Team | Published October 2, 2026 | Savannah, GA | Southern charm

A $20K Southern charm redesign launched a new Savannah 3BR/2BA cottage at $310 ADR, 80% occupancy and Superhost status within 90 days.

Property Overview

This three-bedroom, two-bath cottage sits a short walk from Savannah's famed squares, with live oaks, wrought-iron fences, and a private garden courtyard. The owner purchased it for $340,000 as a brand new short-term rental with no prior booking history, meaning there were no reviews, no ranking, and no baseline revenue.

A new listing faces an uphill climb. Without social proof, guests are hesitant to book at premium rates, and algorithms favor listings with a track record. The owner engaged MyBnBDesign before launch with a bold goal: reach Superhost status quickly by delivering a standout first impression.

The Design Challenge

The cottage was a blank canvas, which sounds easy but carries its own risks. Without existing furniture or a design direction, the owner could easily have defaulted to a generic package that blended into the market.

With no reviews to lean on, photography and the first guest experience had to carry the listing. Every detail needed to read as premium from the first photo through checkout.

The courtyard garden was overgrown and unfurnished, yet it represented the strongest differentiator in a city where many historic rentals have no private outdoor space.

Before MyBnBDesign began, our team reviewed the competing listings in Savannah, studied the guest reviews of the top-ranked homes, and mapped the amenities that drive search filters in that market. That research told us exactly where the property was falling short and which investments would earn the most per dollar. If you are weighing a similar project, our design services follow the same research-first process.

The Design Solution

Our approach for this 3BR/2BA property centered on southern charm style with a clear focus on garden courtyard. Every choice below was made to improve three things at once: how the home photographs, how it feels to stay in, and how efficiently it can be turned over between guests.

Living room: collected Southern charm

Soft blue-gray walls, a camelback sofa in cream, mixed antique and new side tables, and a layered rug scheme create a lived-in elegance. A gallery wall of vintage Savannah maps and botanical prints ties the room to place.

Kitchen and breakfast nook

Open shelving with white dishware, a farmhouse sink, and a breakfast nook with a cushioned banquette make mornings special. A welcome basket with local pralines and coffee begins the stay with delight.

Primary bedroom

A four-poster king bed, crisp white linens, and floral drapes bring a Southern romance. A cushioned bench, soft lamps, and a plush rug add comfort.

Guest bedrooms

One room has a queen with a blue-and-white palette, and the other has twin beds styled with matching quilts. Each has a luggage rack, mirror, and a small bouquet.

Garden courtyard

We cleared and replanted with hydrangeas, ferns, and jasmine, laid pea gravel paths, and furnished a cafe table set, a lounge area, and a fountain. Warm string lights make it magical at night.

Hospitality systems

A digital guidebook, local restaurant list, and a printed welcome card with golden tips for exploring the squares set expectations. Quick-response messaging and a pre-arrival checklist protected the review score.

Our Process and Timeline

Every MyBnBDesign project follows a repeatable sequence. We begin with a market and guest analysis, then produce a design concept with a room-by-room furnishing plan and a line-item budget. After the owner approves the plan, we handle sourcing, delivery coordination, installation, and styling, finishing with a professional photo-ready staging day. For this Savannah property the full design budget was $20,000, and the property was guest-ready in a matter of weeks rather than months.

Budget discipline mattered as much as aesthetics. We allocated the largest share of spending to the spaces that guests photograph and discuss in reviews, namely the main living area, the beds, and the signature amenity, while choosing durable, easy-to-replace items elsewhere. You can see how this approach plays out across other markets in our portfolio.

Before and After: The Numbers

Because this was a new listing, there was no prior revenue to compare against. Instead, we benchmarked against comparable Savannah cottages that had been operating for a year or more. Those competitors averaged materially lower rates and fewer five-star reviews. After launch, the cottage reached a $310 ADR, 80% occupancy, and $7,400 in monthly net income, and it earned Superhost status within 90 days.

Return on the design investment

The design budget of $20,000 equals roughly 2.7 months of post-design net income, and it represents 5.9% of the $340,000 purchase price. Put differently, every dollar spent on design supports about 4.4 dollars of annualized net income at the current run rate. These figures reflect the owner's reported results and will vary by market, season, management, and pricing strategy, so they should be treated as an example rather than a guarantee.

The rate increase is also durable for reasons beyond the initial novelty. Better photos raise click-through, better stays produce better reviews, and better reviews lift search ranking and justify further rate increases. That compounding loop is why design tends to outperform other upgrades on a per-dollar basis. For more on how we think about this, browse the MyBnBDesign blog.

Pricing, Operations, and Long-Term Performance

Design only pays off when pricing and operations support it. After the Savannah redesign, we recommended a rate strategy that anchored the base nightly rate to the new positioning rather than to the old comparables, with dynamic adjustments for weekends, holidays, and local events. Many owners are tempted to keep prices low after a redesign to protect occupancy. In this case the better move was to trust the new photos and reviews, test higher rates early, and let the calendar show how much demand the property could absorb.

Operationally, the finishes and furnishings were chosen for durability and fast turnover. Washable slipcovers, commercial-grade rugs, matching linen sets, and clearly labeled storage keep the cleaning team efficient and reduce replacement costs. We also provided a simple restocking list and a seasonal refresh plan so the home continues to look like its listing photos months and years after launch. A property that stays photo-accurate protects its review average, and the review average protects the nightly rate.

Finally, we recommend revisiting the design every 12 to 18 months. Small updates such as new throw pillows, refreshed art, and replaced linens cost little and keep the listing feeling current in a competitive Savannah market.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long did the Savannah project take?

From design approval to a guest-ready, photographed property, the project took a matter of weeks. Timelines depend on furniture lead times and the scope of any construction, which is why we place orders as early as possible after the plan is approved.

Was the $20,000 budget fixed?

Yes. We build a line-item budget before ordering anything and track spending against it. Where we found savings, we redirected them to the high-impact areas such as garden courtyard.

Can this approach work for a smaller or larger budget?

It can. We scale the plan by prioritizing the spaces that influence bookings most. A smaller budget concentrates on the main living area, the beds, and photography, while a larger budget extends the same standards to every room and the outdoor areas.

Do you guarantee these results?

No. These numbers describe one owner's experience in one market. We design to maximize the odds of a strong result, but revenue also depends on management, pricing, regulation, and demand.

Key Takeaways

  1. Launch with design, not discounts. A distinctive first impression allowed a premium launch rate.
  2. Prioritize the photos. Courtyard and living room styling drove the first bookings.
  3. Systemize hospitality. Guidebooks and messaging protected early reviews.
  4. Differentiate with private outdoor space. The courtyard was the strongest competitive advantage.
  5. Superhost status accelerates growth. The cottage reached 80 percent occupancy and $7,400 monthly net.

What This Means for Your Property

Not every home needs a $20,000 budget, and not every market behaves like Savannah. What transfers is the method: define the guest, identify the one or two experiences that win bookings in the market, design the whole property around them, and measure results against a clear baseline. Whether you own a 3BR/2BA home like this one or a studio near a city center, the same principles apply.

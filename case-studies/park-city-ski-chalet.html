Park City Chalet: $18K/Month Peak Season Revenue

By MyBnBDesign Team | Published October 2, 2026 | Park City, UT | Mountain luxury

A $45K mountain luxury redesign with apres-ski lounge and boot room lifted a Park City ski-in/ski-out chalet from $280 to $520 ADR and $18,200 peak monthly net.

Property Overview

Park City is one of the most lucrative ski markets in North America, and a ski-in/ski-out property sits at the very top of demand. This four-bedroom, three-and-a-half-bath chalet was purchased for $850,000 and offers direct access to the slopes, a stone fireplace, and sweeping views of the mountain.

Despite a premium location, the original design was dated and underperforming at $280 per night. The interior felt like a tired lodge from the 1990s, there was no dedicated space for ski gear, and the common areas did not support after-ski gatherings. The owner sought a luxury overhaul to compete with the resort's best.

The Design Challenge

Ski guests are among the highest-spending travelers, and they compare listings on details: gear storage, heated floors, hot tubs, and a place to gather. The chalet failed on most of these. Wet gear spilled into hallways, and the living area could not host a group after a day on the mountain.

The aesthetic was also a barrier. Peak-season bookings are made far in advance from photos, and the dated interior did not signal luxury. The home was priced like a mid-tier unit despite its ski-in/ski-out location.

Finally, the revenue profile is seasonal. Success meant maximizing peak weeks, which requires an offering that justifies top-of-market rates in a short window.

Before MyBnBDesign began, our team reviewed the competing listings in Park City, studied the guest reviews of the top-ranked homes, and mapped the amenities that drive search filters in that market. That research told us exactly where the property was falling short and which investments would earn the most per dollar. If you are weighing a similar project, our design services follow the same research-first process.

The Design Solution

Our approach for this 4BR/3.5BA property centered on mountain luxury style with a clear focus on apres-ski lounge and boot room. Every choice below was made to improve three things at once: how the home photographs, how it feels to stay in, and how efficiently it can be turned over between guests.

Great room: mountain luxury

Rough-hewn beams were lightened with a warm white, and a oversized sectional in charcoal wool, leather poufs, and a shearling throw collection fill the space. A massive stone fireplace and floor-to-ceiling glass dominate the room.

Apres-ski lounge

A dedicated lounge near the entry holds a built-in bar, banquette seating, a gas fireplace, and a large TV for watching race coverage. A beverage fridge and hot chocolate station make arrival feel like a ritual.

Boot room

Heated boot dryers, custom cubbies for each guest, bench seating, and floor drains keep wet gear organized. Guests mention this room in nearly every review.

Primary suite

A king bed with a leather headboard, cashmere throws, a gas fireplace, and a spa bathroom with a steam shower deliver five-star comfort. Heated floors are standard throughout.

Guest bedrooms and bunks

Three additional bedrooms offer queens and a bunk room with built-in beds and privacy curtains. Each features blackout shades and quality bedding in neutral tones.

Hot tub terrace

A deck with a six-person hot tub, fire table, and heated seating allows stargazing in snowfall. Snow-melt pavers prevent slipping.

Our Process and Timeline

Every MyBnBDesign project follows a repeatable sequence. We begin with a market and guest analysis, then produce a design concept with a room-by-room furnishing plan and a line-item budget. After the owner approves the plan, we handle sourcing, delivery coordination, installation, and styling, finishing with a professional photo-ready staging day. For this Park City property the full design budget was $45,000, and the property was guest-ready in a matter of weeks rather than months.

Budget discipline mattered as much as aesthetics. We allocated the largest share of spending to the spaces that guests photograph and discuss in reviews, namely the main living area, the beds, and the signature amenity, while choosing durable, easy-to-replace items elsewhere. You can see how this approach plays out across other markets in our portfolio.

Before and After: The Numbers

The average daily rate moved from $280 to $520, an increase of 86%. Occupancy settled at 90% peak season, and the property now produces $18,200 per month peak net. Because occupancy held up while the rate rose so sharply, the gain flows almost directly to the bottom line.

Return on the design investment

The design budget of $45,000 equals roughly 2.5 months of post-design net income during peak season, and it represents 5.3% of the $850,000 purchase price. Put differently, every dollar spent on design supports about 4.9 dollars of annualized net income at the current run rate. These figures reflect the owner's reported results and will vary by market, season, management, and pricing strategy, so they should be treated as an example rather than a guarantee.

The rate increase is also durable for reasons beyond the initial novelty. Better photos raise click-through, better stays produce better reviews, and better reviews lift search ranking and justify further rate increases. That compounding loop is why design tends to outperform other upgrades on a per-dollar basis. For more on how we think about this, browse the MyBnBDesign blog.

Pricing, Operations, and Long-Term Performance

Design only pays off when pricing and operations support it. After the Park City redesign, we recommended a rate strategy that anchored the base nightly rate to the new positioning rather than to the old comparables, with dynamic adjustments for weekends, holidays, and local events. Many owners are tempted to keep prices low after a redesign to protect occupancy. In this case the better move was to trust the new photos and reviews, test higher rates early, and let the calendar show how much demand the property could absorb.

Operationally, the finishes and furnishings were chosen for durability and fast turnover. Washable slipcovers, commercial-grade rugs, matching linen sets, and clearly labeled storage keep the cleaning team efficient and reduce replacement costs. We also provided a simple restocking list and a seasonal refresh plan so the home continues to look like its listing photos months and years after launch. A property that stays photo-accurate protects its review average, and the review average protects the nightly rate.

Finally, we recommend revisiting the design every 12 to 18 months. Small updates such as new throw pillows, refreshed art, and replaced linens cost little and keep the listing feeling current in a competitive Park City market.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long did the Park City project take?

From design approval to a guest-ready, photographed property, the project took a matter of weeks. Timelines depend on furniture lead times and the scope of any construction, which is why we place orders as early as possible after the plan is approved.

Was the $45,000 budget fixed?

Yes. We build a line-item budget before ordering anything and track spending against it. Where we found savings, we redirected them to the high-impact areas such as apres-ski lounge and boot room.

Can this approach work for a smaller or larger budget?

It can. We scale the plan by prioritizing the spaces that influence bookings most. A smaller budget concentrates on the main living area, the beds, and photography, while a larger budget extends the same standards to every room and the outdoor areas.

Do you guarantee these results?

No. These numbers describe one owner's experience in one market. We design to maximize the odds of a strong result, but revenue also depends on management, pricing, regulation, and demand.

Key Takeaways

  1. Solve the ski logistics. A boot room and heated storage delivered daily value and recurring praise.
  2. Create a gathering place. The apres-ski lounge transformed group stays.
  3. Match luxury to location. Design aligned the interior with the premium slope-side position.
  4. Optimize for peak weeks. Peak-season pricing delivered $18,200 monthly net.
  5. Larger investments can still pay back quickly. The $45,000 design is recouped in roughly two and a half peak months.

What This Means for Your Property

Not every home needs a $45,000 budget, and not every market behaves like Park City. What transfers is the method: define the guest, identify the one or two experiences that win bookings in the market, design the whole property around them, and measure results against a clear baseline. Whether you own a 4BR/3.5BA home like this one or a studio near a city center, the same principles apply.

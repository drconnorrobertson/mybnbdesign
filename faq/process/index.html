STR design project sequence and handoff FAQ

Use these answers to prepare a clear rental-design decision. The scope is str design project sequence and handoff faq, with practical evidence to gather from your actual property and proposal. Prices, timelines and tax treatment are not universal across projects.

What should happen before design concepts?

Prepare a measured property brief, photographs, retained inventory, intended guest and operating constraints. Identify unresolved building conditions and the responsible parties. The designer needs reliable information about the assignment before a concept can become a useful layout or product schedule.

When should furniture be ordered?

Order after the required measurements, product specifications, site conditions and approvals are resolved for the item. Confirm delivery and receiving arrangements. Some decisions may proceed while others remain open, but keep those dependencies explicit rather than treating the whole project as equally ready.

How do construction and furnishing schedules connect?

List the work that must be complete before delivery or installation, including access, protection and storage arrangements. Identify who confirms readiness. A vendor dispatch estimate is not the same as a confirmed receiving date, and a receiving date does not establish that the property is ready for assembly or styling.

What belongs in a remote design handoff?

Provide room plans, exact product variants, quantities, dimensions, care information, placement and unresolved interfaces. Name a local measurement verifier and execution lead. Include the substitution and question process so installers do not have to infer the plan from a visual board alone.

What should be checked before photography?

Close the essential punch list and repeat the agreed guest-use and turnover checks. Confirm that advertised furniture and amenities are present and usable. Photograph the completed arrangement guests will receive, and distinguish any planning renderings from actual property photographs.

How does the process continue after launch?

Observe operating reports, record condition and replacement issues, and review whether the design supports the intended routines. Keep updates tied to evidence. A refresh can then address a defined problem instead of restarting the entire design whenever a decorative trend or product availability changes.

Airbnb Interior Design Cost and Furnishing Pricing FAQ

Use this guide to build a budget for designing, furnishing and setting up your short-term rental. Start with the same room list for every quote and record who pays for each step.

How much does Airbnb interior design cost?

Budget for the design fee, furniture and decor, and the work required to get the rooms ready for guests. MyBnBDesign quotes furniture separately from the design fee. Your proposal shows whether procurement and installation are included in your scope. A whole-property launch usually requires a different budget from a refresh that keeps existing beds and seating.

For an illustrative two-bedroom quote comparison, a $12,000 furniture allowance, $2,500 design fee, $1,000 delivery allowance, and $1,500 installation allowance total $17,000 before any unlisted tax or reserve. Those figures are example inputs. Replace each one with the written amount for your property and check which costs are already included.

If the quote leaves receiving or household essentials open, carry those as unpriced lines in the budget. Ask the provider to confirm whether delivery includes room placement, whether the installation team removes packaging, and which items you need to buy before the first guest arrives. Add sales tax shown on the proposal and any separately quoted photography before setting your launch budget.

See Airbnb interior design and furnishing services for scope details and the landed furnishing cost guide to compare delivery and setup charges.

How do I compare flat and hourly design fees?

Ask each designer to price the same rooms, layouts, product selections, and review meetings. For a flat fee, confirm how many revisions are included and what triggers a change order. For an hourly quote, ask for an estimated range, which tasks count toward it, and when you’ll be asked to approve more hours.

For example, a hypothetical $2,400 flat fee equals 16 hours at $150 an hour. An hourly proposal that estimates 12 to 20 hours would put design at $1,800 to $3,000. Compare those figures only after checking that both quotes include the same deliverables and revision rounds. Keep purchasing and installation charges on separate lines.

Can my Airbnb furnishing quote change after I approve it?

Ask for the expiration date and which amounts are fixed, estimated, or capped. Confirm whether furniture is held at the quoted price and whether freight, storage, tax, or installation depends on the final address, access, or schedule. Ask who approves substitutions and whether the provider needs your written approval before exceeding an allowance.

For example, a fixed $2,500 design fee and a $12,000 furniture allowance leave the final furniture cost open. Ask whether you’ll see the updated total before orders are placed and which refund or cancellation terms apply to each payment. Record the answers in the furnishing quote checklist.

Does the price include furniture and installation?

A design fee may cover layouts, selections and revisions. If your quote includes a furnishing allowance, ask whether the allowance also covers linens, kitchenware and décor. Find out which lines cover freight, receiving, assembly and final styling, and who pays if a second visit is needed.

Compare quotes against the same room list. If one proposal leaves installation to you, add a local installation bid to that total before comparing it with a turnkey proposal. Use the Airbnb furnishing package comparison to reconcile the differences line by line.

What is the budget boundary?

State which rooms, retained items and finished condition the budget covers. Separate a focused refresh from a complete setup and identify work already funded elsewhere. A furnishing budget that excludes installation or household essentials should say so clearly. Define the boundary before deciding whether the total is adequate.

How do I prioritize a limited budget?

List essential guest functions and observed condition problems first. Use a keep, repair and replace inventory to avoid replacing useful items automatically. Reserve funds for the work needed to make selected purchases usable, then consider optional styling. Defer an optional layer if it would leave an essential task unfinished.

How should uncertainty be represented?

Keep committed costs, current estimates and unresolved amounts in separate columns. Identify the assumptions behind each estimate and the decision needed to replace it with a quote. Any uncommitted reserve should have a stated purpose; do not describe an arbitrary percentage as a universal rule for every rental.

Does the budget need to include owner time?

At minimum, identify the work the owner will perform: measurements, order tracking, receiving, assembly coordination and acceptance. If comparing a self-managed plan with a managed proposal, make that workload visible. The owner can decide how to value it, but leaving it unnamed makes the two approaches difficult to compare.

How do substitutions affect the budget?

Recheck item price, quantity, delivery, installation and any return or cancellation costs. Evaluate whether the substitute still meets the room’s functional and care requirements. A lower-priced item is not automatically a saving if it creates additional work or requires changes elsewhere in the room.

What should be reviewed after completion?

Compare the original plan with the actual final inventory and costs. Record which assumptions were wrong, which changes were necessary and which purchases did not solve the intended problem. Use that record to improve the next property brief rather than treating one project’s total as a universal market benchmark.

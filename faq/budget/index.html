Budget & Cost FAQ

Clear answers on what it costs to design and furnish a short-term rental, and what you can expect in return.

Home / FAQ / Budget & Cost FAQ
How do I set a realistic furnishing budget for my short-term rental?

Start with the property size, bedroom count, target guest type, and the nightly rates comparable properties achieve. A common approach is to budget enough to compete with the top-performing listings in your market without overbuilding for your price point. Include a 10 to 15 percent contingency for delivery, shortages, and surprises. We can help you build a line-item budget during a consultation.

How much does it cost to furnish a short-term rental per room?

Costs vary by market and quality level, but as a general guide, a styled bedroom often runs from a few thousand dollars at a starter level to well over ten thousand at a premium level. Living rooms and kitchens usually cost more than bedrooms because of larger furniture and more equipment. We provide a per-room estimate after reviewing your floor plan and goals.

What is the typical cost per square foot to furnish an STR?

Many full furnishing projects land somewhere between roughly 10 and 40 dollars per square foot, depending on finish level, local labor and delivery costs, and how much decor is included. Smaller homes cost more per square foot because kitchens, baths, and beds are fixed costs. Treat square-foot figures as a planning tool rather than a quote.

How should I allocate my budget between furniture, decor, and linens?

A typical split puts about 50 to 60 percent into furniture, 15 to 20 percent into decor and lighting, 10 to 15 percent into linens and bedding, and the remainder into kitchen supplies and consumables. High-use items like mattresses and sofas deserve more of the budget. We adjust the mix based on your guest profile and property type.

Where should I save money and where should I splurge?

Splurge on mattresses, bedding, seating, and lighting, since these drive reviews and comfort. Save on wall art, accessories, and secondary furniture that guests rarely scrutinize. Choosing commercial-grade or performance fabrics for sofas and chairs is a smart investment because they hold up to heavy use.

What is included in a starter package?

A starter package covers the essentials to make a property bookable and comfortable: beds and mattresses, a sofa, dining setup, basic lighting, linens, kitchen basics, and simple decor. It is designed for investors who want to launch quickly at a controlled cost. You can upgrade over time as revenue grows.

What does a mid-range package add?

A mid-range package adds more cohesive design, higher quality furniture, layered lighting, curated decor, and upgraded linens and kitchenware. It typically includes a stronger focal point in the main living space and a more polished look for photography. For many markets, this tier balances cost with nightly rate potential.

What is included in a premium package?

A premium package delivers a fully custom design with high-end furniture, statement lighting, designer textiles, artwork, and amenity upgrades such as outdoor living areas, game rooms, or spa features. It suits luxury markets and large group properties where guests pay a premium for unique experiences. Premium design often supports significantly higher nightly rates.

Which package is right for my property?

The right package depends on your market, your competition, and the nightly rate you aim to achieve. If top listings in your area are highly designed, a mid-range or premium package is usually needed to compete. For budget-conscious markets, a starter package with strong photography can still perform well.

What ongoing costs should I plan for after furnishing?

Beyond the initial setup, plan for restocking consumables, cleaning supplies, linen replacement, minor repairs, and periodic decor refreshes. Many hosts budget a monthly or annual percentage of revenue for these items. Tracking these costs from the start protects your margins.

How much should I set aside for replacement budgeting?

A common guideline is to reserve around 3 to 5 percent of gross revenue for furniture, linen, and equipment replacement. Linens and towels need frequent replacement, while furniture lasts longer but still wears out under heavy use. A dedicated replacement fund keeps your property looking fresh without disrupting cash flow.

How often do linens and towels need to be replaced?

Most hosts replace towels and sheets every 12 to 24 months depending on turnover, laundering method, and quality. Stains, thinning fabric, and fading are signs that it is time. Keeping spare sets on hand also makes turnovers smoother.

Are furnishing costs tax deductible for a short-term rental?

In many cases, furniture, appliances, and equipment for a rental can be depreciated, and some may qualify for accelerated deductions such as bonus depreciation or Section 179, depending on your situation and current tax law. Rules vary by how you operate the property and your level of participation. Always confirm your specific situation with a qualified tax professional.

What is cost segregation and how does it relate to furnishing?

Cost segregation is a tax strategy that reclassifies parts of a property into shorter depreciation categories so deductions can be taken sooner. Furniture and certain fixtures are often part of that analysis. Because the benefits depend on your facts, we suggest speaking with a CPA experienced in short-term rentals.

Can I finance my furnishing costs?

Some owners fold furnishing costs into acquisition financing, use a home equity line of credit, or use a business line of credit or credit card with promotional terms. Others use specialized lenders offering furniture financing for investors. Compare interest rates and fees carefully, and make sure projected revenue comfortably covers payments.

Should I include furnishing in my purchase loan?

Some loan programs and lenders allow a renovation or furnishing component, while others do not, so options vary. Discuss this with your lender early in the process. Including furnishing in your plan from the start helps you avoid a cash crunch right after closing.

How does DIY furnishing compare to hiring a professional designer?

DIY can look cheaper on paper, but it often costs more in time, returns, mismatched pieces, and delayed launch. Professionals use trade pricing, avoid costly mistakes, and design for guest experience and photography. A delayed opening can cost far more in lost bookings than a design fee.

What hidden costs does DIY furnishing have?

Common hidden costs include delivery fees, assembly time, damaged goods, return shipping, trial and error purchases, and weeks of lost revenue while the property sits unfinished. There is also the cost of poor photos or an unfocused design that earns lower nightly rates. These factors often erase the apparent savings.

What kind of ROI can I expect from professional design?

Well-designed properties typically earn higher nightly rates, higher occupancy, and better reviews than comparable undesigned listings. Results depend on your market, pricing strategy, and property type, so no outcome is guaranteed. We frame design spending as an investment measured against revenue lift and payback period.

How long does it take to recoup furnishing costs?

Payback time depends on cost, market, and revenue performance, but many owners plan for roughly one to three years. Properties launched quickly with strong photography and pricing tend to recover costs faster. We help you model projected revenue against your investment before you commit.

Is it better to over-furnish or under-furnish?

Both extremes have drawbacks. Over-furnishing raises cost, clutter, and cleaning time, while under-furnishing leaves guests missing essentials and hurts reviews. We aim for a well-edited space that covers sleeping capacity, comfort, and key amenities without excess.

Can I phase my furnishing budget over time?

Yes. Many owners launch with core spaces and essentials, then add amenities like a game room, patio furniture, or upgraded decor as revenue comes in. We can plan a phased roadmap so early purchases fit into the long-term design. Phasing reduces upfront cost while keeping the property booking-ready.

What amenities give the best return for the cost?

Amenities such as hot tubs, game tables, fire pits, outdoor seating, and dedicated workspaces often justify higher rates in the right markets. Smaller upgrades like blackout curtains, quality mattresses, and coffee stations are inexpensive but boost reviews. We prioritize amenities guests in your market actually search for.

Do you offer fixed pricing or does the budget change during a project?

We prepare a detailed proposal up front so you know what to expect. If you request changes or upgrades mid-project, we update the budget and get your approval before proceeding. A built-in contingency helps absorb minor price changes or shortages.

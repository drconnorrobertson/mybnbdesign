Outdoor Space Photography for Listings

Your outdoor spaces can be the reason guests choose your property over every other option.

Home / Blog / Outdoor Space Photography for Listings

Why Outdoor Spaces Drive Booking Decisions

Outdoor spaces are among the most powerful selling points for any short-term rental. A well-photographed patio, deck, or garden can be the single image that tips a guest from browsing to booking. Travelers are increasingly searching for properties with usable outdoor areas, whether it is a sprawling backyard with a pool or a small balcony with a bistro set. The outdoor experience you offer can set your listing apart in a crowded market.

Despite their importance, outdoor spaces are frequently the most poorly photographed areas of a rental listing. Hosts rush through exterior shots at the end of an interior shoot when the light has changed, the energy has faded, and the staging has been neglected. Treating your outdoor photography with the same care as your interiors will pay significant dividends in clicks and bookings.

Golden Hour Is Your Best Friend

The most important decision you can make about outdoor photography is when to shoot. Golden hour, the period roughly 30 to 60 minutes after sunrise or before sunset, produces warm, directional light that makes outdoor spaces look absolutely stunning. During this window, shadows are long and soft, colors are rich, and the overall mood is inviting and aspirational.

Plan your outdoor shoot specifically around golden hour rather than treating it as an afterthought at the end of your interior session. If your property faces west, the evening golden hour will light the front beautifully. If it faces east, morning is your window. Walk the property at both times before your shoot to determine which golden hour works best for each outdoor area.

Midday sun creates harsh, unflattering shadows on outdoor furniture and makes surfaces look washed out. Overcast skies eliminate shadows but can make outdoor photos look flat and gray. Golden hour consistently delivers the most compelling outdoor images regardless of the property style.

Stage Your Outdoor Spaces for Entertaining

An empty patio with bare furniture looks forgettable in photos. A styled patio that suggests an evening of entertaining looks like a destination. Before you shoot, stage your outdoor areas the way a guest would use them. Set the dining table with plates, glasses, and cloth napkins. Drape a blanket over a lounge chair. Place a tray with a pitcher and glasses on the side table.

Add cushions and throw pillows to outdoor seating if the furniture allows it. Place a few potted plants or lanterns to add visual interest and color. If you have string lights, turn them on even during golden hour shots. The warm glow adds atmosphere and signals that this is a space designed for relaxation and enjoyment.

Remove all maintenance items from view before shooting. Garden hoses, trash cans, pool equipment, leaf blowers, and cleaning supplies should be hidden completely. Nothing breaks the aspirational mood of an outdoor photo faster than a visible trash bin or a coiled green hose.

Pool Photography That Sells

If your property has a pool, it deserves special attention during the photoshoot. A well-photographed pool can be the hero image of your entire listing. Start by ensuring the water is crystal clear and the pool deck is spotless. Remove all pool toys, floats, and cleaning equipment. The water surface should be calm and reflective.

Shoot the pool from multiple angles: a wide view that shows the pool in context with the house, a closer shot from pool level that emphasizes the water, and an elevated angle if you have access to a second-story window or balcony. Include lounge chairs with neatly placed towels and perhaps a pair of sunglasses to suggest the pool experience.

Golden hour and twilight are the two best times to photograph a pool. At golden hour, the warm light reflects off the water surface beautifully. At twilight, pool lighting creates a dramatic glow against the darkening sky that looks luxurious and inviting.

Gardens and Landscaping as Selling Points

Well-maintained landscaping signals to guests that the property is cared for, and gardens add character and charm that differentiate your listing from competitors. Photograph flower beds, mature trees, garden paths, and any unique landscaping features. If you have fruit trees, herb gardens, or vegetable plots, include them as they appeal strongly to guests who enjoy cooking with fresh ingredients.

Shoot garden areas from a low angle to make plants appear lush and full. Include the property in the background to provide context and show how the garden relates to the living spaces. If a garden path leads to the front door or a seating area, photograph along the path to create a sense of journey and discovery.

Balconies and Small Outdoor Areas

Not every property has a sprawling backyard, and that is perfectly fine. A small balcony or compact patio can be just as appealing in photos when styled and shot correctly. The key is to show the space as functional and inviting rather than trying to make it look larger than it is.

For a small balcony, stage with a bistro table and two chairs, a small potted plant, and perhaps a lantern or candle. Set the table with coffee cups to suggest a morning ritual. Shoot from inside the room looking out through the open door to show the indoor-outdoor connection and frame the view beyond the balcony.

If the balcony has a view, whether it is a city skyline, ocean, mountains, or even a pleasant street scene, make the view the star of the photo. Position yourself so the railing and styled furniture are in the foreground and the view fills the background. This composition tells the guest exactly what their morning coffee experience will look like.

Capturing Views and Surroundings

The area around your property is part of the guest experience, and your photos should communicate what that experience includes. If you have a mountain view, a water view, or a view of a charming downtown, photograph it from the vantage point where guests will actually enjoy it. Stand on the deck, sit in the Adirondack chair, or lean on the porch railing and capture what a guest will see.

Include recognizable landmarks or natural features when possible. A glimpse of the ocean, a ski slope in the distance, or a downtown skyline in the background helps guests place the property geographically and builds excitement about the location.

Night and Twilight Outdoor Shots

Twilight photography is one of the most effective ways to make your listing stand out. The period just after sunset, when the sky turns deep blue, creates a dramatic backdrop that makes interior and exterior lights glow warmly. Before sunset, turn on every light in and around the property: porch lights, landscape lighting, string lights, pool lights, and interior fixtures visible through windows.

Use a tripod or rest your camera on a stable surface, as low-light conditions require longer exposures. The resulting image, with warm glowing lights against a deep blue sky, communicates luxury and atmosphere in a way that daytime photos simply cannot match. Even modest properties look elevated and inviting in a well-executed twilight shot. Include one twilight exterior in your gallery, ideally as the final image, to leave a lasting impression on potential guests.

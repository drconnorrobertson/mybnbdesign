Practical evidence checks, worked examples and editable worksheets for design and installation.

6 guides

Control revisions to rental room measurements

Room measurements can change after flooring, trim work or furniture removal. A measurement register identifies the version used for each purchase and marks assumptions that still need an on-site check. Preserve the previous version so a rejected item can be traced to the information available when it was approved.

Map furniture components for future replacements

A replacement plan should identify the model, finish, dimensions and parts needed to maintain the installed room. Product links can disappear. Keep order records, assembly instructions and component references with the inventory so management can request a compatible replacement without starting a new design search.

Plan installation access windows and receiving coverage

Installation time depends on access, unloading and the availability of the people doing the work. Build the schedule around confirmed delivery windows and the actual building constraints. A delivery booking is different from an installation appointment, and neither should be assumed complete because a carrier marked an order shipped.

Hand off textile care information to the cleaning team

Textile choices affect the daily cleaning workflow. Preserve care labels and product identifiers, then ask the operator to confirm that the intended laundering process is compatible. Record approved replacement colors and sizes so replenishment does not gradually alter the room or introduce mismatched sets.

Set a room photography change freeze

A photography freeze defines which room version is being represented in the listing images. Record the approved setup, outstanding substitutions and any temporary items. The freeze helps design and management coordinate the shoot; it does not justify showing features that will not be available to guests.

Create an outdoor furniture storage handoff

Outdoor furniture needs a practical storage and handling plan as well as a visual layout. Record manufacturer guidance, storage locations and responsibility for moving items when necessary. The plan should fit the actual available space and the operator’s staffing; avoid assuming that every piece can simply be carried indoors.

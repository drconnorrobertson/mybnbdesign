Rental Layouts and Circulation

Plan clear routes, dining seats, luggage storage, work areas, and convertible sleeping spaces around real guest activities.

Updated October 6, 2026

A room plan should work when guests arrive with bags, move chairs, open doors, and use several activities at once. These guides focus on those conflicts. Use measured mock-ups to judge fit, and obtain professional review where building or accessibility requirements apply.

Work through the decisions in order

  1. Draw fixed constraints first: doors, windows, outlets, vents, and built-ins.
  2. Mark travel paths and open furniture positions before adding decorative pieces.
  3. Walk the plan with bags, chairs, and cleaning equipment, then document any compromises.

Start with the earliest unresolved decision. Use the examples as worksheets, then replace their assumptions with measurements, written product specifications, and local operating information.

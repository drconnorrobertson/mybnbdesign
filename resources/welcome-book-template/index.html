Welcome Book Template Guide for Vacation Rentals

Everything you need to create a polished, guest-friendly welcome book that reduces questions, earns five-star reviews, and sets your rental apart.

By MyBnBDesign Team · Published October 2, 2026

Why Every Vacation Rental Needs a Welcome Book

A welcome book is one of the most impactful, low-cost investments you can make for your short-term rental. It serves as a comprehensive reference that answers the vast majority of questions guests would otherwise send you through messaging apps at all hours of the day. Properties with well-designed welcome books consistently see a measurable drop in repetitive guest inquiries, often by 60% or more in the first month after implementation.

Beyond reducing your workload, a welcome book directly shapes the guest experience. When travelers arrive at an unfamiliar property, they want to feel oriented quickly. A clear, well-organized guide gives them confidence that the host is professional, attentive, and prepared. This confidence translates into more relaxed stays, stronger reviews, and a higher likelihood of repeat bookings.

Guests frequently mention the welcome book in their reviews when it is done well. Phrases like "the host thought of everything" and "we had all the information we needed" appear in five-star reviews across Airbnb and Vrbo, and they are almost always tied back to a thoughtful welcome guide. On the other hand, missing or outdated information is a common source of frustration that can drag an otherwise solid listing down to four stars.

A welcome book also projects professionalism. It signals that you run a hospitality business, not just a side project. Guests notice the difference, and it influences how they treat your property. Hosts who invest in a polished welcome book often report fewer rule violations and less property damage.

Essential Sections to Include

Welcome Message and Property Overview

Open your welcome book with a warm, personal greeting. Address your guests directly and express genuine gratitude for their choice to stay at your property. This is also the right place to share a brief story about the property: how it came to be, what inspired its design, or what makes it unique. Keep the tone conversational and inviting without sharing private personal details.

Include the property name, a one-sentence description of the space (for example, "a two-bedroom mountain retreat with panoramic valley views"), and a note about what you hope guests will enjoy most during their stay. This section sets the emotional tone for the entire book, so invest time in getting the language right.

Sample Welcome Message Template

"Welcome to [Property Name]! We are so glad you chose to spend your time here. This cozy retreat was designed with relaxation in mind, and we hope every detail helps you unwind and recharge.

Inside this guide, you will find everything you need: WiFi details, appliance instructions, local recommendations, and house guidelines. If anything is missing or if you have questions during your stay, please do not hesitate to reach out.

We truly appreciate you being here. Enjoy every moment!"

Check-in and Check-out Instructions

This section needs to be crystal clear. Walk guests through the arrival process step by step. Include the exact address (with any clarifications for GPS navigation), where to park, how to access the property (smart lock code, lockbox location, or key pickup), and any gate codes or building entry instructions. If the property has a long driveway, shared entrance, or non-obvious entryway, describe it in detail and consider including a photo.

For check-out, list everything the guest needs to do before leaving. Common items include setting the thermostat to a specific temperature, locking windows and doors, starting the dishwasher, and placing trash in designated bins. Keep instructions numbered so they are easy to follow, and keep the list as short as possible to avoid overwhelming guests.

WiFi and Technology Guide

WiFi credentials are the single most requested piece of information from vacation rental guests. Make the network name and password impossible to miss by placing them prominently, in a larger font if possible. If your property has multiple networks (such as a main network and a guest network), specify which one to use.

Beyond WiFi, cover all technology in the property. Include instructions for the TV (how to switch inputs, access streaming apps, and use the remote), any smart home controls (smart thermostat, lighting systems, voice assistants), Bluetooth speakers, and sound systems. If you have a streaming service logged in for guest use, mention it. If guests need to use their own accounts, say so clearly.

Pro Tip Print the WiFi password on a small card and place it next to the router or on the coffee table, in addition to including it in the welcome book. Guests will thank you.

House Rules

Frame your rules positively whenever possible. Instead of "No loud music after 10 PM," try "Please keep noise to a comfortable indoor level after 10 PM so our neighbors can rest." This subtle shift makes guests feel guided rather than scolded. Cover quiet hours, parking rules, pet policies, smoking policies, maximum occupancy, and any restrictions on specific areas of the property.

If your property is in an HOA community or has specific local regulations, mention them briefly and explain the reasoning. Guests are far more likely to follow rules when they understand the context behind them.

Sample House Rules Template

  • Quiet Hours: We kindly ask that outdoor noise be kept to a minimum between 10:00 PM and 8:00 AM, in consideration of our neighbors.
  • Parking: Two vehicles may park in the driveway. Street parking is available for additional cars. Please avoid parking on the grass.
  • Smoking: This is a smoke-free property. Smoking is welcome on the back patio, and a designated ashtray is provided.
  • Pets: We love animals. Approved pets are welcome with a pet fee. Please keep pets off the furniture and clean up after them in the yard.
  • Maximum Occupancy: The property comfortably accommodates up to [number] guests. For safety and comfort, we ask that this limit be respected.
  • Shoes: We encourage removing shoes at the door to keep the floors clean and comfortable for everyone.

Appliance and Equipment Guides

Not every coffee maker or thermostat is intuitive. Provide brief instructions for every appliance a guest might use. Cover the washer and dryer (cycle settings, detergent location, lint trap), the dishwasher (how to load, which soap to use, how to start), the coffee maker (filters, grounds, water reservoir), and the thermostat (how to adjust temperature, recommended settings).

If your property includes special amenities like a hot tub, fireplace, or outdoor grill, dedicate a full subsection to each one. Include safety precautions, startup procedures, and shutdown steps. For hot tubs in particular, mention cover replacement, temperature limits, and chemical safety. These details protect both your guests and your equipment.

Local Recommendations

This section is where your welcome book can truly shine. Guests want insider knowledge, not a generic list they could find on Google. Organize recommendations by category: restaurants (broken down by cuisine type), grocery stores, coffee shops, outdoor activities, family-friendly attractions, nightlife, and hidden gems that only locals know about. For each recommendation, include the name, approximate distance or drive time from the property, and a one-sentence description of why you recommend it.

Consider organizing entries by distance as well, so guests can quickly find options that are nearby versus worth a day trip. Seasonal recommendations are also valuable. If certain trails are best in fall or a particular restaurant runs a summer patio series, mention it.

Emergency Information

Safety information must be easy to locate in an emergency. List the nearest hospital with its address and phone number, the closest urgent care clinic, the non-emergency police line, and your personal or property manager contact number. Include the location of the fire extinguisher, first aid kit, and any emergency exits or assembly points.

If the property is in an area prone to specific hazards (hurricanes, wildfires, earthquakes, severe winter storms), add a brief section on what guests should do in the event of a weather emergency. This level of preparedness reassures guests and demonstrates responsible hosting.

Checkout Procedures

Reiterate checkout procedures in a clear, numbered format. Common requests include starting the dishwasher, placing used towels in the bathtub or laundry basket, taking out the trash and recycling, turning off all lights and fans, locking all doors and windows, and returning the thermostat to a specified setting. Keep this list to ten items or fewer. Guests are far more likely to follow a concise list than a lengthy one.

Design Tips for Your Welcome Book

The visual quality of your welcome book matters nearly as much as the content. Choose no more than two fonts: one for headings and one for body text. Use generous spacing, clear section dividers, and a consistent color palette that matches your property's branding or aesthetic.

Include high-quality photos of the property throughout the book. Images of the kitchen, living room, outdoor space, and surrounding area help guests feel oriented and excited about their stay. Icons can replace or supplement text for universal concepts like WiFi, parking, and trash, making the book more scannable.

Design a cover page with the property name, a hero photo, and your contact information. This cover sets the tone and gives the entire document a professional, polished feel. If you use a consistent color scheme and layout across every page, the book will feel cohesive and intentional.

Digital vs. Physical Welcome Books

Both formats have clear strengths, and many successful hosts use a combination of the two. The table below summarizes the key differences to help you decide which approach fits your property.

Feature Digital Welcome Book Physical Welcome Book
Accessibility Available on guest's phone anytime, anywhere Available only at the property
Updates Instant updates with no reprinting Requires reprinting changed pages
Guest engagement Can include links, maps, and videos Tactile, browsable, and always visible
Cost Low to none after initial setup Moderate (printing, binding, page protectors)
Durability No wear and tear Subject to spills, bending, and loss
Pre-arrival use Can be sent before check-in Only available on-site
Personal touch Professional but less tangible Feels warm, curated, and intentional

The strongest approach is often to maintain a digital version as the primary reference and place a physical copy in the property for guests who prefer flipping through pages. Link the two by including a QR code on the cover of your physical book that directs guests to the digital version. This way, guests can access the information on their phones when they are out exploring and still have a printed reference at the house.

Digital Welcome Book Tools and Platforms

Several platforms specialize in digital welcome books for vacation rentals. Hostfully offers a dedicated guidebook builder with drag-and-drop sections, maps, and integration with major booking platforms. Touch Stay provides a similar feature set with a focus on branded, mobile-friendly guides that can be shared via link or QR code.

For hosts who prefer a DIY approach, Canva offers hundreds of welcome book templates that can be customized with your photos, colors, and content, then exported as a PDF. Google Sites is a free option for creating a simple, web-based welcome page. For maximum control and branding, a custom-designed PDF created in a tool like Adobe InDesign or Figma gives you complete creative freedom.

When choosing a platform, consider how often you update your content, whether you manage multiple properties, and how important mobile responsiveness is. Platforms like Hostfully and Touch Stay justify their subscription cost for hosts with multiple listings, while Canva and Google Sites work well for single-property hosts who want to minimize expenses.

Physical Welcome Book Production

If you are creating a physical welcome book, durability is a priority. A three-ring binder with clear page protectors is the most practical option for properties that need frequent updates. You can swap out individual pages without replacing the entire book. Choose a binder with a clear front sleeve so you can insert a custom cover page.

For a more polished look, consider a professionally printed and bound booklet. Saddle-stitched (stapled) booklets work well for shorter guides of 16 to 24 pages. Perfect-bound (glued spine) books are better for longer guides and give a premium, magazine-like feel. Coil binding is another durable option that allows pages to lie flat, which is helpful for kitchen countertops.

Regardless of format, use heavyweight paper (at least 80 lb text or 100 gsm) and a matte or satin finish to reduce glare and resist fingerprints. Laminated covers add durability without adding much cost. Many local print shops and online services like Blurb, Mixbook, or Lulu can produce professional-quality booklets at reasonable prices.

Enhancing Guest Experience Through Your Welcome Book

A welcome book can go beyond logistics and become a genuine hospitality touchpoint. Consider adding personalized elements that surprise and delight guests. A handwritten note on the cover page or tucked inside the book adds warmth that digital communication simply cannot replicate. Even a short, handwritten "Welcome! We hope you love it here" on a card placed on top of the book creates a memorable first impression.

Seasonal updates keep your book feeling fresh and relevant. In winter, include recommendations for nearby ski resorts, cozy restaurants, and hot chocolate spots. In summer, highlight the best beaches, farmers markets, and outdoor concerts. This kind of seasonal specificity makes guests feel like they are getting insider access rather than a static document.

You can also include a small "guest favorites" section that highlights things previous guests have loved. If guests consistently rave about a nearby bakery or a sunset viewpoint, mention it. This social proof makes the recommendation more compelling and gives guests a starting point for their trip.

Welcome Book Mistakes to Avoid

The most common mistake hosts make is creating a welcome book that is too long. If your guide exceeds 20 to 25 pages, guests are unlikely to read it thoroughly. Edit ruthlessly and focus on the information guests actually need. Supplementary details (like a complete restaurant directory) can live in a separate appendix or digital link.

Outdated information is another frequent problem. A welcome book with a wrong WiFi password, a closed restaurant recommendation, or an incorrect checkout time damages trust instantly. Commit to reviewing your book at least once per quarter.

Avoid using a negative, punitive tone in your house rules. Phrases like "Violators will be charged" and "Absolutely no exceptions" create an adversarial relationship before the guest has even unpacked. Frame rules as mutual respect guidelines and explain the reasoning where helpful.

Skipping photos is a missed opportunity. A text-only welcome book feels sterile and is harder to navigate. Including even a handful of images (the front of the property, the kitchen layout, the thermostat panel) makes the guide more useful and visually engaging.

Finally, do not forget essentials. Every welcome book should include WiFi credentials, emergency contacts, check-in/check-out procedures, and basic appliance instructions. Leaving out any of these creates a gap that generates guest messages and frustration.

Quick Checklist Before publishing your welcome book, confirm it includes: WiFi details, emergency numbers, check-in/out steps, house rules, at least five local recommendations, and appliance guides for any non-obvious equipment.

Updating and Maintaining Your Welcome Book

Set a recurring schedule to review and update your welcome book. A quarterly review works well for most properties. During each review, verify that all contact numbers, WiFi credentials, and business recommendations are current. Check that no restaurants or attractions have closed, moved, or changed their hours.

Seasonal transitions are natural update points. Before each peak season, refresh your local recommendations, update any seasonal house rules (pool hours, fireplace usage, snow removal), and swap in relevant photos. If you have made any property changes (new appliances, renovated rooms, added amenities), update the corresponding sections immediately.

Keep a running list of guest questions throughout the year. If you notice the same question coming up repeatedly, add the answer to your welcome book. This iterative improvement process ensures your book becomes more comprehensive and effective over time, reducing your workload with every update cycle.

Calculate the Landed Cost of Rental Furnishings

Compare furniture quotes with shipping, receiving, assembly, tax, removal, and a separately stated contingency allowance.

Updated October 6, 2026

The product price is only one part of getting a usable item into a rental. A sofa with a low advertised price can become the more expensive choice after delivery, stair handling, assembly, or returns. Build a landed-cost worksheet so the selection decision reflects the work your project actually requires.

Make the decision from the actual setup

Start with item quantity and unit price, then add actual quoted shipping, receiving or storage, installation, and any disposal charge. Include tax using the applicable quote or rate for the delivery location; do not assume a national rate. Check whether a delivery fee covers the room of choice or only a curbside drop. Keep optional service contracts distinct from required charges.

Show contingency as its own planning allowance rather than hiding it inside each item. Do not add tax or delivery twice when they are already included. Record currency, quote date, expiration, and the payment schedule. Compare quotes only after matching the scope: a flat-pack bed needing assembly and a fully installed bed are different deliverables.

Worked planning example

Illustrative scenario: Examples, prices, quantities, and timing assumptions below are hypothetical; use actual product information and local quotes for your property.

Hypothetical example, using assumed prices and tax solely to demonstrate arithmetic: two chairs at $180 each cost $360. Shipping is $70, assembly is $40, and assumed tax is $36. The landed total is $506, or $253 per installed chair. A second quote of $480 for both chairs delivered and assembled, plus assumed tax of $38.40, totals $518.40. The difference is $12.40. That small gap may matter less than dimensions, replaceable parts, or whether the delivery service can meet the receiving plan.

Practical checklist

  1. Record quantity, model, finish, unit price, and which services the product listing explicitly includes.
  2. Obtain the delivery location surcharge and confirm access limits before treating a shipping estimate as final.
  3. Ask whether receiving, storage, assembly, mounting, and packaging removal are separately priced.
  4. Verify the tax basis, discount treatment, and any refundable deposit with the vendor quote.
  5. Compare the full installed totals and record a separate contingency assumption with its reason.

Carry the decision into the handover

Keep estimated and committed totals in separate columns. Replace estimates as written quotes arrive, then reconcile invoices after installation. If a vendor changes the model, rerun the entire line because size, freight, and assembly may change too. This is a purchase-planning exercise; it does not predict bookings or returns from the furnishing investment. Use actual local quotes to set the project budget.

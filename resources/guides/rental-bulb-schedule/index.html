Create a Bulb Schedule That Survives Replacements

Record bulb base, shape, lumens, appearance, dimming, fixture compatibility, and spare locations for consistent rental lighting.

Updated October 6, 2026

A room can start with coordinated lighting and gradually become a mix of unrelated bulbs as replacements occur. A bulb schedule prevents the maintenance team from guessing. It records the actual specification of each installed light and makes the spare stock manageable.

Make the decision from the actual setup

Assign each fixture a location code. Record bulb base, shape, size, brightness in lumens, light appearance, and any dimming requirement. FTC’s Lighting Facts guidance explains why wattage and brightness are different measures. Check fixture limits and suitability for enclosed, damp, or other relevant conditions using the actual fixture and bulb instructions.

Group fixtures that can share the same approved bulb, but do not standardize away important requirements. A bedside lamp and a covered ceiling fixture may need different products. Integrated fixtures need their own maintenance record because they may not accept a replaceable bulb. Test the chosen bulbs with existing dimmers before ordering a large batch.

Worked planning example

Illustrative scenario: Examples, prices, quantities, and timing assumptions below are hypothetical; use actual product information and local quotes for your property.

Hypothetical schedule: living-room lamps L1 and L2 use the same approved base, shape, and light appearance. The kitchen fixture K1 has a different enclosure requirement, so its bulb has a separate stock code. The cleaner reports a failed lamp and the manager selects the labeled L1/L2 spare. They do not use a brighter kitchen spare merely because the base fits. A photograph of the box and fixture record supports future reordering.

Practical checklist

  1. Give every fixture a room and position identifier matching the floor plan or inventory photographs.
  2. Record the complete bulb specification and the fixture’s limits or location requirements from manufacturer instructions.
  3. Test ordinary switching and dimming with the chosen bulb before standardizing it across the room.
  4. Label approved spares by compatible fixture groups and store them where the responsible team can find them.
  5. Record integrated fixtures, driver information where applicable, and the service contact separately from replaceable bulbs.

Carry the decision into the handover

Update the schedule when a fixture or approved bulb changes. After replacement, check appearance from several positions and ensure controls still work normally. A product with the same base can differ in output, size, and compatibility. Keep the schedule simple enough that a person arriving for one maintenance visit can use it without opening several unrelated shopping lists.

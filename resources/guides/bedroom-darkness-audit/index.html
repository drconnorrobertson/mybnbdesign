Audit Bedroom Light Gaps Before Ordering Curtains

Measure and test bedroom light entry from windows, doors, exterior lights, and devices before selecting window treatments.

Updated October 6, 2026

Blackout fabric addresses light through the material, but bedroom darkness also depends on gaps, hardware, doors, and nearby lights. Audit the room during the relevant daylight and nighttime conditions before selecting the treatment. The result should guide measurement and installation rather than relying on a product label alone.

Make the decision from the actual setup

Record where light enters: top and side gaps, a door opening, neighboring exterior lighting, or a device indicator. Photograph the room from the bed with normal lights off. Measure the window, surrounding wall, hardware position, and any vent or door conflict. Ask the installer to define suitable coverage and fixing for the actual window conditions.

Consider operation and care as well as darkness. Guests need to open the treatment easily, and the cleaner needs a supported maintenance process. CPSC guidance highlights the risks of window-covering cords; use appropriate products and installation. Do not tape over safety indicators, block vents, or obstruct exits to darken a room. Address each light source with a suitable solution.

Worked planning example

Illustrative scenario: Examples, prices, quantities, and timing assumptions below are hypothetical; use actual product information and local quotes for your property.

Hypothetical bedroom: a dense curtain leaves a bright side gap because the rod ends almost at the glass edge. A wider suitable installation is assessed, while the nearby wardrobe door limits the available side coverage. A separate exterior light shines toward the window; its placement is reviewed as part of the outdoor lighting plan. The final test happens from the bed rather than from a flattering listing-photo angle.

Practical checklist

  1. Observe the room in daylight and at night, recording each significant source of unwanted light from the sleeping position.
  2. Measure window and wall conditions, hardware location, and conflicts with doors, vents, or existing furniture.
  3. Confirm product suitability, cord safety, mounting, and care instructions before approving the treatment.
  4. Test operation and cleaning access after installation without blocking ventilation or required routes.
  5. Describe the installed arrangement accurately instead of promising complete darkness under every external condition.

Carry the decision into the handover

Include curtain positions and operation in the bedroom reset guide. Recheck after moving exterior lights or replacing devices with visible indicators. If a treatment catches, sags, or detaches, resolve the installation issue before asking guests to handle it differently. A darkness audit can improve comfort, but it is not a medical claim or a guarantee that every guest will sleep well.

Plan Laundry Capacity Against the Turnover Schedule

Compare required linen loads, approved washing and drying processes, machine capacity, staffing, and off-site backup for a rental.

Updated October 6, 2026

Enough linen sets do not guarantee that the laundry process can handle a busy changeover. Compare the actual wash, dry, sort, and storage cycle with the volume the property generates. The worksheet should reveal constraints before the team is forced to return damp or incomplete stock to service.

Make the decision from the actual setup

List the textiles in one ordinary reset and group them according to their care instructions. Check usable machine capacity from the equipment and textile guidance, not only the drum’s advertised size. Include sorting, loading, transfer, drying, inspection, folding, and return to clean storage. A wash program’s duration is not the whole process.

Map the available staff time and equipment occupancy. If loads overlap across several properties, assign their capacity rather than assuming each can use the same machine simultaneously. Build a supported backup for peak volume or equipment failure. Keep used and clean textiles separated, and do not count items as available until they are properly processed and ready.

Worked planning example

Illustrative scenario: Examples, prices, quantities, and timing assumptions below are hypothetical; use actual product information and local quotes for your property.

Hypothetical capacity exercise: a reset creates four compatible loads after following textile and machine instructions. Each load’s assumed wash-and-dry process occupies the equipment for two hours, totaling eight hours if processed sequentially. Sorting and handling add further work. The available on-site interval is shorter, so the owner arranges suitable off-site processing or changes the stock-and-return plan. These assumed times demonstrate the calculation; the real schedule needs measured cycles.

Practical checklist

  1. Count the reset textiles and separate loads by the actual washing and drying instructions.
  2. Confirm permitted load capacity and program requirements for the equipment and textiles being used.
  3. Measure the full process, including handling, inspection, folding, transport, and clean storage.
  4. Map equipment and staffing availability across the actual properties and turnover pattern.
  5. Define an off-site or spare-stock contingency that keeps unsuitable or damp textiles out of guest use.

Carry the decision into the handover

Review the worksheet after a busy period, changed linen specification, or machine replacement. A larger machine may not solve drying or handling bottlenecks. Update par stock together with the processing plan so the two assumptions remain consistent. Record actual failures and delayed returns to improve the next planning cycle rather than relying on an ideal schedule that never occurs.

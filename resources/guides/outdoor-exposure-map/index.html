Map Sun, Rain and Wind Before Buying Patio Furniture

Create an outdoor exposure map that connects guest activities, product suitability, loose-item storage, and local reset procedures.

Updated October 6, 2026

An outdoor furniture specification should start with the site, not a coordinated product collection. Sun, driving rain, wind, salt, irrigation, and ground conditions can affect different parts of the same patio. Map those exposures at the times guests are likely to use the space before selecting furniture or textiles.

Make the decision from the actual setup

Draw the usable area, entrances, fixed equipment, planting, and places where water collects or wind is noticeable. Visit at representative times and note that a single visit does not describe every season. Ask the local manager about conditions they observe. Separate a guest’s comfortable sitting area from the handling and storage needed when conditions change.

Check the actual furniture and textile instructions for the proposed location. “Outdoor” is not a promise of unlimited exposure or zero maintenance. Verify anchoring or mounting through appropriate product instructions and professionals where needed. Keep movement and storage practical; a weather procedure that requires equipment the team does not have will not be reliable.

Worked planning example

Illustrative scenario: Examples, prices, quantities, and timing assumptions below are hypothetical; use actual product information and local quotes for your property.

Hypothetical patio: the wall-side seating is shaded during breakfast, while the center receives direct afternoon sun and occasional wind-driven rain. Loose cushions can be stored in a dry indoor location, but the proposed oversized sectional cannot be moved by the usual team. The owner compares smaller serviceable pieces and the actual exposure guidance rather than assuming the most substantial-looking set is the best fit.

Practical checklist

  1. Map outdoor activities, entrances, drainage observations, fixed equipment, and the locations proposed for furniture.
  2. Observe representative conditions and combine the visit with the local manager’s seasonal experience.
  3. Check product suitability and care instructions for the actual sun, moisture, wind, and salt exposure.
  4. Define how loose items are handled and where they can be stored clean and dry.
  5. Confirm appropriate mounting or other technical work and document the local reset owner and process.

Carry the decision into the handover

Keep the exposure map with the outdoor inventory and revisit it when shade, planting, or drainage changes. During a seasonal handoff, compare observed conditions with the original assumptions. If a product deteriorates, investigate exposure and care before ordering the same replacement. The map guides selection and maintenance; it does not certify the site against every weather condition.

Build a Furnishing Replacement Reserve Worksheet

Estimate a rental furnishing reserve from individual replacement scenarios, useful-life assumptions, and real failure records.

Updated October 6, 2026

A replacement reserve is money held for worn or damaged items after launch. It is separate from cleaning supplies and the original furnishing budget. You do not need to predict each failure perfectly to start a useful worksheet. You do need to show your assumptions and update them when the property provides better evidence.

Make the decision from the actual setup

List items that would disrupt the guest experience if unavailable: mattresses, bed frames, key seating, curtains, lamps, and small appliances. Enter an estimated replacement cost including delivery and installation. Add a planning interval based on product condition and your operating expectations, clearly marking it as an assumption. Divide the cost by that interval to create an annual allowance; the result is a budgeting convention, not a claim about product lifespan.

Keep accidental damage scenarios in a separate column. Coverage, deposits, and reimbursement processes may not pay the full cost or pay quickly. Ask the relevant provider about actual terms rather than deducting a hoped-for recovery. A spare item can reduce an interruption, but it still ties up cash and requires storage.

Worked planning example

Illustrative scenario: Examples, prices, quantities, and timing assumptions below are hypothetical; use actual product information and local quotes for your property.

Hypothetical worksheet: a sofa replacement is assumed to cost $1,200 installed after four years, giving $300 a year. Two lamps together cost an assumed $180 after three years, giving $60 a year. A curtain set costs an assumed $300 after five years, giving $60 a year. These three lines total $420 annually, or $35 monthly. They are only a partial inventory. A real reserve would also consider other assets and a separate allowance for unexpected failures.

Practical checklist

  1. Inventory the essential furnishings and flag items with long replacement lead times or unusual dimensions.
  2. Record installed replacement cost, condition, planning interval, and where each estimate came from.
  3. Calculate annual allowances per line, then sum them without presenting the interval as a warranty.
  4. List damage scenarios and any coverage questions separately from normal wear assumptions.
  5. Review the worksheet after actual replacements and during the property’s scheduled condition review.

Carry the decision into the handover

After a failure, record age, cause, downtime, repair cost, and whether a different specification would have helped. Keep cosmetic updates separate from necessary replacements so style preferences do not consume the entire reserve. If your records show cushions wear while the frame remains sound, model cushion replacement separately. The reserve should become more specific over time, using this property’s experience rather than an unsupported industry percentage.

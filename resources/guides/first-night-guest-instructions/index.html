Write First-Night Instructions from a Real Test Stay

Create a short arrival-to-bed guide from observed controls, storage, lighting, bedding, and contact needs in the installed property.

Updated October 6, 2026

A welcome guide can become a list of features the guest does not need on the first night. Write the essential arrival-to-bed instructions from a real walkthrough of the installed property. Focus on the actions a new guest must complete before they can settle in.

Make the decision from the actual setup

Have a tester unfamiliar with the room enter, set down bags, find useful lights, locate the bathroom, and prepare for sleep. Observe confusion rather than coaching immediately. Record the questions that recur: which switch works, where extra bedding is stored, how curtains operate, or where to put wet towels. Correct the layout or malfunction first, then write instructions for the remaining necessary actions.

Use accurate, short statements and ordinary names that match the labels in the property. Keep private access information and security-sensitive details in the manager’s appropriate guest-delivery process rather than publishing them in a public design article or uncontrolled file. Provide a clear contact route for problems the guest cannot resolve.

Worked planning example

Illustrative scenario: Examples, prices, quantities, and timing assumptions below are hypothetical; use actual product information and local quotes for your property.

Hypothetical trial: the tester cannot find the bedside lamp switch and looks for extra blankets in owner storage. The lamp is repositioned and the clean blanket location becomes obvious. The resulting guide needs only a short curtain explanation, the climate-control steps, and the help contact. It does not compensate for every design issue with another paragraph. The owner verifies each instruction against the final installed setup before photography and launch.

Practical checklist

  1. Run the arrival-to-bed journey with a tester who does not already know the property’s control layout.
  2. Record confusion and distinguish broken functions, poor placement, and genuinely necessary instructions.
  3. Fix functional or layout issues before writing a workaround into the guest guide.
  4. Use names matching the property and keep confidential access details in the appropriate private operating process.
  5. Verify the final instructions after installation and assign an owner to update them when the setup changes.

Carry the decision into the handover

Keep the public listing, guest guide, and cleaner reset consistent about what is provided. Repeat the first-night test after major changes or repeated feedback. Short instructions work best when the room itself is understandable. Save the observations in the commissioning log so the team can see why a control, storage location, or instruction was changed.

Review Glare from Screens, Mirrors and Glossy Surfaces

Test light reflections at actual guest eye positions before fixing television, mirror, lamp, and dining-table placement.

Updated October 6, 2026

A bright reflection may be almost invisible in a staged photo but distracting from a sofa or bed. Review glare at the positions guests actually use. Screen placement, shade design, window coverage, and reflective finishes interact, so the solution may involve moving one element instead of increasing or decreasing every light.

Make the decision from the actual setup

Sit at each main position and look at the television, mirror, tabletop, and other glossy surfaces. Repeat during daylight and after dark. Turn light sources on individually so the team can identify the cause. Note whether the reflection prevents a task, is merely visible, or depends on a temporary arrangement.

Try practical alternatives with temporary placements before committing to wall mounting or custom wiring. A lamp moved slightly out of a sight line may solve the issue; a very opaque shade can create a new task-light problem. Use suitable mounting and electrical work through the responsible installer. Do not solve reflections by making an ordinary route too dark.

Worked planning example

Illustrative scenario: Examples, prices, quantities, and timing assumptions below are hypothetical; use actual product information and local quotes for your property.

Hypothetical living room: two lamps reflect prominently in the television from the center sofa seat. One is repositioned to support reading from a nearby chair, and the other gets a suitable shade reviewed with the fixture. Daylight glare still appears at a particular hour, so the window treatment is tested. The owner records the useful control combination for television viewing while retaining the brighter arrangement for arrival and cleaning.

Practical checklist

  1. Inspect screens, mirrors, and glossy surfaces from actual seated and standing guest positions.
  2. Repeat the observation in representative daylight and nighttime conditions with light sources switched on separately.
  3. Trial fixture position, shade, furniture orientation, and window coverage before permanent installation.
  4. Confirm that changes preserve useful task lighting and clear routes rather than simply reducing all light.
  5. Record approved positions and control combinations in the room reset instructions.

Carry the decision into the handover

Revisit glare when artwork, mirrors, screens, shades, or finishes change. A replacement table with a glossier surface can alter the same room’s lighting. Ask trial-stay testers about the task they attempted rather than whether the room looked stylish. The aim is usable visibility in the installed conditions, and the test should keep that activity at the center.

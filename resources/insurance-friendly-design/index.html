Why Design Matters for Insurance

Insurance companies evaluate vacation rental properties based on risk. Every design choice you make, from the type of flooring in your bathroom to the railing height on your balcony, directly affects how insurers assess your property. The good news: thoughtful design decisions can meaningfully reduce your premiums while also creating a safer, more enjoyable guest experience.

Short-term rental properties face unique insurance challenges. Unlike long-term rentals, where a single tenant becomes familiar with the space over months or years, vacation rentals host a rotating cast of guests who are unfamiliar with the layout, fixtures, and potential hazards. Children, elderly guests, and visitors who may have been drinking all present elevated risk profiles that insurers factor into their pricing models.

A proactive approach to design can reduce your annual insurance costs by 10 to 25 percent, depending on your market and carrier. More importantly, it reduces the likelihood of claims that could raise your rates or lead to policy cancellation. Below, we break down the most impactful design decisions across every area of your rental property.

Flooring and Slip-Resistant Surfaces

Slip-and-fall accidents are the number one source of liability claims for vacation rental owners. The flooring you choose is your first and most important line of defense. Insurance adjusters pay close attention to floor surfaces, particularly in wet areas like bathrooms, kitchens, entryways, and pool surrounds.

Recommended Flooring Materials

Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) has emerged as the gold standard for vacation rental flooring, and insurance companies agree. Products from brands like COREtec, LifeProof, and Shaw Floorte offer textured surfaces that provide excellent grip even when wet. Look for flooring with a slip resistance rating of R10 or higher (measured by the DIN 51130 ramp test). LVP is also waterproof, which eliminates the risk of water damage to subflooring that can lead to mold claims.

Porcelain tile with a matte or textured finish is another excellent choice, especially for bathrooms and kitchens. Avoid polished or glazed tiles, which become dangerously slick when wet. Look for tiles rated with a Dynamic Coefficient of Friction (DCOF) of 0.42 or higher, as recommended by the American National Standards Institute (ANSI). Brands like Daltile, Florida Tile, and Marazzi all offer lines specifically designed for wet environments.

For outdoor areas and entryways, consider stamped concrete with a broom finish or natural stone pavers with a honed surface. These provide reliable traction in all weather conditions and are easy to maintain.

Pro Tip

Keep the receipts and specification sheets for your flooring materials. When your insurer asks about slip resistance ratings, having documentation on hand demonstrates proactive risk management and can support lower premium quotes.

Bathroom-Specific Considerations

Bathrooms deserve special attention because they combine hard surfaces with water, creating the highest-risk environment in any rental property. Install textured, non-slip tiles or LVP rather than smooth ceramic. Place high-quality suction-cup bath mats in every tub and shower. Consider walk-in showers with built-in benches and grab bars instead of tub/shower combinations. These features appeal to guests of all ages and significantly reduce fall risk.

ADA-compliant grab bars from companies like Moen, Delta, and Ponte Giulio now come in stylish finishes like brushed nickel and matte black. They no longer look institutional. Installing them in showers and beside toilets signals to your insurer that you take guest safety seriously, and your guests will appreciate the thoughtful design.

Fire Safety Through Design

Fire-related claims are among the most expensive for both property owners and insurance companies. Beyond the required smoke detectors and fire extinguishers, your design and material choices play a critical role in fire prevention and containment.

Fire-Resistant Materials

Choose upholstery fabrics that meet California Technical Bulletin 117-2013 (TB 117-2013) flammability standards. Most commercial-grade furniture fabrics, such as Sunbrella, Crypton, and Revolution Performance Fabrics, meet or exceed these standards. These materials resist ignition from small open flames and smoldering sources, giving guests more time to react in an emergency.

For window treatments, select curtains and drapes made from inherently flame-resistant (IFR) fabrics rather than chemically treated materials. IFR polyester and modacrylic blends maintain their fire resistance through repeated washing, whereas chemical treatments can degrade over time. Roller blinds and cellular shades from companies like Hunter Douglas and Levolor also offer fire-rated options that look clean and modern.

In the kitchen, install a backsplash made from porcelain tile, stainless steel, or tempered glass behind the stove. These materials will not ignite or contribute to the spread of a grease fire. Avoid open shelving near cooking surfaces where combustible items like paper towels and wooden utensils could catch fire.

Kitchen and Cooking Safety

Induction cooktops are increasingly popular in vacation rentals, and insurance companies favor them for good reason. Since induction cooktops heat the pan directly rather than the surface, the risk of burns and kitchen fires drops dramatically. Brands like Bosch, GE Profile, and Samsung offer sleek induction models that appeal to guests while reducing your liability. If a gas range is non-negotiable for your property, install an automatic stove shut-off device such as the FireAvert, which cuts power when the smoke detector activates.

Keep a Class K fire extinguisher in the kitchen (designed for cooking oil fires) and a Class ABC extinguisher in the hallway. Mount them in visible, accessible locations. Document the installation dates and inspection schedule in your property records.

Insurance Insight: Many insurers offer a premium discount of 5 to 10 percent for properties with monitored smoke and CO detectors connected to a smart home system. Products like Nest Protect and First Alert Onelink send alerts to your phone and can notify emergency services automatically.

Water Damage Prevention

Water damage is the most common type of property insurance claim in the United States, and vacation rentals are especially vulnerable because leaks can go undetected between guest stays. Designing your property to minimize water damage risk will protect your investment and keep your premiums low.

Install smart water leak detectors at every potential failure point: under kitchen and bathroom sinks, behind toilets, near the water heater, beside the washing machine, and at the base of dishwashers. Systems like Flo by Moen, Phyn, and Flume can detect leaks in real time and automatically shut off the main water supply before significant damage occurs. Many insurance companies now offer discounts of 5 to 15 percent for properties with automatic shut-off systems.

Choose plumbing fixtures with ceramic disc cartridges rather than rubber washer valves. Ceramic disc faucets from Kohler, Moen, and Grohe are far more durable and less prone to leaks. For supply lines, use braided stainless steel hoses instead of rubber. Rubber supply lines are a leading cause of catastrophic water damage in residential properties because they can burst without warning after several years of use.

In bathrooms, apply a waterproof membrane system such as Schluter DITRA or LATICRETE Hydro Ban beneath tile surfaces. These systems prevent water intrusion even if grout lines crack or deteriorate. Combined with properly sloped shower floors and quality linear drains, they eliminate the risk of slow leaks that cause mold and structural damage over time.

Childproofing and Family Safety

Vacation rentals that welcome families with children face additional liability considerations. Childproofing is not just a courtesy; it is a risk management strategy that insurers value highly.

Start with furniture anchoring. Every freestanding bookshelf, dresser, TV stand, and armoire should be secured to the wall with anti-tip straps or L-brackets. Use commercial-grade anchors rated for the weight of the piece. The Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) reports that a child is injured by a falling piece of furniture every 17 minutes, and these incidents generate significant liability claims.

For windows above the first floor, install window guards or window stops that prevent the sash from opening more than four inches. Brands like Guardian Angel and KidCo make devices that are easy for adults to override in emergencies but prevent children from opening windows wide enough to fall through. Cordless window treatments are essential, as window blind cords are a strangulation hazard. The Window Covering Manufacturers Association (WCMA) has promoted cordless designs since 2018, and most insurers now expect them.

Cover all electrical outlets with tamper-resistant receptacles (TRRs). These are required by the National Electrical Code in new construction, but many older properties still have standard outlets. Replacing them is inexpensive and eliminates a common hazard. For sharp furniture corners, apply transparent corner guards from brands like Roving Cove or CalMyotis. These are barely visible but prevent painful injuries to toddlers at table height.

Stairway Safety

If your property has stairs, ensure the railing height meets local building codes (typically 36 inches for residential and 42 inches for commercial or mixed-use). Baluster spacing should be no more than four inches apart to prevent a child from getting their head stuck. Non-slip stair treads or carpet runners on wooden stairs reduce fall risk for guests of all ages. Products from Dean Flooring and House, Home and More offer attractive runners that stay securely in place.

Liability-Reducing Furniture Choices

The furniture you select affects both insurance risk and guest satisfaction. Focus on stability, durability, and commercial-grade construction.

Choose beds with low profiles and sturdy frames. Platform beds eliminate the risk of a box spring sliding off a frame, which can injure guests. Avoid bunk beds if possible; if your property requires them to accommodate larger groups, select models that meet ASTM F1427-13 safety standards with integrated ladders, full-length guardrails, and weight ratings clearly posted. Bunk beds generate a disproportionate number of injury claims, so document compliance with safety standards in your records.

For dining and living areas, choose furniture with rounded edges and wide, stable bases. Glass tabletops should be tempered safety glass at minimum, and laminated safety glass is preferable because it holds together when broken rather than shattering into dangerous shards. Avoid rocking chairs and lightweight folding furniture that can tip or collapse. Instead, invest in solid wood or high-quality upholstered pieces rated for commercial use.

Outdoor furniture should be weighted or securable. Lightweight patio furniture can become a projectile in high winds, damaging property or injuring guests. Choose pieces made from heavy-gauge aluminum, wrought iron, or weighted resin wicker. Brands like Polywood, Telescope Casual, and Tropitone manufacture commercial-grade outdoor furniture built for hospitality environments.

Outdoor Spaces and Pool Areas

Outdoor areas present elevated risk, and insurers evaluate them carefully. If your property has a pool, hot tub, or elevated deck, expect these features to be significant factors in your premium calculation.

Pool barriers are typically required by local code, but going beyond minimum requirements demonstrates risk awareness. Install self-closing, self-latching gates with locks that are at least 54 inches from the ground. Pool alarms that detect when someone enters the water provide an additional layer of protection. Products like the PoolGuard PGRM-SB and Safety Turtle wristband system are recognized by insurers as effective safety devices.

For pool decking, use textured concrete, travertine pavers with a tumbled finish, or composite decking with embossed grain patterns. These surfaces provide excellent traction when wet. Avoid smooth sealed concrete or polished stone near water features. Add clear "No Diving" signage in pools that are too shallow, and post depth markers at regular intervals.

For hot tubs, install locking covers that meet ASTM F1346 standards. These covers prevent unsupervised access and are a requirement for most vacation rental insurance policies. Keep the hot tub temperature at or below 104 degrees Fahrenheit and post safety rules in a weatherproof frame nearby.

Security Features That Lower Premiums

Security systems protect against both theft and liability claims, and they often qualify for insurance discounts. A comprehensive security setup for a vacation rental should include smart locks with unique codes for each guest (eliminating the lost key problem), doorbell cameras at entry points, motion-activated exterior lighting, and a monitored alarm system.

Smart locks from August, Schlage Encode, and Yale Assure are popular in the vacation rental industry. They integrate with property management platforms like Guesty and Hospitable, automatically generating and expiring access codes for each reservation. This creates a verifiable record of who had access to the property at any given time, which is invaluable for insurance purposes.

Exterior lighting is one of the most cost-effective security investments. Motion-activated LED floodlights at all entry points, along pathways, and around parking areas deter intruders and reduce trip-and-fall incidents. Solar-powered path lights from brands like Ring, LITOM, and BAXIA Technology are easy to install and require no wiring. Well-lit exteriors tell insurers you are managing premises liability proactively.

Documentation for Insurance Claims

If a claim does arise, your ability to document the pre-loss condition of your property and the safety measures you had in place will significantly influence the outcome. Good documentation practices start at the design phase and continue throughout the life of the property.

Pre-Loss Documentation

Create a comprehensive property inventory with photographs and video of every room, fixture, appliance, and piece of furniture. Record serial numbers, purchase dates, and replacement costs. Store this documentation in the cloud using a service like Google Drive, Dropbox, or a dedicated property management platform. Update the inventory annually and after any significant purchase or renovation.

Maintain a file of all material specifications, safety certifications, and installation records. This includes flooring slip resistance ratings, furniture flame resistance certificates, pool barrier compliance documentation, and electrical inspection reports. When you install grab bars, anti-tip anchors, or smoke detectors, photograph the installation and file the receipt.

Maintenance Records

Keep a detailed maintenance log that records every inspection, repair, and replacement. Document smoke detector battery changes, fire extinguisher inspections, pool chemical testing, HVAC filter replacements, and plumbing inspections. A well-maintained log demonstrates to insurers and, if necessary, to a court that you exercised reasonable care in maintaining the property.

Schedule professional inspections annually for critical systems: electrical, plumbing, HVAC, structural, and (if applicable) pool equipment. Keep the inspection reports in your property file. If an inspector identifies a deficiency, document the repair and retain the contractor's invoice.

Claims Tip

In the event of property damage, document the scene immediately with photos and video before beginning cleanup or repairs. Contact your insurer within 24 hours and provide your pre-loss inventory documentation. Properties with thorough documentation typically see faster claim resolution and higher recovery amounts.

Working with Your Insurer

Building a collaborative relationship with your insurance provider pays dividends over time. When you are shopping for coverage, share your design specifications and safety features upfront. Many carriers offer credits or discounts for specific improvements, but they can only apply them if they know what you have installed.

Ask your insurer for a property risk assessment before finalizing your design plan. Some carriers will send an inspector to walk the property and identify specific improvements that would reduce your premium. This consultation is often free and provides a prioritized list of upgrades with the highest return on investment from an insurance perspective.

Consider specialized vacation rental insurance providers like Proper Insurance, CBIZ, and Safely. These companies understand the short-term rental industry and offer policies tailored to its unique risk profile. They are often more receptive to design-based risk reduction strategies than general insurers because they have deep data on the types of claims vacation rentals generate.

Review your policy annually, especially after making improvements. If you have installed a water shut-off system, added a security system, replaced flooring with slip-resistant materials, or made other upgrades covered in this guide, inform your insurer. Each improvement is an opportunity to negotiate a lower premium or better terms.

Final Thought: Insurance-friendly design is not about compromise. The materials and features that reduce your insurance risk also create a better guest experience, earn higher reviews, and protect the long-term value of your property. Every dollar spent on safety-conscious design pays for itself through lower premiums, fewer claims, and happier guests.

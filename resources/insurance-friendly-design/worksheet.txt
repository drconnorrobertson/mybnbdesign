Prepare a furnishing record pack for the actual insurance review

Use this guide to organize information for your qualified insurance adviser and relevant service providers. It does not determine coverage, establish compliance, prescribe inspection intervals, or promise a premium reduction. Describe the actual short-term rental use accurately when obtaining advice. Ask the adviser what evidence and policy questions matter for this property, rather than assuming that a furniture or security purchase changes the insurance position.

Keep one index linking the current room inventory, product specifications, installation documents, receipts, condition photographs, and maintenance records. Retain actual paid costs and dated replacement quotes as different fields. Do not present a generic online price as the amount your policy would reimburse. The adviser’s requirements govern the records needed for a particular review or claim.

Record group: Property and actual use
Useful documents to organize: Current property description, rental operation, amenities and relevant changes
Question for the responsible adviser/provider: What use and features need to be disclosed or reviewed?

Record group: Furnishing inventory
Useful documents to organize: Room, item/model, quantity, purchase date, receipt and condition photos
Question for the responsible adviser/provider: What inventory evidence or valuation information is required?

Record group: Materials and installation
Useful documents to organize: Exact product specifications, intended-use limits, installation scope and relevant provider records
Question for the responsible adviser/provider: Which technical questions or documentation need qualified review?

Record group: Maintenance and repairs
Useful documents to organize: Fault reports, inspection/service documents, invoices and completion evidence
Question for the responsible adviser/provider: What process and records are appropriate to the actual products and property?

Record group: Special amenities
Useful documents to organize: Documents for any supplied pool, balcony, outdoor, cooking or other relevant feature
Question for the responsible adviser/provider: What exclusions, conditions or separate requirements apply?

Record group: Policy and adviser record
Useful documents to organize: Current documents, contact details, questions and written responses
Question for the responsible adviser/provider: Which proposed changes need approval or clarification before implementation?

Copy the inventory and document index

Photograph the current item and its identifying details where available, and keep the receipt or supplier record with the same identifier. Record retained items honestly when receipts or dates are missing. Store the pack in an appropriately controlled location with a backup separate from the property, and confirm that the person who will need it can retrieve it. These records support an organized conversation; they do not guarantee claim acceptance or a particular settlement.

Discuss design-related questions without assuming coverage

A room or surface choice may raise practical questions that deserve technical review. Document the exact proposed product, its intended use, and the work being contemplated. Ask the qualified provider to address relevant requirements rather than labelling a material “insurance friendly,” “non-slip,” or “fireproof” on the strength of a marketing description.

Area to discuss: Floors, wet areas and stairs
Facts to bring: Exact surfaces, transitions, product information and installation documents
Question to record: What relevant specification, maintenance or qualified inspection is needed?

Area to discuss: Furniture and mounting
Facts to bring: Product identifiers, condition, mounting instructions and intended room use
Question to record: Who should confirm installation and suitability for the intended use?

Area to discuss: Cooking and supplied equipment
Facts to bring: Actual appliances, installation/service records and operating instructions
Question to record: Which technical or insurance questions remain unresolved?

Area to discuss: Water or moisture issues
Facts to bring: Observed issue, location, photographs and relevant provider assessment
Question to record: Who investigates the cause and records the completed work?

Area to discuss: Family-oriented amenities
Facts to bring: Exact supplied products and intended users/use
Question to record: What suitability review, instructions or restrictions need confirmation?

Area to discuss: Outdoor, pool or balcony features
Facts to bring: Actual amenities, service records, relevant property constraints and operating limits
Question to record: Which policy or qualified-provider requirements apply to these features?

Area to discuss: Security/access changes
Facts to bring: The specific proposed change and its documented purpose
Question to record: Is any adviser or provider review required? Do not assume a discount.

Maintain a factual service and follow-up log

Set schedules from the relevant instructions and qualified advice, then record the work actually done. Avoid a universal annual inspection promise for every system or a blanket quarterly replacement rule. A record should distinguish reported, inspected, repaired, tested, and still open. Close a task only when the responsible person has documented its completion within their scope.

Know where to find the applicable incident instructions

Keep the current adviser, insurer, property contact and relevant emergency instructions accessible through the approved operating process. If damage or an incident occurs, follow applicable emergency and policy instructions and seek the appropriate qualified help. Record factual observations and communications when it is appropriate to do so; do not use this template to invent notification deadlines, coverage, liability conclusions, or settlement amounts.

- Know who holds the current policy documents and who is authorized to communicate about them.

- Locate the actual incident/reporting instructions before a problem occurs.

- Record dates, observed condition, document references and the people handling the issue.

- Distinguish an observed fault from an unconfirmed cause or insurance conclusion.

- Retain relevant communications and completed-work records in the controlled property pack.

- Ask the qualified adviser what further evidence or action is required for the specific circumstances.

Brief a Designer for a Short-Term Rental Project

Prepare a designer brief with measurements, retained items, guest functions, itemized budget, responsibilities, constraints, and approval steps.

Updated October 6, 2026

A designer brief should explain the property and the decisions the team needs to make. It does not need a claim about revenue improvement to be useful. Provide a measured, honest record of the existing space, intended guest activities, and practical operating constraints before discussing an aesthetic direction.

Make the decision from the actual setup

Include room plans, labeled photographs, retained furnishings, maintenance issues, and delivery access. Describe guest functions and the separately verified operating capacity. Set an itemized budget basis that distinguishes furnishings, freight, receiving, installation, technical work, and contingency. Label assumed prices and avoid presenting a universal cost percentage as a rule.

Define the requested deliverables and responsibilities. Specify who purchases, approves substitutions, receives deliveries, arranges trades, performs laundry, and signs off readiness. State schedule dependencies and which dates are confirmed or estimated. Provide style references with explanations rather than asking the designer to reproduce another property’s image.

Worked planning example

Illustrative scenario: Examples, prices, quantities, and timing assumptions below are hypothetical; use actual product information and local quotes for your property.

Hypothetical brief: the owner retains two bed frames pending condition inspection, replaces living-room seating, and wants clear work/dining use in a compact room. Painting is handled before installation by a separate provider. The designer supplies the measured furnishing plan and product specification. Procurement and assembly responsibilities are stated explicitly so the word full service cannot conceal an omission.

Practical checklist

  1. Supply measured plans, labeled photos, retained-item condition, and delivery-route information.
  2. Describe intended guest activities and actual operating constraints without assuming market-wide performance.
  3. State the complete budget basis and label unconfirmed cost or timing assumptions.
  4. Define deliverables, exclusions, approval owners, and procurement or installation responsibilities.
  5. Agree on completion evidence and a handover pack that the local team can use.

Carry the decision into the handover

Keep revisions and approvals with the brief. If a constraint changes, update the scope and product decisions rather than treating the original board as still approved. At handover, compare the installed functions with the brief. Clear inputs and responsibilities make the project reviewable without promising a particular rate increase, payback period, or guest outcome.

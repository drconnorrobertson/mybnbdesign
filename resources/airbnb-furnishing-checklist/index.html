Complete Airbnb Furnishing Checklist

By MyBnBDesign Team · Published October 2, 2026 · 12 min read

Furnishing a short-term rental can feel overwhelming. Miss a single essential item and your guest reviews will reflect it. This room-by-room checklist covers every piece of furniture, decor, kitchen tool, safety device, and supply you need to create a five-star experience. Print it, share it with your designer, or use it as a procurement guide the next time you set up a property.

1. Why a Comprehensive Checklist Matters

Every experienced short-term rental host has at least one story about a guest complaint caused by a missing item: no corkscrew on wine country weekend, no extra blankets during an unexpected cold snap, or no cutting board in an otherwise beautiful kitchen. These small oversights translate directly into lower star ratings and fewer bookings.

A structured furnishing checklist protects you from those gaps. It also helps you budget accurately, compare vendor quotes, and coordinate with contractors or interior designers. If you have already read our ultimate STR design guide, think of this page as the tactical companion: every line item you will need to purchase, organized by room, with specific quantities based on your property's guest capacity.

Whether you are furnishing your first studio apartment or your tenth four-bedroom cabin, this checklist will keep you organized from first order to final walkthrough.

2. Living Room Furnishings

The living room is where guests form their first impression of your rental. Prioritize comfort, durability, and a cohesive look that photographs well. For staging advice, see our photography staging guide.

Seating and Tables

  • Sofa (1 for studios/1BR; sectional or sofa + loveseat for 2BR+)
  • Accent chairs (1 to 2, depending on room size)
  • Coffee table (1, preferably with rounded edges for safety)
  • Side tables (1 to 2, one per seating group)

Entertainment

  • Smart TV, 50" minimum (wall-mounted saves floor space)
  • TV stand or media console (if not wall-mounted)
  • Streaming device (Roku, Apple TV, or Fire Stick as backup)
  • Board games or card games (2 to 3 options for rainy days)

Lighting and Decor

  • Floor lamp (1 to 2)
  • Table lamp on side table (1)
  • Throw pillows (4 to 6, mixing textures and patterns)
  • Throw blankets (2, one light and one heavier weight)
  • Area rug (1, at least 5x7 feet under the seating area)
  • Wall art (2 to 4 pieces; local or nature themes perform well)
  • Decorative tray or books for coffee table (1 set)
  • Curtains or drapes (1 set per window, blackout-lined recommended)
Pro Tip: Choose performance fabrics like Crypton or Sunbrella for your sofa. They resist stains and clean easily, saving you hundreds in replacement costs over the life of the property.

3. Kitchen Essentials

A well-stocked kitchen is one of the highest-impact areas for guest satisfaction. Guests expect to cook basic meals without needing to bring their own tools. Stock for a "service for 8" as a baseline for any property sleeping four or more guests.

Cookware and Bakeware

  • Nonstick skillet, 10" (1)
  • Stainless steel skillet, 12" (1)
  • Saucepan with lid, 2-quart (1)
  • Stock pot with lid, 6-quart (1)
  • Baking sheet, half-size (2)
  • 9x13 baking dish (1)
  • Mixing bowls, nested set (1 set of 3)

Utensils and Tools

  • Spatula, silicone (2)
  • Wooden spoons (2)
  • Tongs (1)
  • Whisk (1)
  • Can opener (1)
  • Bottle opener and corkscrew (1 combo or separate)
  • Vegetable peeler (1)
  • Colander (1)
  • Cutting boards (2: one for produce, one for meat)
  • Chef's knife, 8" (1)
  • Paring knife (1)
  • Serrated bread knife (1)
  • Measuring cups and spoons (1 set each)

Small Appliances

  • Coffee maker (drip or single-serve; both if space allows)
  • Toaster or toaster oven (1)
  • Microwave (built-in or countertop, 1)
  • Electric kettle (1, especially popular with international guests)
  • Blender (1, for smoothie-friendly markets)

Dishware, Glassware, and Flatware

  • Dinner plates (8)
  • Salad or dessert plates (8)
  • Bowls (8)
  • Mugs (8)
  • Drinking glasses (8)
  • Wine glasses (6 to 8)
  • Flatware set: forks, knives, spoons, teaspoons (service for 8)
  • Serving spoon (1) and serving fork (1)

Kitchen Linens and Cleaning

  • Dish towels (4 to 6)
  • Oven mitts or pot holders (2)
  • Dish soap and sponge (1 of each)
  • Trash bags (starter supply under the sink)
  • Paper towels (2 rolls on arrival)
  • Salt, pepper, olive oil, and basic spice set (starter supply)
Pro Tip: Buy white ceramic dishware in bulk from a restaurant supply store. It is inexpensive, easy to replace piece by piece, and photographs beautifully for your listing.

4. Master Bedroom

Sleep quality is one of the strongest predictors of a five-star review. Invest heavily in the mattress and bedding; these items touch every single guest.

Bed and Frame

  • Bed frame, queen or king (1, platform style eliminates the need for a box spring)
  • Mattress, medium-firm, at least 10" thick (1; budget at least $500 for a queen)
  • Waterproof mattress protector (1, zippered encasement style)
  • Mattress topper (1 optional, adds a hotel-quality feel)

Bedding Layers

  • Fitted sheet, white (3 sets for rotation during turnovers)
  • Flat sheet, white (3 sets)
  • Duvet insert, all-season weight (1)
  • Duvet cover, white (3 for easy laundering)
  • Pillows, standard or king (4: 2 firm, 2 soft to suit preferences)
  • Pillow protectors, zippered (4)
  • Decorative shams or euro pillows (2 to 3 for staging)
  • Extra blanket, folded at foot of bed (1)

Furniture and Accessories

  • Nightstands (2, one per side of the bed)
  • Bedside lamps (2, with USB charging ports built in if possible)
  • Dresser or closet organizer (1, with at least 4 drawers or shelves)
  • Hangers in closet (15 to 20, matching wooden or velvet)
  • Full-length mirror (1)
  • Blackout curtains (1 set, critical for guest sleep quality)
  • Luggage rack (1, foldable to save space)
  • Alarm clock with USB charging (1)
  • Wall art (1 to 2 pieces, calming tones)
Pro Tip: Use only white sheets and towels across your entire property. They can be bleached for a consistently fresh appearance, and replacements always match existing inventory.

5. Guest Bedrooms

Guest bedrooms should receive the same attention to comfort as the master. The mattress and bedding quality should be comparable, even if the furniture is simpler.

Standard Guest Room

  • Bed frame, queen or full (1)
  • Mattress with waterproof protector (1)
  • Sheets, duvet cover, pillows (same quantities as master)
  • Nightstand (at least 1)
  • Bedside lamp (1)
  • Dresser or 3-drawer chest (1)
  • Hangers (10 to 15)
  • Blackout curtains (1 set)
  • Small mirror (1)

Bunk Room or Kids Room

  • Bunk bed frame with safety rails (1)
  • Twin mattresses with waterproof protectors (2)
  • Twin sheet sets, white (3 sets per bed)
  • Lightweight comforters (2)
  • Pillows (2 to 4)
  • Bunk bed shelf or clip-on light for each bunk (2)
  • Small toy bin or bookshelf (1, for family-friendly properties)
  • Night light (1)

6. Bathroom Essentials

Plan towel inventory based on maximum guest capacity. The rule of thumb is three full sets per guest: one in use, one in the laundry, and one staged for the next turnover.

Towels and Linens (per bathroom)

  • Bath towels (2 per guest the bathroom serves, plus 2 extras)
  • Hand towels (2 per bathroom)
  • Washcloths (2 per guest)
  • Bath mat (1 per bathroom, non-slip backing)

Toiletries and Accessories

  • Shampoo, conditioner, body wash (refillable wall-mounted dispensers recommended)
  • Hand soap at every sink (1 per sink)
  • Toilet paper (2 rolls staged per bathroom, plus backup in closet)
  • Hair dryer (1 per full bathroom, wall-mounted or in a drawer)
  • Wastebasket with liner (1 per bathroom)
  • Vanity mirror, magnifying (1 in the master bathroom)
  • Shower curtain and rings or glass door squeegee (1)
  • Over-the-door hooks or towel rack (1 set per bathroom)
  • Toilet brush with holder (1, kept discreetly near the toilet)
  • Plunger (1 per bathroom, stored in a cabinet or closet)

First Aid and Extras

  • First aid kit (1 per property, stocked with bandages, antiseptic, pain reliever)
  • Extra toilet paper (6-roll pack stored in a closet or cabinet)
  • Tissues (1 box per bathroom)

7. Outdoor and Patio Furnishings

Outdoor spaces are among the most photographed areas of any listing. Even a small balcony benefits from thoughtful furnishing. Larger properties with decks or yards should treat this section as a separate "room" in the budget.

  • Outdoor dining table with chairs (seating for max guest count)
  • Lounge chairs or Adirondack chairs (2 to 4)
  • Outdoor sofa or loveseat with cushions (1 for larger patios)
  • Side table (1 to 2)
  • Outdoor rug (1, weather-resistant polypropylene)
  • Umbrella or shade sail (1, essential for sunny climates)
  • String lights or lanterns for ambiance (1 set)
  • Citronella candles or bug repellent station (seasonal)
  • Grill (gas or charcoal, depending on HOA rules)
  • Grill utensil set: spatula, tongs, brush (1 set)
  • Outdoor doormat (1 at each entry)
Pro Tip: Powder-coated aluminum furniture resists rust and weighs less than wrought iron, making it easier for your cleaning team to move during turnovers. Pair it with Sunbrella cushions for all-weather durability.

8. Utility and Cleaning Supplies

Guests rarely mention these items in rave reviews, but they will absolutely mention them in complaints if something is missing. Stock your utility closet generously.

  • Vacuum cleaner (1, upright or cordless stick)
  • Broom and dustpan (1 set)
  • Mop and bucket or Swiffer-style mop (1)
  • Laundry detergent pods (starter supply)
  • Dryer sheets or wool dryer balls (1 set)
  • Iron and ironing board (1 set, stored in a closet)
  • Stain remover spray (1 bottle)
  • All-purpose cleaning spray (1 bottle under the kitchen sink)
  • Extra bed linens (1 full set per bed)
  • Extra towels (2 to 4 bath towels in reserve)
  • Light bulbs, assorted sizes (small pack of replacements)
  • Batteries, AA and AAA (1 pack of each)
  • Extension cord (1)
  • Flashlight (1)
  • Tool kit, basic: screwdriver, pliers, tape measure, picture hooks (1 small kit)

9. Technology and Connectivity

Reliable technology is no longer a bonus; it is a baseline expectation. Guests will test the Wi-Fi within minutes of arrival. Slow speeds lead to immediate complaints.

  • Wi-Fi router with at least 100 Mbps service (1; post network name and password clearly)
  • Wi-Fi extender or mesh system (1 for multi-level properties)
  • Smart lock with keypad entry (1, eliminates key handoff logistics)
  • Streaming device on every TV (Roku, Fire Stick, or Apple TV)
  • Bluetooth speaker (1, portable and waterproof for outdoor use)
  • USB charging stations or power strips with USB ports (1 per bedroom, 1 in living area)
  • Smart thermostat (1, with guest-friendly temperature limits)
  • Outdoor security camera at entrance (1, disclosed in listing per Airbnb policy)
Pro Tip: Print the Wi-Fi network name and password on a small framed card placed near the TV and on the welcome book. Guests should never have to search for connection details.

10. Safety Equipment

Safety equipment is non-negotiable. Most jurisdictions require specific devices by law, and Airbnb's own policies mandate smoke and carbon monoxide detection. Check your local regulations for additional requirements.

  • Smoke detectors (1 per bedroom, 1 in each hallway, 1 near the kitchen)
  • Carbon monoxide detectors (1 per sleeping area and near any gas appliance)
  • Fire extinguisher, 5 lb ABC-rated (1 in the kitchen, 1 per floor)
  • First aid kit, fully stocked (1 per property)
  • Flashlights with batteries (2, in accessible locations)
  • Emergency contact card posted near the phone or on the fridge (1)
  • Fire escape route posted inside a closet door or near the exit (1)
  • Fire blanket (1, in or near the kitchen)
  • Deadbolt or chain lock on the main entry door (1)
  • Window locks on ground-floor windows (all)

11. Quality Tiers and Budget Ranges

Your furnishing budget depends on your target market, local competition, and revenue goals. Below are three tiers for a typical one-bedroom rental. Scale up proportionally for larger properties.

Budget Tier

$3,000 to $5,000
  • IKEA or Wayfair furniture
  • Basic mattress (memory foam in a box)
  • Simple dishware from a big-box store
  • Minimal decor: 1 to 2 wall prints, basic throw pillows
  • Standard towels and sheets (cotton-poly blend)
  • No outdoor furniture beyond a doormat

Mid-Range Tier

$8,000 to $15,000
  • Quality sofa with performance fabric
  • 10" hybrid mattress, medium-firm
  • White ceramic dishware, service for 8
  • Curated decor: matching art, styled shelves
  • 100% cotton sheets, 300+ thread count
  • Basic patio set with cushions
  • Smart lock and streaming devices

Premium Tier

$20,000+
  • Designer or custom furniture pieces
  • Luxury mattress (Beautyrest, Saatva, or equivalent)
  • Hotel-grade linens, 400+ thread count percale or sateen
  • Professional interior design with cohesive theme
  • Full outdoor living area with dining, lounging, and lighting
  • Smart home ecosystem: thermostat, lighting, speakers
  • Custom welcome book with professional photography

Most hosts see the best return on investment at the mid-range tier. It produces listing photos that stand out from budget competitors and delivers the comfort guests expect for $150 to $300 per night properties. Premium furnishing makes sense for luxury cabins, beachfront homes, and high-ADR urban markets where nightly rates exceed $400.

12. Sourcing Strategies

Where and when you buy can save you 20% to 40% on total furnishing costs. Here are proven strategies used by experienced hosts.

Best Retailers by Category

  • Furniture: IKEA (budget), Wayfair and Article (mid-range), Crate & Barrel and West Elm (premium)
  • Mattresses: Zinus and Lucid (budget), Nectar and Tuft & Needle (mid-range), Saatva (premium)
  • Linens: Amazon Basics (budget), Brooklinen and Parachute (mid-range to premium)
  • Kitchenware: Restaurant supply stores like WebstaurantStore or Katom for commercial-grade items at lower prices
  • Decor: Target, TJ Maxx, and HomeGoods for affordable, stylish accessories
  • Outdoor: Costco and Sam's Club often have the best value on patio sets

Timing Your Purchases

Furniture sales follow predictable seasonal patterns. Presidents' Day (February), Memorial Day (May), Labor Day (September), and Black Friday (November) consistently offer the deepest discounts, typically 25% to 40% off retail. If you can plan your property launch around these windows, the savings are substantial.

Wholesale and Bulk Options

Hosts managing multiple properties should consider opening wholesale accounts. Suppliers like Sysco Guest Supply, HD Supply, and AmerSleep offer hospitality-grade products at volume pricing. Restaurant supply stores also sell dishware, flatware, and glassware at a fraction of retail prices because their products are designed for durability and frequent replacement.

13. Budget Planning Tips

Smart budgeting is not about spending the least amount possible. It is about directing your dollars toward the items guests notice and care about most.

Where to Splurge

  • Mattress: Guests spend 8+ hours on it. A great mattress earns five-star sleep reviews.
  • Sofa: The most-used and most-photographed piece of furniture in the property.
  • Bedding: High-quality sheets and a fluffy duvet create instant comfort.
  • Towels: Thick, absorbent bath towels signal quality. Budget $8 to $12 per towel.
  • Lighting: Warm, layered lighting makes any space feel inviting in photos and in person.

Where to Save

  • Kitchenware: Restaurant supply plates and glasses cost $1 to $3 each and last for years.
  • Nightstands and side tables: Simple designs from IKEA or Facebook Marketplace work well.
  • Decor accessories: TJ Maxx, thrift stores, and discount retailers offer stylish options at a fraction of retail.
  • Cleaning supplies: Buy in bulk from warehouse clubs and keep a restocking schedule.
  • Hangers: Uniform velvet hangers from Amazon cost under $0.50 each in packs of 50.

Prioritization Framework

If your budget is tight, work through this priority order. Focus on guest-facing, high-use items first and fill in secondary items as revenue allows.

  1. Tier 1 (Day One essentials): Beds, bedding, towels, toilet paper, basic kitchen setup, smart lock, Wi-Fi, safety equipment.
  2. Tier 2 (First month additions): Sofa, dining table, TV with streaming, decor, outdoor seating.
  3. Tier 3 (Revenue-funded upgrades): Premium coffee maker, Bluetooth speaker, board games, decorative styling, upgraded lighting, welcome book.

Track every purchase in a simple spreadsheet with columns for item, vendor, cost, date purchased, and expected replacement date.

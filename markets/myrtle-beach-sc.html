The Myrtle Beach Short-Term Rental Market

Myrtle Beach is a high-volume family beach destination along the Grand Strand, known for its boardwalk, golf courses, and affordable vacations. Millions of visitors arrive each year, especially from the Southeast, Mid-Atlantic, and Midwest, making it one of the busiest short-term rental markets on the East Coast.

Condo supply is large, and many oceanfront buildings have their own rental rules. Single-family homes in Surfside Beach, Garden City, and North Myrtle Beach, as well as golf community properties, offer opportunities for groups and families who want more space than a condo.

At MyBnBDesign, we treat every market as its own design problem. A listing in Myrtle Beach competes against a specific set of local alternatives, serves a specific guest, and has to survive specific wear and weather. That is why our short-term rental design services start with local research before any furniture is chosen.

Common Property Types in Myrtle Beach

Understanding which property types dominate a market tells us how guests search and what they expect. In Myrtle Beach, the most common short-term rental properties include:

Oceanfront condos

Mid and high-rise units with balconies, resort amenities, and pools.

Beach houses

Larger homes for multi-family groups in North Myrtle Beach, Surfside, and Garden City.

Golf community villas

Townhomes near attractions

Because purchase prices and renovation budgets vary this much, we scope each design plan to the property, the guest, and the target rate rather than applying a one-size-fits-all package.

Who Books Airbnbs in Myrtle Beach

Guests are mostly families, golf groups, multigenerational travelers, and snowbirds seeking a warm winter stay. Designing for the real guest, not a generic traveler, is the first step to higher conversion. A couple on a weekend trip judges a listing differently than a family of eight, and the photos, bed configurations, and amenities should answer the questions that guest is asking.

Peak seasons. Peak season is Memorial Day through Labor Day, with strong shoulder periods during spring golf season and fall. Winter has snowbird demand but lower nightly rates. We plan layouts, bedding, and styling so a property is ready for its highest-demand weeks, and we recommend pricing strategies that match those windows.

Local Competition and Why Design Matters in Myrtle Beach

Myrtle Beach is highly competitive, with thousands of condos furnished the same way. Because many guests are price sensitive, design has to deliver visible value. Standout photography, bold but tasteful decor, and family-focused features such as themed bunk rooms allow owners to hold rates above the pack and capture more bookings in shoulder seasons.

In every market, the guest decision happens in seconds. The first photo, the cover image, and the first five images in the gallery drive click-through rate, and the overall feel of the home drives review language. Better design improves both. ADR in Myrtle Beach and How Design Affects It

Average daily rates in Myrtle Beach typically range from $130 to $330 depending on size, location, season, and quality. Design is one of the few levers an owner controls directly, and it influences ADR, occupancy, and review scores at the same time.

Operating Considerations for Myrtle Beach Hosts

Design works best when it supports operations. In Myrtle Beach, we recommend choosing finishes that clean quickly between turnovers, stocking duplicate linen and towel sets so housekeeping never limits your calendar, and using layered, dimmable lighting so the home photographs well by day and feels welcoming at night. Smart locks, reliable Wi-Fi, and clear wayfinding in the home reduce guest questions and protect your review average. Always confirm local permits, tax registration, insurance, and any HOA rules before you finalize a design budget, since these affect which layouts and capacities you can legally market.

Myrtle Beach Airbnb Design FAQ

Is Myrtle Beach a good short-term rental market?

Yes, for volume-focused owners. It has strong summer demand and a large family guest base, though competition and condo supply are high.

What design helps condos stand out?

Distinctive decor, updated lighting, themed bedrooms, and professional photos help differentiate in a sea of similar units.

Who books in Myrtle Beach?

Mostly families, golf groups, and multigenerational travelers, along with winter snowbirds.

What is a realistic ADR?

Most properties range from about $130 to $330 depending on size, location, and season, with oceanfront and large homes at the high end.

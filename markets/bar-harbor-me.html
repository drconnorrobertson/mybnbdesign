Local Design Trends

Design Trends in Bar Harbor, Maine

Bar Harbor and the surrounding Acadia National Park region represent one of New England's most iconic vacation destinations. The short-term rental market here serves nature enthusiasts, outdoor adventurers, and travelers seeking the authentic Maine experience. Design should capture the rugged beauty and unpretentious charm that define Downeast Maine.

The Bar Harbor aesthetic draws from the region's maritime heritage and dense natural environment. Weathered wood, granite, natural stone, and iron are the foundational materials. Interiors should feel like they belong to the landscape rather than imposing upon it, with colors and textures drawn from the rocky coastline, evergreen forests, and dramatic ocean views.

Unlike more polished coastal destinations, Bar Harbor embraces a rusticity that feels intentional and authentic. Exposed ceiling beams, stone fireplaces, handmade quilts, and furniture with visible joinery create warmth and character. This is not about roughing it but about celebrating craftsmanship and natural materials in their honest forms.

Acadia National Park drives the majority of visits to the Bar Harbor area, and properties should support the outdoor adventure lifestyle. Practical design elements like mudrooms, outdoor gear storage, and easy-clean entryways address the reality of guests returning from hiking, biking, and kayaking with wet and muddy equipment.

Maine's shoulder seasons and winter months are gaining popularity among travelers seeking solitude and dramatic scenery. Properties designed for year-round comfort with efficient heating, cozy gathering spaces, and well-lit interiors for short winter days can capture this growing market segment and extend revenue beyond the traditional summer season.

Expert Recommendations

Design Recommendations for Bar Harbor

01

Build the living room around a substantial stone or brick fireplace. This is the emotional center of a Maine rental, and it should feel like it has been warming the home for generations.

02

Use a palette of forest green, warm gray, cranberry, and natural wood tones against white or cream walls. These colors reference the Maine landscape and create interiors that feel rooted in place.

03

Install wide plank pine or reclaimed wood flooring with area rugs in classic patterns like buffalo check, tartan, or braided wool. These floor treatments add warmth and define living areas in open floor plans.

04

Furnish with a mix of sturdy farmhouse antiques and comfortable contemporary seating. A vintage pine dining table paired with a modern sectional sofa bridges heritage and comfort effectively.

05

Create a covered porch or screened-in room for enjoying Maine's outdoors without the bugs. Equip it with rocking chairs, blankets, and lanterns for evening use during the magical late-summer and fall seasons.

Compare project scope, furniture and launch decisions

These practical guides compare approaches and materials. Each includes tradeoffs, a property scenario and questions to ask before committing.

Full-service vs design-only STR furnishing

Choose how much of the furnishing project you want to manage yourself. A room plan and shopping list do not automatically include purchasing, receiving or installation.

Remote vs local interior design for a rental

Compare the need for physical site access with the quality of your measurements, photos and local execution team. Location alone does not establish rental experience.

Furniture package vs custom furnishing plan

Compare a predetermined bundle with a room-by-room plan built around your property. Evaluate the exact included inventory and measurements rather than the number of pieces advertised.

New vs secondhand furniture for a short-term rental

Use acquisition cost, inspection effort and replacement needs to decide where secondhand pieces make sense. A low purchase price can be offset by transport, cleaning and repair.

Performance fabric vs leather rental sofas

Compare documented cleaning instructions, comfort and repair options for the actual sofa model. Material labels alone do not predict how a product will hold up in your property.

Washable vs traditional rugs in vacation rentals

Choose a rug by the room, turnover workflow and cleaning equipment available. Washable does not mean a large rug will fit the machine at your property.

Sleeper sofa vs dedicated guest bed

Compare the nightly guest experience with the space needed during the day and at bedtime. A theoretical extra sleeping position is useful only when guests can access it comfortably.

Matching bedroom sets vs mixed furniture

Choose between a coordinated set and individually selected pieces based on room dimensions, storage and future replacement needs. Visual consistency does not require every piece to come from one collection.

DIY vs professional furniture installation

Compare available labor, tools, delivery access and the complexity of the item list. Installation includes more than assembling the biggest pieces.

Full property refresh vs room-by-room upgrades

Choose the project sequence around the condition of existing furniture, available cash and scheduling. Prioritize functional defects before decorative improvements.

Statement design vs neutral rental interiors

Compare visual identity with maintenance, lighting and the rest of the property. Neither approach guarantees bookings; choose a plan that works in person as well as in listing photos.

Pre-launch furnishing vs redesigning an operating rental

Compare preparing the complete property before opening with improving a property that already hosts guests. The latter needs a work schedule that fits actual reservations and access.

Compare the written scope, not just the headline price

  • List the rooms, measurements, guest capacity and intended use.
  • Separate design fees, furniture, shipping, assembly, installation and photography.
  • Record who handles ordering, delivery problems, returns and replacements.
  • Confirm approval steps, revision limits, timeline and responsibility for on-site access.
  • Ask for relevant portfolio evidence and permission to contact references.

A design example or testimonial does not guarantee your occupancy, nightly rate or revenue. Evaluate property economics separately.

New vs secondhand furniture for a short-term rental

Use acquisition cost, inspection effort and replacement needs to decide where secondhand pieces make sense. A low purchase price can be offset by transport, cleaning and repair.

What changes between the options?

Compare the practical tradeoffs
DecisionNew furnitureSecondhand furniture
ConditionReview product specifications, return terms and warranty exclusions.Inspect joints, odors, upholstery, hardware and missing parts before purchase.
RepeatabilityCheck whether the model and spare parts are likely to remain available.Matching replacements may be difficult for one-off finds.
LogisticsTrack shipping charges, lead times and assembly.Plan pickup, access, lifting help and any necessary restoration.

A property scenario

A host finds a used solid-wood dresser locally but needs identical bedside lamps in four rooms. A mixed approach can use the inspected dresser while buying repeatable lighting. Compare each item category separately instead of imposing one purchasing rule on the entire property.

Questions to resolve before committing

  • Can the item be inspected before payment?
  • What are the all-in pickup, repair and cleaning costs?
  • Can damaged components be replaced?

Compare the complete project cost

Put both options against the same rooms, guest capacity and completion date. List product costs, design fees, shipping, receiving, assembly, installation and any repeat visits separately. Assign a person to each task and document exclusions. A lower quoted fee is only comparable when the responsibilities and deliverables match.

How will the team identify and accept the right goods or deliverables?

Prepare before arrival

Give the receiver the exact order or deliverable schedule, approved identifiers, quantities and expected arrival information. Distinguish a shipping estimate from a confirmed appointment. Identify who can make decisions when an incorrect, damaged or incomplete item appears.

Review the seller, carrier and receiving agreement's current inspection and reporting procedures. Record relevant deadlines and the evidence needed for a remedy. Do not invent a universal claim window or assume that a signature establishes complete product acceptance. Keep access, temporary storage and packaging handling in the plan.

Separate quantities and condition statuses

Use distinct records for ordered, arrived, inspected, accepted and released for installation. Match labels or file identifiers to the approved specification. Preserve relevant photographs and notes according to the provider process. A closed carton or delivered file can still contain a mismatch with the scope.

Separate wrong item, missing quantity, visible damage and an installation concern. Each may need a different response. Keep exception goods or incomplete deliverables distinguishable from accepted inventory so the next person does not use or discard them by mistake.

Carry exceptions into the schedule

Assign a claim or correction owner and a next review date. Record what the provider has actually agreed to do. A replacement dispatched later is not yet an inspected remedy, and a promised refund is not a reconciled credit. Keep the installation and readiness decision owners informed of effects.

Close the receiving line when the accepted quantity and remedy evidence reconcile. Transfer exact product or deliverable information to installation and the final inventory. The goal is a traceable acceptance process rather than a carton count that hides unresolved work.

Common questions for this decision

Does a delivery signature close the order?

Not by itself. Follow the relevant agreement and provider procedures, and preserve separate records for arrival, condition and accepted quantity.

Should packaging be discarded immediately?

Use the actual inspection and claim requirements to decide. Packaging can contain identifiers or evidence needed for an exception, while the project also needs a suitable temporary storage and removal process.

What counts as a closed exception?

The accepted remedy or corrected deliverable has been checked and the related quantity, financial and schedule records reconcile. A promise alone is not closure.

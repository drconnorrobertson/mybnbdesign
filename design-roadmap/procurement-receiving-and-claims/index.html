Manage procurement, receiving and substitutions

Procurement is a sequence of controlled decisions. Ordering the right product is only the beginning; receiving, inspecting and resolving exceptions determine what becomes usable inventory.

Updated October 13, 2026

Stage 9 of 12. See the full curriculum and choose your starting stage

The decision to make

Keep one approved purchasing register with room, SKU, finish, quantity, quote, approval, order number and expected delivery. Confirm cancellation and return terms before purchase. Record the receiving contact and inspection process before the first shipment arrives. An unattended delivery can create a storage, weather or access problem even when the order itself is correct.

How to carry it out

Inspect against the actual order and preserve claim evidence according to the seller’s procedures. Connect photographs to the shipment and product line. Separate a missing item from a damaged item and assign the next action. For substitutions, compare size, access, care, appearance, timing and the total landed cost. Require an approval before purchase and update every downstream record, including the floor plan and installed inventory.

Worked planning example

Illustrative scenario; not a client case or performance result.

A hypothetical delivery contains four chair cartons, but one has the wrong finish. The receiver photographs the label and chair, records three accepted units and one exception, and follows the seller’s remedy process. The purchasing tracker remains open until the corrected unit is received and inspected.

Decision gate: evidence before moving on

Close an order only after its accepted quantity, credits, replacement items and installation status reconcile. Keep open claims visible to the person deciding room readiness.

Build the working record

Use one record per property and decision. Replace the prompts with verified information, retain the source documents and mark anything that still requires confirmation. Assign a person and a date to open questions instead of leaving them buried in conversation.

Use the record with your project team

Start with the current approved revision. Send the same document to the owner, designer and local coordinator so each person can see which decisions are settled. Keep quotes and manufacturer information attached to the relevant line. When a decision changes, record what changed, why it changed and which downstream documents must be updated.

At the next review, compare the installed or contracted result with the recorded requirement. Close an item only when the required evidence exists. If the record describes a planned purchase, do not relabel it as installed inventory until receiving and site checks confirm it. If the property is already operating, schedule work around the actual calendar and communicate the affected rooms and amenities to the person responsible for listing accuracy.
